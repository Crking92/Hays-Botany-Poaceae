## Important issues surfaced

### Hooded versus tumble windmillgrass

Some old floras simplify the distinction to a single terminal cluster in hooded windmillgrass versus multiple tiers in tumble. Modern and regional treatments describe **1–3 crowded apical whorls for hooded**, compared with **well-separated longer whorls for tumble**. The much more decisive examination is the **rounded inflated upper sterile floret** and its very short awn in hooded, versus the more oblong sterile floret and several-millimeter awns in tumble. Hybrids can intergrade.

The site head-option caption was updated to teach that *tier spacing is a clue, not a verdict*, and both tight and separate whorl patterns can be inspected.

### Sideoats grama true ligule

FNA says **a tiny membranous ligule with cilia**, while Kew's GrassBase describes a **fringe of hairs**. Their visual difference may depend on whether the membrane body is visible underneath cilia. The key currently accepts either broad choice with explicit source conflict; this is **not** a determination that two anatomically distinct ligules exist.

### Anatherum taxon crosswalk

A. tenuispatheum is recognized by Kew and was formerly treated under multiple *Andropogon glomeratus/virginicus* concepts. The measurable published distinctions are useful, but they cannot be automatically applied to historic photographs of var. *pumilus*, or prove a locally collected Hays County plant's identity. Voucher and specimen-level proof remain open.

## Targeted photo needs after gallery review (not downloaded)

1. **Chloris:** attached whole mature head with clear whorl insertion (one image with a measuring scale); separate intact spikelet, *fertile lower floret*, *upper sterile floret*, and both awns (mm scale). Hooded and tumble first. Annual feather fingergrass needs close fertile-lemma apical hair crown.
2. **Broomsedge vs bushy:** same-culm showing each raceme pair emerging from its subtending spathe; nonflowering upper and basal leaf sheath shapes.
3. **A. tenuispatheum:** 2.5 mm-width threshold on raceme sheath, less-than-1-mm true ligule, sub-3.8-mm sessile spikelet, each on the same authenticated voucher.
4. **Sideoats grama:** collar under magnification showing the 0.3–0.5 mm membrane under its hairlike cilia, and an axis with one whole lateral branch detaching.
5. **Big bluestem:** measured paired sessile/pedicelled spikelets and actual bent awns, while retaining a whole-plant view.
6. **Winter variation where useful:** mature dry heads and real rooted bases; the same structures remain important even when foliage has browned. Confirm existing gallery coverage before requesting new downloads.
