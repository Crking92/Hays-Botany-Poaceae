## What the pass accomplished

- **11/112** grass species received first source-linked, species-separating morphology checklists (**24** checks total).

## Detailed species comparisons

| Species | Important anatomical character | Nearby look-alike / caution |
|---|---|---|
| Little bluestem (*Schizachyrium scoparium*) | Short paired-spikelet-bearing branches terminating **separate leafy branches**; true membrane ligule around 1–3 mm | KR has several terminal finger branches on a short common axis; nodes and ligules are different structures |
| KR bluestem (*Bothriochloa ischaemum*) | Rame branches around 3–9 cm arising from short 0.5–2 cm axis; fertile sessile-spikelet awn **9–17 mm**; papilla-based hairs near leaf base | Can mimic native cane and silver, plus Kleberg; whole-head view may hide awns |
| Silky bluestem (*Dichanthium sericeum*) | Actual node hairs, true membrane ligule, lower-glume transverse fringe, and twisted fertile-lemma awn around 25–30 mm | Kleberg also has node hairs and may have glume fringe; a single hair ring is not enough |
| Cane bluestem (*Bothriochloa barbinodis*) | True bearded culm node; 4.5–7.3 mm sessile spikelets; roughly 20–30 mm bent awns | KR can have short node hairs; compare measured spikelets, not only pubescence |
| Silver bluestem (*Bothriochloa torreyana*) | Silvery flowering branches along an elongate main axis; source under *B. laguroides* subsp. *torreyana* needs explicit name crosswalk | Cane may look equally silvery; separate on node and attached floral parts |
| Kleberg bluestem (*Dichanthium annulatum*) | About 2–9 rames 2.5–7 cm; bare initial portions on rames; sessile spikelets 2.5–5 mm; membrane ligule 1–1.8 mm | Pubescence of node varies across published accounts; contrast rachis and pedicel with KR |
| Blue grama (*Bouteloua gracilis*) | Usually 1–3(4) curved combs with rudimentary spikelet near terminal end; true ligule is a hair fringe | Hairy grama has pronounced naked rachis extension; hair ligule alone will not distinguish them |
| Hairy grama (*Bouteloua hirsuta*) | Rachis usually extends 5–10 mm beyond last spikelet; 0.2–0.5 mm hair-fringe ligule, with frequently hairy spikelets | Compare blue grama tip morphology, not just branch count or purple coloring |
| Tall grama (*Bouteloua pectinata*) | Often 3–5 relatively straight combs, 2.5–4.5 cm, with axis-like **bifid rudimentary spikelet** apically | Resembles hairy grama's naked tip at a distance; macro tip detail essential |
| Texas grama (*Bouteloua rigidiseta*) | Several short racemes around 8–16 mm (often 7–10), only ~3–5 fertile spikelets each; fertile lemma with **three very short awns** | Avoid assigning to generic 'three LONG awns' field button, which depicts *Aristida* |
| Red grama (*Bouteloua trifida*) | Usually 2–7 relatively narrow pectinate racemes, 12–25 mm; tiny fertile lemma around 2 mm and its three short awns | Distinguish from Texas grama's shorter, more numerous branches; no diagnosis from color alone |

## Important awn correction

The prior broad review transcribed the KR-bluestem awn as **1–1.5 mm** and treated it as categorically lacking a visible long awn. That number is incompatible with Flora of North America, which reports 9–17 mm for the **lemma awn of the sessile spikelet**. In the revised prototype, the species may match either `long` or `none_obvious` in a general photo view. The latter describes *visual conspicuousness*, not biological absence. This is an **intentional safe multi-state**, pending field-photo verification. The existing broad filtering regression for a hidden awn is not broken by this change.

For *Bouteloua rigidiseta* and *B. trifida*, the 3-awned **fertile lemma** and also awned **sterile floret** are different from a prominently three-long-awned *Aristida* unit. Their broad awn option remains **unknown**, not falsely absent.

## Phase 4 targeted photos now worth checking (not yet collected)

1. Blue versus hairy grama: same-scale macro of **entire comb apex** and last attached spikelet, with naked extension visible.
2. Tall grama: isolated axis-like terminal rudimentary spikelet, viewed against ruler, showing **split tip**.
3. Texas versus red grama: whole heads on the culm **plus** dissected fertile lemmas and sterile rudimentary florets, measured.
4. KR: actual inner ligule, papilla-based blade hairs near collar, and separate awned sessile spikelet with pedicelled companion at scale.
5. Cane versus silver: same-plant true culm nodes, long-axis silhouette and measured mature paired spikelets.
6. Silky versus Kleberg: matched lower glumes, node hairs, true membrane ligules, and lower spikelike branch portions.
7. Winter/dormant stage: mature dry seedheads and rooted tufts when useful in the Hays County November–February field window; do not require every stage of every species.

Existing photo records have *not* been certified for these roles. Check gallery images first; then add only genuine gaps to the single master photo-needs queue in the established gallery workflow. No new spreadsheets or unlicensed photos.
