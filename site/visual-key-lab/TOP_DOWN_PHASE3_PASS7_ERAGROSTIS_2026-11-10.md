## Source-backed differentiating checks

| Grass | High-value real structure | Main comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Purple lovegrass — *Eragrostis spectabilis* | Wide 25–45 cm open, sometimes **tumbling/disarticulating whole panicle**; usually 7–11 florets in 5–7 mm spikelets, 1.8–2.2 mm 3-veined lemmas; perennial with **short knotty rhizomes** | Plains lovegrass and red lovegrass can have purple heads, but red lovegrass has larger, denser, many-flowered spikelets |
| Plains lovegrass — *E. intermedia* | Open pyramid with hairy lower primary branch **pulvini/axils**; 4–7 mm spikelets typically 2–9 florets in Williamson County specimen treatment, ~1.8–2.2 mm lemma; tiny 0.3–0.55 mm ciliate ligule | Sand lovegrass is often larger with finer long branches, longer lemmas and terminal spikelets; counts overlap |
| Sand lovegrass — *E. trichodes* | Airy oblong 35–55 cm panicle, capillary sinuous branches/pedicels; mature spikelets 4–10 mm, 4–18 florets; lemmas **2.4–3.4 mm**; collar/throat tufts ~4–5 mm and true tiny hair-fringe ligule | Plains lovegrass: usually shorter lemma in matched Williamson County specimen measurements, but no absolute cutoff |
| Stinkgrass — *E. cilianensis* (introduced) | ANNUAL root base; craterlike glands especially on lemma keels, glumes, branchlets/leaf margins, sometimes below nodes; comparatively broad 3–20 mm spikelets with 5–60 florets | Red lovegrass may also have dense multi-flowered spikelets but typically lacks glands and is perennial |
| Bigtop lovegrass — *E. hirsuta* | **Genuinely hairy sheath BODY**, not merely hairs at the throat; bearded primary panicle branch axils, open head 25–60 cm; tiny 2–4 mm, 2–6-flowered spikelets | Mourning lovegrass tends to have smoother sheaths, narrower involute leaves and smaller, narrower spikelets |
| Mourning lovegrass — *E. lugens* | Involute slender leaves 1–2 mm wide; sheath body usually smooth with hair at mouth, open 18–28 cm panicle of flexuous capillary branches; narrow 3–4.5 mm by 0.5–1 mm, 5–7-flowered spikelets | Bigtop's wider 4–8 mm blades, hairy sheaths and much larger panicle; a panicle silhouette is not enough |
| Red lovegrass — *E. secundiflora* | Compressed reddish 6–20 mm spikelets with MANY florets clustered on stiff branches; Kew species account usually 10–24, FNA southern-US subsp. **oxylepis** up to ~45; red three-veined lemmas | Stinkgrass is glandular and annual; purple lovegrass much more diffusely branched, fewer florets |

### Source and taxon-concept conflicts retained

1. **Purple lovegrass ligule.** Kew GrassBase calls it a **hair fringe**, while University of Missouri WeedID calls it a very short **membrane** (well below 1 mm). The broad picture-first key now allows both `hairs` and `membrane`, but that is a *visual tolerance*, not evidence of two unrelated ligule anatomies. A 10–20x same-plant photograph of the inner ligule is needed.
2. **Plains lovegrass rhizome.** The [UT Austin Williamson County `Eragrostis intermedia` specimen account](https://w3.biosci.utexas.edu/prc/K12/pdf/Eragrostis%20intermedia) explicitly calls the plant short, knotty **rhizomatous**. The [Yavapai County Extension field account](https://yavapaiplants.extension.arizona.edu/eragrostis-intermedia) reports **no rhizomes**. Both `tuft` and `rhizome` are now eligible broad states; neither is taken as proven in a Hays specimen. The UT account describes 0.3–0.55 mm densely ciliate ligule plus MUCH longer collar hairs—these must be visually distinguished.
3. **Plains lovegrass pedicel wording.** Texas A&M's general guide describes pedicels shorter than the spikelets, while the detailed UT botanical specimen treatment describes terminal axes often longer than a spikelet. Axes and branch-order terminology may account for some discrepancy. No pedicel-length cutoff was added to broad filtering.
4. **Red lovegrass taxonomy.** Kew recognizes *E. secundiflora* with two accepted subspecies; FNA explicitly applies **subsp. oxylepis** to US material. Whole-species GrassBase 10–24 florets versus US FNA up to 45 is not a data integrity error. The key records the infraspecific concepts and does not use a universal floret count.
5. **Stinkgrass glands vary** by individual and organ. Absence of a visible gland in a macro photo is *unknown*, not a positive diagnosis of another Eragrostis species. Odor is auxiliary, not botanical proof.
6. **Small source variation in floret numbers** is common; retain full overlapping measured ranges, look for 3-veined lemmas, paired 2-keeled paleas, ciliate hair vs membrane distinction, and annual versus perennial connected crowns.

## Specific anatomical photo roles — check current galleries before requesting any new images

- **All lovegrasses:** one mature intact side-compressed spikelet at 10–20x with mm reference; count visible fertile florets, isolate LOWER and UPPER glume, one **three-veined fertile lemma** and **two-keeled palea**; separate inner ligule (usually only ~0.2–0.5 mm) from longer sheath-throat hairs.
- **Purple:** entire panicle still ATTACHED and mature detached tumbling head (if existing photos already show it, do not re-download); connected knotty rhizome; ligule tissue resolving short membrane/hair-fringe wording.
- **Plains versus sand:** pair comparable scale photos of 1.8–2.2 mm versus commonly 2.4–3.4 mm lemmas, and branch-axil hairs on the same attached flowering stem; verify their field identities and overlapping size ranges.
- **Bigtop versus mourning:** sheath BODY versus sheath-MOUTH hairs, blade at cross-section (involute vs flat), exact spikelet sizes and mature open panicle.
- **Red versus stinkgrass:** red densely many-flowered spikelet versus glandular lemma keel or glume; photograph an actual craterlike gland with hand lens; annual or perennial root base.
- **Cold-season field coverage:** both attached dry panicles and persistent rooted dry basal tufts if useful to the November–February crew; green winter-growing Eragrostis should not be assumed without relevant specimen evidence.
- Retain licensed originals and credits. Do not create a separate photo-needs spreadsheet; communicate only real uncovered roles to the established **single master Photo Needs Queue** after independently inspecting current photos.
