King Ranch vs. Little Bluestem

They can both look blue-gray when vegetative and fuzzy when mature. Look at the architecture of the entire flowering stem, then check spikelets and the blade–sheath junction. Two independent characters are better than a single impression.

Evidence level: Existing Hays Botany hosted photographs and regional-flora-supported descriptions, not new specimen-level certification. If you only have leaves or an incomplete head, keep the ID provisional and compare other bluestems before deciding to remove a plant.

How I'd check these in the field

  1. Find an intact flowering culm. KR generally places several longer, fingerlike branches close together near one terminal axis. Little bluestem has multiple shorter silky racemes at the ends of separate leafy branches along the upper culm.
  2. Follow the branch to its attachment. A clipped head, dried tuft or loose single raceme can conceal the strongest difference. Check the whole plant, not only a detached piece.
  3. Use a hand lens on a mature spikelet. Both can have awned spikelets; match the structure and attachment to the published species descriptions rather than relying on color or general fuzziness.
  4. Open the inner collar if needed. A real ligule occurs at the blade–sheath junction. Hairs around a culm node are a different structure.
  5. Don't force a two-choice ID. Silver, silky, Kleberg, cane and big bluestems can enter the comparison. Check at least two linked characters before making a restoration decision.
