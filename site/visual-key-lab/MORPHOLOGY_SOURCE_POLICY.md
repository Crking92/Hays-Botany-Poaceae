# Visual Grass ID Key — morphology-source and character decision rules

Updated: 2026-11-11 (owner-approved source-role hierarchy). Scope: all **Poaceae identification-character work** in this repository, including `site/visual-key-lab/`, the Grass Learner, Field ID lessons, visual comparisons, diagnostic packets, research branches and future species expansions. This rule does not automatically rewrite species records, county-native status, photo approval, or public key scoring.

## Source priority and purpose — role-specific, not a blind numeric override

1. **Regional teaching set and evidence:** Existing Hays Botany species records/dashboard define the scoped 112-species key; draft expansion taxa do not become automatically approved. Preserve local observations, photographed characters, old/new name crosswalks, uncertainty and progress. A dashboard state or photograph of a correctly named species alone is not proof of a specific anatomical character.
2. **PRIMARY FOR VEGETATIVE identification — Hagenbuch, K. W. & D. E. Lemke (2015), *Grasses of the Texas Hill Country: Vegetative Key and Descriptions*, Phytoneuron 2015-4: 1–93.** Prefer this Hays-inclusive Hill Country study when researching or coding vegetative morphology: growth habit, basal portions, roots/rhizomes/stolons, buds, culms/nodes, collars, sheaths, auricles, ligules, blades, venation, pubescence, hairs and other usable nonflowering traits. It covers 160 species/66 genera, not every possible Hays grass. Verify the specific account and source page. [Official original PDF](https://www.phytoneuron.net/2015Phytoneuron/04PhytoN-HillCountryGrasses.pdf).
3. **PRIMARY FOR REPRODUCTIVE identification — Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), *Shinners & Mahler's Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas* (user-provided monocot/Poaceae excerpt).** Prefer the Flora for inflorescence architecture, fertile versus sterile parts, spikelets, glumes, lemmas, paleas, awns, bristles, flower and fruit characters, species keys and flowering look-alikes. Its descriptions of vegetative traits remain valuable as explicit cross-checks, especially where the 2015 Hill Country treatment lacks a species. Preserve the flora's historical name/taxon scope with a modern crosswalk. [Private reference metadata](PRIMARY_MORPHOLOGY_REFERENCE.md).
4. **THIRD-LINE CHARACTER SUPPLEMENT — Kew GrassBase / GrassBase v2 / PolyClave-style character data** for additional structured morphology and ranges, particularly when the two regional treatments omit a state or to flag a source conflict. **Flora of North America** is a supplementary taxon treatment for wider variation, missing taxa, and additional reproductive/vegetative comparisons. Neither automatically overrides a directly applicable specialist account; treat disagreements as review tasks with both citations.
5. **Local confirmation and taxonomy stay separate:** Verified Hays-region field observations, herbarium vouchers, and the UT Austin Plant Resources Center test how characters apply locally. Kew POWO and current specialist taxonomy resolve accepted names/synonyms, without silently changing diagnostic observations. BONAP NAPA and authenticated county records are for the **later** county nativity/planting review, not evidence of a character or an automatic new-species admission. Introduced look-alikes remain identifiable and are not planting recommendations.

**Critical exception rule:** An authority is first for its *area of expertise*, not for everything it says. For the same species, the vegetative and reproductive accounts may be complementary or describe different subspecies, developmental stages, management responses, local variants, sexes, or taxonomic concepts. Check those causes before calling them contradictory. Do not force uncertain character states into a key, infer absence from an unmentioned trait, or conflate vegetative leaf hairs with floral bristles. Never claim source agreement without checking both sources directly.

**Reference availability:** The 275-page monocot excerpt was verified as stored in the user's ChatGPT Library at `/Hays Botany ID/References/Flora of North Central Texas-monocots-Acor-Zanni.pdf` and Google Drive as `Flora of North Central Texas-monocots-Acor-Zanni.pdf` (2026-11-11). Hagenbuch & Lemke is recorded in the Google Drive reference folder as a verified official PDF **link**, not a downloaded private file. The prior 2026-11-10 storage-failure note in older project documents is historical, not current. Verify actual document access in each workflow; a link/index does not imply a locally saved PDF or an automatically approved diagnostic. Do not redistribute copyrighted book pages or figures in the public site.

## Key conversion rules

- **Provenance required for new or revised traits:** Record the exact character/structure, vegetative vs reproductive role, scientific name and synonym concept, named reference and page, reported states and measurement range, certainty, season/sex/management or habitat qualifiers, actual photo/voucher support and any unresolved cross-source disagreement. Use existing schema fields where possible; do not invent a mandatory database field or falsely label all historical data verified.

- Scientific names: preserve current Hays Botany display names. Store original GrassBase taxon name and accepted-name crosswalk separately; flag ambiguous synonyms/hybrids.
- Each coded character must have a source reference, the actual observed state(s), a certainty level (reviewed; provisional; conflicting; unavailable) and, where relevant, season and sex/floral phase. A missing state means **unknown**, never **absent**.
- Multiple states are allowed for species with variability; e.g. awned **or** awnless fertile spikelets. Do not automatically rule a species out for a state not yet verified.
- Differentiate nonhomologous structures: *Setaria* sterile branch bristles are not lemma awns; stigmas are neither. Rhizomes are underground stems, stolons are surface runners, and root hairs are not ligules.
- A photo is not morphologically verified merely because the species identity was identified. Select images showing the **actual botanical character** requested; whole-panicle choices require whole-head views; microstructures need close focus/scale and may require a hand lens. Mark unclear photos as pending; do not substitute a photograph of a different plant while labeling it as the target.
- Preserve the exact image rights, photographer, original image identity/source page and license. Do not treat CC BY-NC, educational permission, and public-domain/CC BY licenses as interchangeable; do not import new files without rights review.
- Multi-access UI: allow selecting traits in any order; provide “Can't tell / skip”; don't penalize unknown states; show look-alikes and explain conflicting selections without falsely claiming an ID is definite.
- Use field-friendly photograph + short botanical terminology. Longer explanations belong in existing learner, not inside the key's selection workflow.
- **Framework assembled 2026-11-10:** All 112 indexed Poaceae species now have preliminary multi-access character profiles across 9 data files. Characters are **provisional** and originate in existing Hays Botany species records; they are not yet a full GrassBase row-by-row mapping. The first pass validates data integrity and candidate mechanics, NOT the botanical accuracy of every state.
- **Current exceptions:** Canyon muhly (*Muhlenbergia ×involuta*) still lacks a widely reusable source photo in the key; leave the placeholder rather than presenting limited-permission photos as open licensed. Missing or conflicting character states must remain unscored and visible as uncertain.
- **Next workflow:** (1) check taxon concepts and top-level definitions across all 112 live species and track expansion taxa separately; (2) label each character as vegetative, reproductive, or mixed; (3) for vegetative traits verify Hagenbuch & Lemke first and cite paper page, then Flora as applicable; for reproductive traits verify the Flora first and cite printed/PDF page, then Hagenbuch if relevant; (4) use GrassBase/PolyClave and Flora of North America for gaps, ranges and explicit conflict checks, with Hays specimens/photos to confirm visibility; (5) review look-alikes, correct only verified photo captions/diagnostic mismatches using proper rights; (6) test candidate filtering, learner comparisons, phone view, and live deployment separately. Keep unsupported/uncertain states unscored; never auto-approve draft species, rewrite originals, duplicate spreadsheets, or touch unrelated pages.
- The previously uploaded 275-page excerpt of the *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas* contains family recognition, keys, species diagnoses and botanical illustrations. Use the morphological facts and key distinctions as source evidence; **do not republish its illustrations or long text passages** without an appropriate copyright basis. It is a North Central Texas reference, **not** a direct Hays County nativity map.
