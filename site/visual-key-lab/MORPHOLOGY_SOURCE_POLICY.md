# Visual Grass ID Key — morphology-source and character decision rules

Updated: 2026-11-10. Scope: `site/visual-key-lab/` in the active Poaceae repository. This rule controls the **identification key** only; it does not rewrite species records, county-native status, or photo approval.

## Source priority and purpose

1. **Hays Botany species records and the established dashboard** determine which 112 grasses are in the regional teaching set. Retain original field observations, accepted names with crosswalks, photographic credits, identification lessons, known uncertainty and project progress; the dashboard does NOT independently prove a botanical diagnosis.
2. **Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon, *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas* (1999), user-supplied monocots excerpt** is the project's **primary/authoritative regional reference for anatomical terminology, grass keys and distinguishing morphological characters**, within the species/taxon concept treated in the book. Use its verified distinctions first when designing question logic and deciding what diagnostic photo must show. Record historical names and differences in scope where the modern name changed. This source is regional North Central Texas, not itself a Hays County occurrence or BONAP nativity determination.
3. **Flora of North America** supplements the regional flora for wider within-species variation, updated taxon concepts and technical comparisons, particularly where the 1999 regional treatment lacks a species, includes only a synonym or has a narrower range. **GrassBase v2 / Kew POWO** supply additional measured morphological ranges and modern name/synonym crosswalks; do not let a conflicting generic GrassBase description silently replace the regional flora's specific key character. **UT Austin Plant Resources Center and local authenticated vouchers** further refine Central Texas comparisons. Preserve genuine conflicts with citations and verification status.
4. **BONAP NAPA plus authenticated county vouchers/occurrences** govern Hays County and relevant west/north neighboring-county nativity, separately from anatomy. Introduced species remain as comparison options, not restoration planting recommendations.

## New source for Hays County expansion — 2015 Hill Country grass key

The **57 new candidate taxa are staged outside the live 112-species key** in `audits/hays-expansion-20261111/hays-57-draft-id-cards.json`. None should enter the scored field key until botanical characters, species identity and photo rights pass their individual QA gates.

**Added regional source:** Hagenbuch, K. W. & Lemke, D. E. (2015), *Grasses of the Texas Hill Country: Vegetative Key and Descriptions*, Phytoneuron 2015-4: 1–93. https://www.phytoneuron.net/2015Phytoneuron/04PhytoN-HillCountryGrasses.pdf. Use its 160-species vegetative key, Hays-area leaf collar, ligule, sheath, rooting and dormant habit descriptions, and county-labelled original herbarium citations to supplement the 1999 *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*. Do not treat inclusion in the 2015 Hill Country source alone as a confirmed occurrence in Hays County; inspect original voucher sheets or Hays observations. Record which 2015 name/variety was used before adopting modern Kew POWO accepted names.

**Recommended source roles:** 1999 Flora = primary regional key for reproductive structures; 2015 Hill Country study = complementary more local vegetative trait and county specimen evidence; Flora of North America/Kew GrassBase = species range, modern morphological comparisons and conflicts; Kew POWO/WCVP = accepted scientific names; BONAP/NAPA + county voucher evidence = later separate nativity check. Any conflicting morphological descriptions or distinct taxon concepts must be recorded in the candidate species' evidence rather than silently converted to a trait state.

`traits.head`, `traits.habit`, `traits.ligule` and `traits.awns` arrays are deliberately **empty** in all 57 draft cards; these mean *unknown pending specimen-character verification*, never absent. Public site photographs are likewise absent pending rights review. Do not bulk-import these drafts into `species-*.json` or the live `poaceae-index.json` without the release checklist.

## Key conversion rules

- Scientific names: preserve current Hays Botany display names. Store original GrassBase taxon name and accepted-name crosswalk separately; flag ambiguous synonyms/hybrids.
- Each coded character must have a source reference, the actual observed state(s), a certainty level (reviewed; provisional; conflicting; unavailable) and, where relevant, season and sex/floral phase. A missing state means **unknown**, never **absent**.
- Multiple states are allowed for species with variability; e.g. awned **or** awnless fertile spikelets. Do not automatically rule a species out for a state not yet verified.
- Differentiate nonhomologous structures: *Setaria* sterile branch bristles are not lemma awns; stigmas are neither. Rhizomes are underground stems, stolons are surface runners, and root hairs are not ligules.
- A photo is not morphologically verified merely because the species identity was identified. Select images showing the **actual botanical character** requested; whole-panicle choices require whole-head views; microstructures need close focus/scale and may require a hand lens. Mark unclear photos as pending; do not substitute a photograph of a different plant while labeling it as the target.
- Preserve the exact image rights, photographer, original image identity/source page and license. Do not treat CC BY-NC, educational permission, and public-domain/CC BY licenses as interchangeable; do not import new files without rights review.
- Multi-access UI: allow selecting traits in any order; provide “Can't tell / skip”; don't penalize unknown states; show look-alikes and explain conflicting selections without falsely claiming an ID is definite.
- Use field-friendly photograph + short botanical terminology. Longer explanations belong in existing learner, not inside the key's selection workflow.
- **Framework assembled 2026-11-10:** All 112 indexed Poaceae species now have preliminary multi-access character profiles across 9 data files. Characters are **provisional** and originate in existing Hays Botany species records; they are not yet a full GrassBase row-by-row mapping. The first pass validates data integrity and candidate mechanics, NOT the botanical accuracy of every state.
- **Current exceptions:** Canyon muhly (*Muhlenbergia ×involuta*) still lacks a widely reusable source photo in the key; leave the placeholder rather than presenting limited-permission photos as open licensed. Missing or conflicting character states must remain unscored and visible as uncertain.
- **Next workflow:** (1) check taxon names and top-level character definitions across all 112; (2) review flowerhead and growth states by genus and look-alike group, prioritizing grasses encountered in Hays County and neighboring west/north counties; (3) verify ligules, spikelets, bristles versus awns, measured glumes/lemmas, and seasonal variation against the user-provided *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas* monocot section first, then Flora of North America, GrassBase and relevant regional voucher floras; (4) replace misleading diagnostic photos using confirmed appropriate rights; (5) run 112-species candidate/filter acceptance tests and live-page visual checks. Avoid introducing new species, duplicate spreadsheets, or changes to unrelated learner pages.
- The previously uploaded 275-page excerpt of the *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas* contains family recognition, keys, species diagnoses and botanical illustrations. Use the morphological facts and key distinctions as source evidence; **do not republish its illustrations or long text passages** without an appropriate copyright basis. It is a North Central Texas reference, **not** a direct Hays County nativity map.
