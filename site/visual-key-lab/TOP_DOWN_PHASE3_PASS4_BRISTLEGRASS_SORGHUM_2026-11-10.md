## Species added

| Species | Species-separating character targets | Main cautions |
|---|---|---|
| Marsh bristlegrass (*Setaria parviflora*) | Usually **4–12 persistent sterile-branch bristles** beneath a spikelet; short knotty **rhizomes**; transversely wrinkled mature upper lemma | Dense foxtail head alone is insufficient; compare Scheele and foxtail millet |
| Scheele's bristlegrass (*S. scheelei*) | Usually **one long bristle**, 10–35 mm; tapered, relatively open flowering head; hairy mature nodes | Bristle = sterile branch, **not** lemma awn |
| Reverchon's bristlegrass (*S. reverchonii*) | Sparse interrupted slender panicle, bristles around **2–8 mm**, 5–7-veined lower glume; short occasionally knotty rhizomes | The previous tuft-only habit code was too restrictive; now includes rhizomatous |
| Foxtail millet (*S. italica*) | Annual, dense 8–30 cm panicle, usually 1–3 bristles, upper lemma smooth to minutely rugose | Cultivated forms vary dramatically in head size and texture |
| Indiangrass (*Sorghastrum nutans*) | Stiff continuous **rifle-sight membranous ligule** with pointed auricles; fertile awned sessile spikelet plus an **empty companion pedicel**, short scaly rhizomes | Cannot diagnose from the golden panicle alone; compare slender Indiangrass |
| Slender Indiangrass (*S. elliottii*) | Looser, darker head; ciliate membrane ligule **2–4 mm**; typically **25–40 mm twice-bent awn** and tuft rather than stout rhizomes | *S. nutans* awn lengths can overlap; same-plant mature florets needed |
| Johnsongrass (*Sorghum halepense*) | Long creeping rhizomes; both sessile and **developed pedicelled spikelets**; ciliate membrane ligule | FNA allows the fertile lemma to be **awnless or awned up to 13 mm**, contradicting the old 1–1.5 mm generalization; both broad states now allowed |
| Grain/weedy sorghum (*S. bicolor*) | Normally no rhizomes; developed sessile/pedicelled pairs; often exposed ripe grain in cultivated varieties; awns can be absent or 5–30 mm | Sorghum cultivars and shattercane relatives vary; no panicle-shape-only identification |
| Coastal sandbur (*Cenchrus spinifex*) | A **fused cup of bristles** encloses around 2–4 spikelets, with flattened backward-barbed spines | Setaria bristles are separate persistent branches; sandbur burrs are not lemma awns |
| Tanglehead (*Heteropogon contortus*) | Basal mostly awnless male/sterile pairs, upper awned sessile fertile spikelets and **sharp reddish bearded callus**, awns often 5–12 cm | Smithsonian says ciliate membranous ligule; some GrassBase accounts emphasize hairs. Retain both broad states |
| Eastern gamagrass (*Tripsacum dactyloides*) | **Upper staminate section** and **lower beadlike pistillate section** on each flowering branch; hard excavated female rachis joints | Distinct from Sorghum, whose developed male/sterile spikelets accompany sessile spikelets rather than forming this beadlike lower section |

## Corrections and conflicts recorded in the actual records

1. **Sorghum halepense:** The older regional flora note suggesting 1–1.5 mm awns is not a reliable upper limit. The published Flora of North America account permits *no awn* or a twisted/bent fertile-lemma awn to **13 mm**. Broad visual `awns` now supports both `none_obvious` and `long`, keeping obscured-awn and visibly awned cases in the candidate pool. This does not claim that every plant has long awns.
2. **Setaria reverchonii:** FNA reports short, sometimes knotty **rhizomes** as well as tufted vegetative form. Both `tuft` and `rhizome` choices now remain eligible. An exposed connected stem is still required for actual photo validation.
3. **Sorghastrum elliottii:** Kew GrassBase explicitly documents a **ciliolate membranous ligule**, generally 2–4 mm, so the previously unknown broad ligule now scores `membrane`. This source state has not been checked against a photo.
4. **Heteropogon contortus:** Real-source descriptions differ in whether the tiny inner ligule is best called a short **ciliate membrane** or a **hair fringe**. Both field-appearance states remain available pending a collar macro that shows membrane tissue.
5. **Key wording:** The visual awn/bristle choice now distinguishes **Setaria separate sterile branch bristles**, **Cenchrus fused involucre spines**, and true lemma or glume awns. These parts are not interchangeable.

## Photo anatomy requests to evaluate within the existing gallery lane

Do not download indiscriminately. Inspect already-approved exact-species photographs first and add only genuine missing anatomy to the **one master Photo Needs Queue**:

- *Setaria*: intact spikelet with attached bristles and mm ruler; a branchlet showing where bristles originate, true hairy ligule, mature fertile lemma texture; connected knotty rhizome where species has one.
- *Sorghastrum*: a **true inner collar** with pointed auricles; a single mature sessile fertile spikelet with visible *empty* companion pedicel; complete lemma awn and root base for *nutans* versus *elliottii*.
- *Sorghum*: side-by-side mature *sessile and developed pedicelled spikelets*, glumes and actual awn; *halepense* connected rhizome; *bicolor* persistent mature grain-bearing unit.
- *Cenchrus*: mature fused burr carefully opened, with **2–4 spikelets** inside, attachment scar and spine bases.
- *Heteropogon*: one whole raceme from lower basal sterile pairs to upper awned fertile pairs; sharp callus with mm scale and 5–12 cm awn.
- *Tripsacum*: one intact branch with **upper male and lower female** zones, plus separate mature hardened female joint.
- Include mature dry winter heads and rooted dormant tufts when that stage is actually useful for Kyle field identification.
