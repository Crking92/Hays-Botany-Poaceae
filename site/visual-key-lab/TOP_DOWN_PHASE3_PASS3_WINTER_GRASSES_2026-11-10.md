| Purple threeawn (*A. purpurea*) | Perennial crown; typical GrassBase lower glume **one-veined**, about 6–8 mm, upper glume ~11–15 mm; panicle may be narrow or open across varieties | A. oligantha; published typical measurements **must not** be applied rigidly to all infraspecific treatments |

| Texas bluegrass (*P. arachnifera*) | Perennial rhizomes; **strictly dioecious**. | Japanese brome (*Bromus japonicus*) | Annual; lower glume usually 3–5-veined; lemma ~7–9 mm, awn 8–13 mm arising ≥1.5 mm below lemma tip | Hairy woodland brome lower glume typically one-veined, perennial |

| Texas brome (*B. texensis*) | Annual; spikelets ~20 mm bearing 6–8 florets; **one-veined lower** and **three-veined upper** glume; lemma 9–12 mm | Lanceolate brome and Japanese brome; regional/native label still requires BONAP county check |
| Sixweeks fescue (*Festuca octoflora*, older *Vulpia*) | Annual with contracted narrow panicle; spikelets ~4–10 mm, 5–17 florets, lemma 2.7–6.5 mm and 0.3–6 mm awns | Perennial Texas fescue; don't require 'long awn' because variety/age varies |
| Texas fescue (*F. versuta*) | Perennial tufted rooted base, no long rhizomes/stolons, 0.5–1 mm membrane ligule, blades ~2–5 mm | Annual, thread-fine *F. octoflora*. Species-specific floret dimensions still require deeper source review |

## Critical evidence boundaries

### 1. Wild oat: historical flora versus modern FNA

The existing *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas* material describes *Avena fatua* as commonly having **3–4 florets**, while the Flora of North America account describes **2(3) florets**. The database's new `floral_count` record records this source conflict explicitly. No number was used as an exclusive yes/no filter. **Requires exact regional specimen and definition of developed versus reduced distal floret to resolve.** Links: [FNA Avena fatua](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?taxon=240108), [original regional flora excerpt](https://fwbg.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/FNCT_1077-1352-Acor-Zanni.pdf). Do not reuse the copyrighted flora illustrations.

### 2. Texas bluegrass: sex specificity

*Poa arachnifera* is **dioecious**. Three silky cobwebs on the *female* floret callus are diagnostically useful; their apparent absence on a *male* plant is expected and must **not** exclude the species. *P. annua* has a different, usually gynomonoecious reproductive system and is normally an annual without rhizomes. More photographs are needed of matched male/female inflorescences, each with a floret scale. [FNA Texas bluegrass](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=0&taxauthid=1&tid=629190), [FNA annual bluegrass](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=0&taxauthid=1&tid=236928).

### 3. Aristida glume veins outperform awn silhouette

Both *A. purpurea* and *A. oligantha* can show widely spreading long threeawns. The useful separation is the **actual lower glume vein count and length** plus **annual/perennial rooted base**. Measurements for *A. purpurea* vary among subspecies/varieties; no trait should be treated as a universal unique cutoff. [GrassBase oligantha](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:325343-2/general-information); [GrassBase purpurea](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:327975-2/general-information).

### 4. True ligules and glume veins in Bromus

Brome ligules are fundamentally **membranous**, sometimes appearing ragged or bearing hairs. Hairy outer sheaths and collar hairs are not the inner ligule. Lower glume **vein numbers**, lemma length, maturity of awns and real perennial rootstock take precedence over panicle shape. Sources: [FNA Bromus](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=0&pid=&taxauthid=1&tid=60225), [Japanese brome](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=0&taxauthid=1&tid=236949), [lanceolate brome](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=0&taxauthid=1&tid=236952), [Texas brome (Kew)](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:37176-2/general-information).

## Practical next-photo questions (not yet marked missing)

Before requesting more images, the independent gallery-quality lane should check the photos already available for:

1. *Aristida* — a lower glume **backlit with veins visible** and mm ruler, all three mature awns in the **same** floret, and a connected crown.
2. *Poa* — the **root-connected rhizome** of Texas bluegrass, female callus three silky webs, corresponding male floret, and annual-bluegrass short flowering culm.
3. *Bromus* — lower/upper glume vein macro, intact floret lemma with awn insertion and a ruler, plus rooted perennial *B. pubescens* crown. Young versus dry mature heads matter.
4. *Festuca* — a true membrane at inner collar and connected annual or perennial rooted bases, plus measured spikelet and lemma.
5. *Nassella* — sharp **callus**, constricted lemma **crown**, one twice-bent awn, and close inner ligule.
6. *Avena* — one mature whole spikelet with every developed/reduced floret counted and disarticulation points visible; corresponding lemma awn insertion.
7. Winter/dormant coverage should be recorded **only if the existing galleries do not already show the useful stage**, following the one shared Photo Needs Queue, no new batch spreadsheets.
