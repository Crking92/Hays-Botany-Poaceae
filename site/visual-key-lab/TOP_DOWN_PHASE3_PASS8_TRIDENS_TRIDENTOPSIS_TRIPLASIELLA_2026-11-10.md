## Six species and practical field comparisons

| Grass | Main reproductive characters to examine | Look-alike or caution |
|---|---|---|
| White tridens — *Tridens albescens* | **Very narrow** 8–25 cm long by 0.6–1.5 cm wide head, close-appressed branches; 4–10 florets per 4–10 mm spikelet; glumes **near equal, 3–4 mm, both one-veined**; lemma 3–4 mm, three-veined and comparatively smooth along nerves; tiny hair-fringe ligule | Slim tridens can be equally narrow but has **hairy lemma nerves** and often longer 8–13 mm spikelets. Panicle color is NOT reliable |
| Purpletop — *Tridens flavus* | Large open 15–40 cm head with long **divergent or drooping branches**, often bare in basal part; 4–8 florets per 5–10 mm spikelet; lemma 3–5 mm with three veins and **small vein-tip points**, hairy toward base; tiny ciliate membrane/hair-looking ligule | *Triplasiella eragrostoides* also has a wide head; its palea keels and usually smaller lemmas differ |
| Texas tridens — *Tridens texanus* | Open 5–16 cm panicle with flexuous, sparsely flowered lower branches; 6–12 florets per 6–13 mm spikelet; **both glumes ~2–3 mm** and one-veined; lemma ~4–5 mm, hairy below on three veins, shallowly two-toothed | White tridens has longer glumes about as long as lemma and glabrous to less-hairy lemma nerves |
| Buckley's fluffgrass — *Tridentopsis buckleyana* | Open 10–28 cm head with distant, stiff spreading 4–13 cm branches; only **3–5 florets** per 7–10 mm spikelet, and **LOWER glume ~5–6 mm LONGER than upper ~4–5 mm**, each one-veined; ciliate membrane ligule 0.4–1 mm | Slim tridens has a dense narrow head, usually 5–11 florets, glumes variable from one to seven veins |
| Slim tridens — *Tridentopsis mutica* | Very narrow 7–20(25) cm by 0.3–0.8 cm panicle; compressed 8–13 mm spikelets with 5–11 florets, one-to-seven-veined glumes ~3–5.5 mm; **hairy lemma nerves**, three-veined **awnless** lemmas; true ciliate membrane 0.5–1 mm | Similar silhouette to White tridens; **glume venation varies by historic varieties** and should not be fixed to one universal state |
| Lovegrass tridens — *Triplasiella eragrostoides* | Broad open **10–30 × 8–20 cm** head; palea KEELS smooth or rough/scabrous, unlike the densely ciliate keels of true *Triplasis*; older regional keys: lemma often ~2–3 mm and primarily the MIDNERVE extending as a tiny point; **no upper-sheath cleistogamous spikelets** | Old `Tridens eragrostoides` and `Tridentopsis eragrostoides` are synonyms of this single accepted species. Historic descriptions of ligule need source crosswalk and actual 10–20× closeup |

### Key corrections written in the species JSON

1. **Accepted taxonomy:** `Triplasiella eragrostoides` preserved as current Hays Botany name. The 2016 Peterson et al. diagnosis establishes its distinction from `Triplasis` using the broad panicle, palea keels, and **absence** of upper-sheath hidden flowers. Historical `Tridens` and `Tridentopsis` combinations are recorded as synonym references, **not counted as more indexed species**.
2. **Short ciliate membranes:** `Tridens flavus`, `T. texanus`, and `Tridentopsis mutica` have sources describing tiny ciliate membranous ligules, so broad choices `hairs` AND `membrane` are now accepted when the user cannot distinguish the tissue from a photo. Anatomically this does **not** mean two unrelated structures are present.
3. **Triplasiella collar caution:** Current old `hairs` field code did not account for older regional membranous ligule reports under `Tridens eragrostoides`. Both broad collar appearance states are provisionally allowed pending true same-plant collar and name-concept audit; **2016 genus diagnosis alone does not certify its ligule**.
4. **Tridentopsis mutica infraspecific variation:** GrassBase under older `Tridens muticus` permits 1–7 veins on glumes. Historical var. `muticus` and var. `elongatus` can be separated with a more detailed specimen key; do not project one variety's single vein count onto the entire species.
5. **No false full-ID claim:** White Tridens and Slim Tridens may both have very narrow seedheads; a source-backed head category alone is an invitation to **dissect the lemma** rather than a confirmed name.

## Needed exact photo roles for the existing separate gallery QA

**Before collecting anything new, check already cleared photographs against the actual botanical structure.** Preserve source, photographer, permission, license and exact-image identity.

- *T. albescens* versus *Tridentopsis mutica*: 1:1 scale mature spikelets, glumes laid apart showing veins, and **lemma nerve hairs** (glabrous versus conspicuous hairy).
- *T. flavus* and *T. texanus*: entire attached mature panicle showing whether branches are very long/drooping or smaller/flexuous, basal naked branch lengths, and 3-veined lemma tips.
- *Tridentopsis buckleyana*: widely spaced branches, **lower glume longer than upper**, 3–5 florets counted on intact 7–10 mm spikelet, short ciliate membrane under collar hair.
- *Triplasiella*: a true **palea with both keels** large enough to distinguish smooth/scabrous versus long-ciliate, and upper leaf sheaths that can be opened without confusing background grasses. Existing photo warning about a different bristly grass behind a panel must remain.
- Autumn and winter: dry panicle attached to a known rooted plant, spikelets still diagnosable, and actual perennial basal crown where helpful. Do not make all stages mandatory or request duplicates.
