**Date:** 2026-11-10. **Scope:** 28 original Hays Botany Poaceae species across 16 genera: *Paspalum, Digitaria, Setaria, Urochloa, Sorghum, Phragmites, Arundo, Zizania, Zizaniopsis, Leersia, Phalaris, Polypogon, Tripsacum, Dinebra, Hopia,* and *Echinochloa*.

Twenty-seven previously provisional records gained source-checked character evidence in this batch. **60/112** total species across the key now have partial character checks, not complete identification verification. All 112 records remain in the one multi-access key.

## Main fixes completed

- **Sparse versus dense bristles:** *Setaria reverchonii* has a **sparse interrupted** reproductive axis; bristles mostly around upper/terminal spikelets, not a dense foxtail cylinder. Its habit is a hard-based tuft, **not assumed rhizomatous**. New whole-head choice uses the exact-species Clifton Ladd CC BY 4.0 flowering photograph. *S. parviflora* remains the dense, knotty-rhizome bristlegrass comparison. *S. scheelei* has a distinct long, tapered bristlegrass head with long individual bristles, but still overlaps the generic foxtail category.
- **Many versus few one-sided racemes:** New whole-head option for *Paspalum urvillei* (Vaseygrass), often 7–36 ascending racemes. Dallisgrass (*P. dilatatum*) typically has 2–8 and notably silky-margined spikelets; *P. floridanum* often 2–5; *P. setaceum* 1–5; *P. pubiflorum* usually 2–5(–7); *P. distichum* normally two but sometimes 3–4 and clearly stolon-rooting in moist ground. These counts **overlap**, so never infer a certain species ID from raceme count alone.
- **Digitaria:** *D. cognata* has a strongly **open tumbleweed-type** panicle with long-pedicelled spikelets and a membrane ligule, in contrast to the digitate racemes of *D. ciliaris*. Taxonomic concept and species-level anatomy still require a separate pass.
- **Signalgrass:** *Urochloa texana* is an annual that can spread and root at lower nodes, with a **fringe-of-hairs ligule**, not a conspicuous projecting membrane. Kew/GrassBase under historic names *Brachiaria reptans* and *B. fasciculata* supports the similar one-sided multi-raceme architecture, hairs and reclining lower stems of *U. reptans* and *U. fusca* respectively.
- **Sorghum:** Annual *S. bicolor* includes compact awnless and open awned forms, while perennial *S. halepense* spreads by extensive rhizomes and may have an open or contracted panicle. Never decide sorghum identity on a detached head alone.
- **Arundo vs Phragmites:** Both are tall rhizomatous canes with flowering plumes, so remain together at broad-key level. In the regional flora, *Arundo donax* has nearly glabrous **rachilla joints** but densely hairy **lemmas**, whereas *Phragmites australis* has long-hairy rachilla joints and glabrous glumes/lemmas. This becomes a high-priority character-photo task in Phase 3. *Phragmites* lineage (native versus nonnative) is a separate determination from species ID.
- **Wetland sexes and habitat:** Texas wild-rice (*Zizania texana*) has aquatic, current-swept ribbon leaves and lower dangling staminate versus upper erect pistillate branches; female spikelets have **9–35 mm awns**. It no longer requires a guessed rhizome as the growth-form selection. Giant cutgrass (*Zizaniopsis miliacea*) has both sexes **intermixed in a large loose panicle**, a rhizomatous base, and a 6–20 mm membranous ligule. Two distinct photograph choices now represent these structures. Texas wild-rice is endangered and endemic to upper San Marcos River: observe only from permitted access; **do not touch, collect, enter habitat, or attempt root exposure**.
- **Other genera:** Verified *Phalaris caroliniana* dense **spikelike panicle** (not a true spike); *Polypogon monspeliensis* conspicuous **glume** awns (not necessarily lemma awns); *Tripsacum dactyloides* separate staminate upper / hardened pistillate lower flowering sections and a fringed membranous ligule; *Hopia obtusa* can have both scaly rhizomes and long stolons (historic *Panicum obtusum*); *Leersia oryzoides* creeping rhizome and open panicle; *Echinochloa crus-galli* absent true ligule and variably awned or awnless lemma; *Dinebra panicea* (historic *Leptochloa panicea*) has numerous slender unilateral racemes and membranous ligule, though subspecies details remain provisional.
