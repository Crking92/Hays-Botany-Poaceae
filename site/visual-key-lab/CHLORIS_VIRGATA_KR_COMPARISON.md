Gould (1975), The Grasses of Texas, exact species treatment on printed p.321, supplied by the owner. Facing p.320 and the following C. verticillata entry are separate taxa. No scan or figure is republished.

## Decision

The KR combination retains Chloris virgata as unresolved, not supported and not automatically excluded. Shared tuft, fingers, membranous/ciliate collar, hairless sheath, basal blade hairs and conditional surface stolons overlap. The supplied page does not establish absence of KR-specific papilla bases, rachis/pedicel grooves or hairs, sessile/pedicelled spikelet partners, or their first-glume states. Silence cannot justify negative assignments.

Useful contrasts are annuality; 4–20 upright terminal fingers; unequal hairless lanceolate glumes with rough midnerve; long hairs at the upper margins of the lower fertile lemma; its usually 5–15 mm awn; and a smaller, similarly shaped upper sterile floret with its own 3–9.5 mm awn. The fertile lemma itself measures 2.5–4.2 mm; this is NOT a hair measurement. Gould's usual fertile awn range is not treated as an absolute exclusion interval. KR's awn-length source dispute remains visible.

Gould describes one sterile floret, rarely two: total two, occasionally three including the fertile floret. Preserve the existing Diggs two-floret account and supplementary occasional-three report; both numerical possibilities now rank positively. Missing or variant ligules are not assigned zero length. Culm glabrousness is not transferred to a node-specific choice. No blade width interval is invented from the stated maximum 1.5 cm.

## Secondary reference review

Directly read Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, printed p.1259 named species account and p.1257 species key through the owner's connected reference PDF. The account corroborates annuality, 4–20 branches, 5–10 cm branch length, gibbous 2.5–4.2 mm fertile lemma, and one smaller sterile floret. The key gives 5–15 mm fertile awns and upper-margin hair tufts to approximately 2–3 mm. These do not erase Gould's occasional second sterile floret. Existing H&L and supplemental evidence is preserved.
