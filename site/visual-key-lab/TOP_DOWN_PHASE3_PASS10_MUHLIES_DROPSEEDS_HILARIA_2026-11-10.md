## Muhlenbergia — seven species already in the 112-grass collection

| Species | Higher-value anatomy versus look-alikes | Uncertainty / photo target |
|---|---|---|
| Lindheimer muhly — *Muhlenbergia lindheimeri* | Dense large tuft, TRUE membranous ligule **8–15 mm**; fairly broad folded blades; Kew narrow contracted panicle, UT Texas specimen an older **more open/feathery** panicle; one-floret **2–3.5 mm spikelets** with one-veined glumes generally approaching floret length, short or missing awn **0–4 mm** | Both `long_narrow_panicle` and `airy` visual states allowed because of source/age descriptions. A wider flowering head is NOT automatic hybrid proof. Need matched early/aging panicle and inner collar with ruler |
| Seep muhly — *M. reverchonii* | Fine tightly tufted foliage, **1–2 mm rolled** blades, shorter true membrane ligule mostly **2–4(9) mm**, open 10–30 cm branching panicle; one-floret **3.5–5 mm** spikelet with **shorter 1–2.5(3) mm glumes** and generally short **0.5–3(6) mm** awn | Pink color is not diagnostic; actual one-veined glumes and 3-veined lemma with ruler needed |
| Canyon muhly — *M. ×involuta* | **Accepted Central Texas hybrid**, parents *M. lindheimeri × M. reverchonii*; intermediate erect clump and **3–12 mm membranous ligule**; open ascending 18–40 × 2–5(7) cm panicle; one-floret **3–4 mm** spikelet, equal ~2–2.5 mm glumes, **two tiny lemma teeth with central 0.5–2 mm awn** | **Zero hosted key photos**. Source-labeled real photos have been located at UT Austin PlantPics, but no rehosting without exact-image permission/rights. A local hybrid identification needs voucher/expert determination, not a photo-only inference |
| Pine muhly — *M. dubia* | Dense non-rhizomatous tuft, rolled thin leaves, **firm 4–10 mm ligule**; narrow gray-green head **10–40 × 0.6–2.4 cm**; one-floret **3.8–5 mm** spikelets, glumes around 2–3 mm; lemma either unawned or with straight awn to ~6 mm | `none_obvious` and `long` kept as field awn appearances; authentic same-head floret and connected perennial crown still needed |
| Nimblewill — *M. schreberi* | Low MAT from **surface stolons** with rooting nodes; short erose/ciliolate true membrane ligule ~**0.2–0.5 mm**; head **5–15 cm**; one-floret **1.8–2.8 mm** spikelets, lower glume tiny or absent, upper glume ~0.1–0.3 mm; lemma awn **1.5–5 mm** | Awn may look short or long depending on distance; never confuse a stolon with a buried rhizome. Winter dormant brown mat can be useful but is not itself proof |
| Aparejo grass — *M. utilis* | **Scaly elongated UNDERGROUND rhizomes**, very thin leaves, eciliate ~0.5 mm membrane ligule; **1–4 cm interrupted** narrow panicle; one-floret **1.6–2 mm** spikelets with both one-veined glumes **0.6–1(1.3) mm**, awnless three-veined lemma | Direct nimblewill comparison: shorter head, full visible glumes rather than minute, and underground scaly rhizome versus surface stolon |
| Tumblegrass — *M. paniculata* | Curving/scythe main head axis with few widely spaced ONE-SIDED rays; ~3–4 mm solitary sessile spikelets on angular branch rachises, each with one floret, lower glume ~half upper; mature head detaches and tumbles | Historical **Schedonnardus paniculatus** is synonymous, NOT a separate species. Broad head accepts `wiry` and `one_sided`. Texas A&M calls perennial; Kew broad range says annual or perennial—do not infer life cycle from detached tumblehead |

## The three indexed Sporobolus dropseeds

| Species | Visual/head clue | Floral microscope and seed caution |
|---|---|---|
| Tall/composite dropseed — *Sporobolus compositus* | **Perennial**, contracted ~5–26 cm panicle often sheathed, inner ligule a tiny ciliate membrane/hair-like fringe | ~4.5–5.4 mm spikelets with generally smoother, **glabrous lemma** compared with *S. clandestinus*. Many keys describe gelatinizing/slipping pericarp when wet, but UT Austin's Bob Harms documents test ambiguity; no binary automatic filter |
| Hidden/rough dropseed — *S. clandestinus* | Perennial, generally **5–10 cm** narrow/sheath-enclosed head, 0.2–0.3 mm hair-fringe ligule | ~5–7 mm single-floret spikelet, ~4–5 mm **pubescent** lemma, unequal one-veined glumes; pericarp response must not replace lemma microscopy |
| Poverty dropseed — *S. vaginiflorus* | **ANNUAL** with tiny **1–4 cm terminal AND axillary heads**, frequently partially inside leaf sheaths | ~3–5 mm one-floret awnless spikelets and hair-fringe ligule. Capture rooted annual and opened axillary sheath; dry November–February tiny heads may be diagnostically valuable |

### Critical Sporobolus complex warning

The [UT Austin Plant Resources Center — Two Sporobolus Complexes in Texas](https://w3.biosci.utexas.edu/prc/SPORO/index.html) records numerous disputed specialist identifications of vouchers. Its [pericarp review](https://w3.biosci.utexas.edu/prc/SPORO/SPORO-pericarp-comp.html) explains why apparent gelatinization versus 'loosening' is not a trustworthy standalone field yes/no. Do not promote an uncertain wet-grain response, lemma photo with insufficient focus, or common name **rough dropseed** to an exclusive species identification. Variety/synonym concepts also differ historically. Use several authenticated ripe florets, lemma hair visibility, location and actual grain response jointly.

## Hilaria side-by-side

| Species | Both share | Better separation |
|---|---|---|
| Curlymesquite — *Hilaria belangeri* | **Repeated TRIADS** of a central fertile spikelet and two largely male lateral spikelets, in short false-spike heads. The triad is not a three-awn floret. | Low mat/sod with long **aboveground stolons**, conspicuously hairy **rooting nodes**, head usually 2–3.5 cm; short ~0.5–1 mm membrane ligule |
| Tobosa — *H. mutica* | Repeated three-spikelet fascicles with central fertile and two lateral predominantly male spikelets; short torn membrane ligule | Coarser erect tuft usually 30–50 cm with SHORT underground **rhizomes**, close-packed false-spike rays, rough leaves and short glume tips |

Never confuse stolons (surface stems), rhizomes (underground scaled stems), true rooting nodes, white node hair tufts, or tiny true inner membrane ligules.

## Photo roles for later, separate gallery-quality phase

**No photos inspected or assigned this pass.** Before requesting or downloading new images, check the current legal-credit galleries and cleared archives, then add *only* actual anatomy gaps to the ONE master Photo Needs Queue.

- Compare Lindheimer, seep and Canyon muhlies: single mature spikelets **to same scale**, separated ONE-veined glumes, THREE-veined lemma, awn arising between tiny two teeth where appropriate; ligules **with mm ruler**; whole panicles at appropriate age and a shared relative-size reference.
- Canyon hybrid: use existing **UT Austin PlantPics source-identified images** as external links only until exact photographer/site licensing for rehosting can be validated; keep zero-photo count, never substitute a parent photo labeled hybrid.
- Pine muhly: single awned vs unawned spikelet, firm 4–10 mm ligule, very thin folded/inrolled blade, non-rhizomatous living crown.
- Nimblewill vs aparejo: connected **stolon** versus scaled **rhizome**, actual rooted node and >10× inner ligule; **0.1–0.3 mm** versus **0.6–1 mm** upper glumes and lemma awn from same spikelet.
- Tumblegrass: a whole curved flowering axis showing ONE-SIDED rays, a single appressed spikelet with sessile attachment, and detached mature tumbling head.
- Dropseeds: exact **hairy vs glabrous lemma** under dissecting scope, compared matched glumes and several ripe grains; if needed a documented carefully prepared pericarp test with limitations, not the only voucher destroyed. Open hidden/axillary sheaths of poverty dropseed.
- Hilaria: three separate spikelets in a triad with labels **central fertile / outer mostly male**, same-head ripe and young structures, plus field-connected stolon or rhizome; winter/dry fascicle if identifiable.
