## Six Paspalum species — the distinctions that matter

| Species | Main floral architecture | Important diagnostic macro | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knotgrass — *Paspalum distichum* | Usually **two** near-digitate branches, occasionally a third | Usually **solitary** 2.4–3.2 mm spikelets; lower glume absent or tiny triangular scale; upper glume and sterile lemma 3-veined | Long rooting wet-ground stolons; allow a third branch in the broad key |
| Dallisgrass — *P. dilatatum* | Typically 2–7 spreading one-sided racemes | **Paired**, pointed 2.3–4 mm spikelets with long silky margins; upper glume AND sterile lemma usually **5–7-veined** | Short knotty rhizomes under upright bunch; not long stolon mats |
| Florida paspalum — *P. floridanum* | Usually 1–6 often long racemes | **Paired broad nearly round, glabrous** 2.9–4.1 mm spikelets; **5-veined upper glume, 3-veined sterile lemma** | Size/count alone overlap Dallisgrass; examine BOTH bracts |
| *P. pubiflorum* | Usually 2–7 spaced branches | Paired 2.8–3.6 mm spikelets; upper glume and lower sterile lemma **3-veined**, glabrous or only minutely hairy (under 0.1 mm) | Lower shoots decumbent, often root at nodes; much less silk than Dallisgrass/Vasey |
| Thin paspalum — *P. setaceum* | 1–6 racemes, **terminal and axillary**; axillary heads sometimes hidden in sheaths | Paired **1.4–2.6 mm** spikelets, absent lower glume, 3-veined upper glume, very short ciliate/glandular hairs variable | Nine FNA varieties; do not assume one hair phenotype or insist on only terminal heads |
| Vaseygrass — *P. urvillei* | Usually (4)10–30 racemes along a tall main axis | Paired **1.8–2.8 mm** silky-margined spikelets; **three-veined upper glume and sterile lemma** | Looks like Dallisgrass until spikelet size, venation and branch count checked |

**Field sequence:** (1) inspect the whole head and distinguish terminal pair vs multiple one-sided branches; (2) look for single vs paired spikelets; (3) magnify upper glume/sterile lemma separately and count veins; (4) look at hair length and placement; (5) inspect the *connected* stolon, rhizome and actual inner ligule. Use `Can't tell / skip` when flowers are absent.

### Knotgrass safe-choice correction

Previous `P. distichum` head state included only `paired_spikes`. FNA also documents a *third* branch, so the broad state now allows both `paired_spikes` and `one_sided`. **This does not mean its typical head has many racemes**; it prevents a valid three-branch plant from being falsely excluded based on an over-literal image choice.

## Three Urochloa species — useful cross-genus comparison

| Species | Spikelet & LOWER glume | Other useful evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Texas signalgrass — *Urochloa texana* | Paired spikelets **~5–6 mm**, developed lower glume around **two-thirds** their length, typically **5–7 veins** | Ciliate **membrane** ligule around 1–1.5 mm; mature fertile lemma 3.5–4 mm and transversely wrinkled |
| Brownseed signalgrass — *U. fusca* (older *Brachiaria fasciculata*) | Paired spikelets **2.4–3 mm**; lower glume around **one-quarter to one-third**, usually **5-veined** | 3–20 often secondarily branched racemes, annual lower stems sometimes rooting at nodes, hair ligule |
| Creeping signalgrass — *U. reptans* (older *Brachiaria reptans*) | Paired **1.5–2.2 mm** spikelets; lower glume only **one-eighth to one-quarter**, often **veinless** and truncate | 5–15 crowded short racemes; hairy pedicels, hair-fringe ligule, rugose fertile lemma, low rooting nodes |

### Group separation: absence versus shortening of lower glume

*Paspalum* species in this collection generally **lack a functional lower glume** (knotgrass can have a small triangular vestige). *Urochloa* species generally **retain a developed lower glume**, very short in *U. reptans* and markedly larger in *U. texana*. Examine an attached mature spikelet from the correct side; a hidden lower glume in a photograph is **unknown**, not botanically absent.

### Texas signalgrass ligule correction

The existing key had *U. texana* scored only for a hair-fringe ligule. Kew GrassBase (under *Brachiaria texana*) and UT Austin descriptions instead document a **1–1.8 mm ciliate membrane** that can *appear* hairlike. The key now retains both `hairs` and `membrane` visual answers; the anatomical fact is **a ciliate membrane**, not two unrelated ligule types. Do not simply replace it with the generic hair ligule used for some other *Urochloa* species.

### Caution about taxonomy and region

Descriptions for *U. fusca* and *U. reptans* on Kew POWO are linked to their old **Brachiaria** name concepts. Store these as source-linked descriptions, not silent new taxon identities. **BONAP NAPA** remains separate authority for Hays County and west/north adjacent-county nativity. Invasive/introduced identification comparisons remain in the key but do not become native restoration recommendations.

## Photo-target decisions (gallery QA has not been done)

Before adding any photo request to the single shared Photo Needs Queue, inspect current photo rights and published galleries. If a gap remains, pursue:

1. *Paspalum distichum*: attached pair versus three-branch head and an actual **solitary spikelet** row with an exposed rooting stolon.
2. Dallisgrass versus Vaseygrass: pair of **same-scale spikelets**, separately positioned upper glumes and sterile lemmas with visible veins, margin-hair comparison.
3. Florida paspalum: **hairless rounded spikelets**, upper glume five veins versus lower sterile lemma three, both on same specimen.
4. *P. pubiflorum*: tiny upper-glume hairs or glabrous margin, rooted lower decumbent culm and paired spikelets.
5. Thin paspalum: **hidden axillary panicle emerging from sheath**, a typical terminal panicle, and very short true membranous ligule.
6. Signalgrasses: exposed **lower glume** with mm scale in all three; show 5–6 vs 2.4–3 vs 1.5–2.2 mm spikelets; show true Texas signalgrass ciliate membrane, separate from collar hair.
7. Useful dry/winter stage: inspect only where an attached dry inflorescence or rooting basal structure adds real November–February diagnostic value.

Do not claim an image proves anatomy merely because it depicts the correct species. Photo rights are exact-image based, and **no new photos were copied** in this pass.
