## Anatomical differences by wetland species

| Species | Published botanical structures | Evidence boundary / comparison |
|---|---|---|
| **Inland sea oats (*Chasmanthium latifolium*)** | Open drooping panicle on relatively long stalks, pendulous conspicuously flattened 20–40mm 'fish-like' spikelets, usually **6–20 florets**. Narrow basal glumes and sometimes sterile lower lemmas; fertile lemmas have many veins (~11–15). Short stout rhizomes and a ciliate membrane ligule. | The whole hanging object is a many-floret SPIKELET, **not one seed**. Compare smaller/short-pedicelled woodoats. Photos of caryopsis and glumes must show exact separated parts |
| **Giant reed (*Arundo donax*)** | Thick cane and broad blade base with brownish wedge, usually large upright 30–60cm plume. Spikelets 2–5-flowered, with **LONG hairs low on the LEMMA** and a mostly **GLABROUS rachilla between florets**; bifid lemma tip with a short central awn. | Main diagnostic difference from Phragmites is the location of silky hairs, not height, head color or habitat. Some morphological/seasonal overlap; same-plant floret separation pending |
| **Common reed (*Phragmites australis*)** | Rhizomatous 1–4m reed and 15–35cm plume; 3–10-flowered spikelets with **SILKY 6–10mm hairs on the RACHILLA INTERNODES**, comparatively **glabrous lemmas**, smaller unequal glumes. | Native *americanus*, introduced *australis*, and Gulf Coast *berlandieri* lineages **cannot be assigned from general photos**. Local management/control decisions require expert lineage evidence, potentially genetics. A species-level ID is not an invasive label |
| **Texas wild-rice (*Zizania texana*)** | Endangered Hays-only aquatic perennial with streaming submerged ribbon leaves and pronounced midvein; emergent panicle has **LOWER dangling branches of MALE awnless spikelets**, **UPPER erect branches of FEMALE spikelets with ~9–35mm awns**. One-flowered GLUMELESS spikelets. | **OBSERVE ONLY from lawful location, NO touching, harvesting, collecting, moving through plants or entering protected habitat.** Source global GrassBase calls annual in one record, but official TPWD/FWS/POWO accept perennial. Lower/upper SEX BRANCH locations matter |
| **Giant cutgrass (*Zizaniopsis miliacea*)** | Large emergent rhizomatous wetland perennial 2–3m with serrulate blade edges and **6–20mm TRUE ECILIATE MEMBRANE** ligule. Very open 30–60cm panicle. **Male and female spikelets on SAME branches** (males toward base, females nearer tip), both GLUMELESS, 1-flowered, female lemma with very SHORT ~2–3mm awn. | In Texas wild-rice, whole LOWER branches are male and higher branches female; female awns much longer. Leaf/submergence and ligule are good supplemental evidence |

### Protected Hays County species rule (reaffirmed)

TPWD identifies *Zizania texana* as a federally and Texas **endangered species known only from the upper San Marcos River, Hays County**. All picture-first identification instructions are **noninvasive and non-disturbing**. Do not direct public users to cut, pull, reach into water, dig, collect, move plants or enter closed protected stretches. Any additional habitat photos must be lawful, authorized and source-licensed.

### Morphological and lineage uncertainties retained

- *Phragmites australis* is not synonymous with non-native/invasive lineage; the key cannot determine native/introduced/Gulf Coast status from species-level panicle photographs. Genetic test/expert local voucher may be warranted for control decisions.
- In *Arundo* versus *Phragmites*, the hair PLACEMENT on rachilla or lemma is high diagnostic value. A photograph that merely looks fuzzy is **not anatomical evidence**.
- Global GrassBase *Zizania texana* life-history 'annual' conflicts with Kew's accepted perennial and TPWD/FWS perennial ecology. Record and prioritize expert conservation source for this endemic; do not apply a general annual first filter.
- *Zizaniopsis* female/male may appear intermixed from a distance, but TPWD/Kew both document male and female **on the same branch** rather than separate upper/lower branches.
- *Chasmanthium* flower counts and panicle/head sizes vary by regional source and fruit stage; count actual florets in an attached mature flattened spikelet.
