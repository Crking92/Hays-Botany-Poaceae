## Dichanthelium — seasonal differences for Central Texas

The genus usually shows a **spring (vernal) open flowering head** and a **later short secondary or concealed often self-pollinating head**, sometimes alongside smaller upper leaves. Not every species has an obvious winter basal rosette. **NEVER let “doesn't have a rosette” eliminate all rosette grasses.**

| Species | Real anatomy and seasonal distinctions | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Variable panicgrass — *Dichanthelium commutatum* | Open spring panicles ~5–12 cm, often April–June; late June–fall secondary mostly **cleistogamous** panicles; ~2.2–3.2 mm pubescent spikelets; short lower glume ~0.7–1.8 mm, and a **0.3 mm ciliate membranous true ligule**. Leaves often asymmetric, cordate/clasping near base. | WFO describes minute fringe or apparently absent ligule. Allow `hairs`, `membrane`, and `absent` as visual ANSWERS when worn/too small to tell—not proof three different anatomical ligules. Need magnified collar and same-plant time series |
| Few-flowered panicgrass — *Dichanthelium oligosanthes* | Well-defined **WINTER basal rosette** of ~2–6 cm blades; spring ~5–9 cm panicles, 6–60 spikelets; late reduced branched flowering heads may be hidden; ~2.7–4.2 mm broad spikelets and often orange/purple basal upper-glume mark; 1–3 mm HAIR ligule. | Texas A&M Scribner rosette and UT var. scribnerianum are variety-specific examples. Color, leaf form and rosette alone do not prove this species/variety |
| Lindheimer's panicgrass — *Dichanthelium lindheimeri* | In the older **D. acuminatum var. lindheimeri** USFS account, small ~4–7 cm spring head, **very tiny 1–2 mm spikelets**, and 4–5 mm white hair ligule; late low, stiffly spreading branches with reduced leaves. | Kew accepts **D. lindheimeri**, whereas historical flora uses *Panicum lindheimeri* or *D. acuminatum* variety/subspecies. Names crosswalk; old character ranges may reflect a broader taxon concept. No photo reidentification claimed |
| Corm-based panicgrass — *Dichanthelium pedicellatum* | **NOT ROSETTED** in A.C. Gibson Williamson County description. Each stem from shallow cormlike swollen base up to ~5 × 3 mm with axillary buds and attached fibrous roots. 0.25–0.5 mm inner HAIR ligule (separate from longer blade-base hairs). Spikelets ~3–3.5 mm; lower glume ~1.5–2.1 mm one-veined; upper glume ~2.5–2.8 mm nine-veined; sterile lemma ~7–9 veined, hardened upper fertile floret. | Do not mistake detached fibrous roots for a proven corm, or claim all Dichanthelium are rosetted. This is a one-county voucher description, not universal population measurements |

### Seasonal field picture plan — target only actual missing examples after approved-gallery check

1. D. oligosanthes: attached **November–February rooted winter rosette**, later same-plant spring culm and adult 2.7–4.2 mm spikelets; a separate secondary late flowering stalk if still intact.
2. D. pedicellatum: expose **small cormlike base and attached root system** (millimeter ruler), photograph the whole stem and a true inner ligule; **NO generic winter rosette** target.
3. D. lindheimeri: spring taller small-flowered head, late short leafy branch stage, 4–5 mm white hair ligule from the older variety name concept. Require actual correctly identified specimens before cross-stage claim.
4. D. commutatum: a matched spring open and autumn partly hidden spikelet head; a ciliate *membrane under longer hair tips* photograph and measured spikelets.

## Panicum — different life cycles, collars and lower florets

| Species | Practical distinctive structures | Source caution |
|---|---|---|
| Hall's panicgrass — *Panicum hallii* | Blue-green **perennial tuft**, dry curling narrow basal blades, 6–20(30) cm ascending, rather organized panicles with close spikelet clusters; spikelets ~2.2–3.7(3.9) mm with 5-veined lower glume approximately 1/3–2/3 spikelet length; **smooth shiny mature fertile lemma**. Short ciliate membrane (~0.5–1.5 mm). | One region describes sterile lower floret PALEA nearly full-length, whereas Kew GrassBase calls it absent/insignificant. Leave palea diagnostic provisional; variety branch differences |
| Switchgrass — *Panicum virgatum* | Large perennial with **long stout scaly rhizomes**, wide airy ~15–55 cm head, often visible dry in winter. Distinguish **hair TRIANGLE on upper leaf blade above collar** from the actual anatomical **ciliate membrane ligule**. Membrane can erode, leaving a visible white hair fringe. FNA allows broad ~2.5–8 mm spikelet range, Kew typical ~3–5 mm. | Avoid arbitrary spikelet size cutoff or treating a worn hair-fringe as a different ligule type. Upland/bottomland forms vary, and Kew state-level nativity isn't county evidence |
| Witchgrass — *Panicum capillare* | **Annual** with diffuse ~13–50 cm panicle often breaking off and tumbling in autumn. Leaf sheaths have hairs arising on conspicuous **papillose swollen bases**. True ligule is a very short **ciliate membrane** that may look like hairs. Two-floret ~1.9–4 mm spikelet; lower glume only ~1/3–1/2, 1–3-veined. | Purple lovegrass tumbles too, but has many florets per narrow spikelet and a perennial root base; actual annual rooted habit needed |
| Kleingrass — *Panicum coloratum* | **Introduced African** short knotty rhizomatous tuft; panicle often more contracted than witchgrass but variable; spikelets ~2.5–3.5 mm, lower glume ~1–1.5 mm (approximately 1/3 spikelet), 1–3-veined; fertile upper floret shiny and lightly beaked. The LOWER floret is **STAMINATE**, with a developed ~2–3 mm palea. | Hall's panicgrass lower floret is described sterile, but Hall's PALEA accounts differ. Confirm actual flowering material; existing Australian photo doesn't establish Texas variety or range |

### New visual state safeguards

- *Panicum virgatum* supports both `membrane` and `hairs` **as field appearances**, while botany retains that true ciliate membrane plus separate upper-blade hair triangle. Old `membrane`-only state was too restrictive for worn collars.
- *Dichanthelium commutatum* supports `hairs`, `membrane`, and `absent` for observer interpretation because published sources describe minute ciliate membrane, near-invisible fringes, or absent/worn structure. True anatomical absence is NOT established solely by lack of visible tissue.
- *Panicum hallii* lower sterile floret/palea source discrepancy retained rather than building a false exclusive filter.
- *Dichanthelium pedicellatum* corm-based/no winter rosette rule is species- and regional-description-specific. The key must NOT require winter rosettes genus-wide.
- Introduced kleingrass/witchgrass remain in ID key for comparison, not restoration planting recommendations. Hays County nativity still governed by **BONAP NAPA** independently.
