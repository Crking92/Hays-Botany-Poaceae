## 1. Catapodium rigidum (L.) C.E. Hubb. ex Dony — p.109
Historical citation: Fl. Bedfordsh. 437 (1953). Historical synonym **Sclerochloa rigida (L.) Griseb.**, Fl. 52. Low, tufted annual with glabrous herbage and narrow contracted panicles or spicate racemes.
- Culms 5–15(–30) cm tall, branching below middle.
- Sheaths rounded on back, thin membranous margins above continuing upward as lateral portions of ligule.
- Ligule a lacerate membrane 1.5–4 mm long.
- Blades flat, thin and soft, 1–3(–4) mm broad, 2–8(–12) cm long.
- Inflorescence typically 3–9 cm long and 5–15 mm thick, occasionally larger, with 5–9-flowered spikelets on short, stout pedicels.
- Disarticulation above glumes and between florets.
- Spikelets narrow, awnless, mostly 5–7 mm long.
- Glumes unequal, stiff, acute; first 1-nerved, about 1 mm; second 3-nerved, about 2 mm.
- Florets widely spaced on a zigzag rachilla; rachilla scabrous or rugose.
- Lemmas rounded on back, minutely rugose, obscurely 5-nerved, broadly acute and with hyaline margins at apex.
- Paleas about as long as lemmas, scabrous on nerves.
- Chromosome number 2n=14.
- Distribution per Gould: Texas regions 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7; growing along roadsides, field borders, ditches and other disturbed sites. General: adventive from Europe, now occasional at widely scattered US localities.
- Flowering March–May.
