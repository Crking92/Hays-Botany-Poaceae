## 97. DICHANTHELIUM (Hitchc. & Chase) Gould — genus p.477
Tufted perennials, mostly with tuft/rosette of short broad-bladed basal leaves developing during cool season. Culms stiffly erect, mostly 20–70 cm, arising singly. Sheaths rounded on back, those of culm leaves typically shorter than culm internodes. Ligule ciliate ring of hairs, short ciliate or glabrous membranous rim. Blades of vernal (spring) shoots typically flat and broad, often narrowly acute, involute in few species. Late-season plants of most species branching at upper nodes; some become bushy-branched and have fascicles of short narrow leaves. Panicles of primary shoots typically open, usually well exserted on long peduncles. Inflorescences of secondary branches often much reduced, occasionally only 2–6 spikelets; partly hidden in fascicled leaves. Spikelets long/short-pedicelled, ovate/elliptic/obovate, awnless, rounded or somewhat pointed at apex, 1.4–4 mm long, usually puberulent but glabrous in a few taxa. Disarticulation below glumes. First glume typically thin, broad, short; second glume and lemma of lower floret about equal in length or longer. Lower floret neuter, palea present or absent, often half or more as long as lemma. Upper floret with shiny glabrous hard or coriaceous lemma and palea, lemma tightly inrolled over margins of palea.

## Genus history / seasonal biology p.477–478
Gould notes Hitchcock & Chase (1910) treated *Dichanthelium* as a section of *Panicum*, with the disposition followed in North American treatments for decades. Gould's concept recognizes related but distinguishable taxa. Cites Smith (1972), Smith & Brown (1973) for physiological/genetic comparison: *Dichanthelium* tends to lower panicoid polyploidy, with base numbers predominantly 9 (2n=18) and some tetraploids 2n=36, though other counts reported. Most species flower early spring and again summer/autumn. Second growing season inflorescences are often hidden and cleistogamous; most seed apparently produced in such late-season cleistogamous spikelets. Gould reports roughly 100 species in American genus, fewer in South America; his then-current Texas treatment recognized 28 taxa after reduction from earlier counts. These are HISTORICAL taxonomic scope and not evidence that 28 currently accepted species correspond to the current learner inventory.

## 1. Dichanthelium dichotomum (L.) Gould — p.481 account begins
Historical synonym *Panicum dichotomum* L. Gould citation: Brittonia 19:363 (1967). Culms slender, 20–50(–100) cm tall, usually tufted from compact short knotty crown; glabrous or nodes bearded with spreading hairs, sometimes much branched and bushy with short reduced fascicled leafy branches in age (autumn phase). Leaves basal rosette and lower cauline leaves broad and short, usually broader than above. Sheaths glabrous, rarely sparsely pilose; blades usually confined to margins (source phrase needs checking for precise hair localization). Ligule glabrous rim or fringe hairs 0.2–1 mm. Blades of main culms 4–12(–15) cm long, 3–10(–13) mm broad, thin, glabrous on both surfaces or less frequently thinly pilose, often sparsely ciliate margins near base, tapering to attenuate tip. Leaves of fascicled autumnal branches narrow, flat or involute blades commonly 3–7 cm long. Panicles 3–8(–10) cm, well exserted, open, branches/pedicels spreading; spikelets not appearing secund on branchlets. Late-formed panicles greatly reduced on short fascicled branches. Spikelets oblong/obovate usually 1.5–2.4 mm long, glabrous or less frequently puberulent. Second glume and lower-floret lemma strongly 7–9-nerved. Chromosome number 2n=18.
Gould historical distribution Texas regions 1,2,3,4,5 in moist sandy soils, usually along woodlands; general Maine/Michigan to Florida/Texas and western Indies. Flowering April–June and again late summer/autumn.
