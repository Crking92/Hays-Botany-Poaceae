## Bothriochloa Kuntze — genus pp.591–592
Perennials and annuals; native Texas species cespitose perennials with stiffly erect culms. Ligules membranous. Blades long, narrow, flat. Inflorescence a panicle, spikelets in pairs on few to numerous primary branches, these rebranched in a few species. Disarticulation at base of spikelets, rachis joint and pedicel falling with awned sessile spikelet. Pedicels and upper rachis internodes with central groove or broad membranous area. Sessile spikelets perfect, usually awned, more or less triangular in outline; first glume dorsally flattened, second with rounded median keel. Lemma of upper floret reduced to thin hyaline body, stout midnerve extended as geniculate twisted awn. Pedicelled spikelets well developed, neuter or staminate and awnless. Historical estimate about 30 species across warmer regions of continents.

## Hierarchical key pp.591–592 (do not flatten conditional statements)
- Pedicelled spikelets neuter, narrower and smaller than sessile: sessile spikelets <4.5 mm; awn of lemma <=18 mm; first glume of sessile spikelet never with a glandular pit. Awns 8–18 mm => *B. saccharoides*; awns 0–6 mm => *B. exaristata*.
- Same initial neuter/smaller branch: sessile spikelets 4.5–7.3 mm; awn 20–33 mm. Panicle axis <5 cm, branches mostly 2–7 rarely >8: culm nodes glabrous or puberulent and first glume of ALL sessile spikelets pitted. Upper nodes glabrous, primary branches never rebranched, sessile glume 5.5–7 mm, glabrous on back, relatively large deep glandular pit, leaves mostly basal, culm leaves reduced, 2 mm blade width => *B. edwardsiana*. Upper nodes glabrous or puberulent, lower 1–2 branches frequently rebranched, sessile glume 4.5–5.7 mm, usually sparse hispid back near base, relatively small shallow glandular pit, leaves developed, 2–5 mm blade width => *B. hybrida*.
- p.592: nodes pubescent with long spreading silky white hairs, first glume of some/all sessile spikelets pitted => *B. springfieldii*.
- p.592: panicle axis usually >=5–20 cm, branches mostly >=9–30: larger culm panicles 14–25 cm, stout stiffly spreading, 1.2–2.5 m culms, bluish-glaucous below nodes, nodes bearded with 3–6 mm spreading hairs, axis/branches remaining kinked when compressed, pollen average 39–40 µm => *B. alta*. - Pedicelled spikelets neuter or staminate, about as large and broad as sessile: panicle axis usually longer than branches => *B. bladhii*. Panicle axis shorter than branches: first glume sessile spikelet never with large glandular pit => *B. ischaemum*; with large glandular pit => *B. pertusa*.

## Bothriochloa saccharoides (Swartz) Rydb. sensu Gould — pp.592–593
Historical synonym *Andropogon saccharoides* Swartz; common name silver bluestem.
Cespitose perennial, somewhat geniculate below, never developing rhizomes or stolons (explicit species-level Gould description). Herbage glaucous or green, usually maturing bronze or reddish. Leaves glabrous except a few long hairs in vicinity of ligule. Ligule an erose hyaline membrane 1–3 mm or more long. Blades linear, firm, flat or irregularly folded, mostly 3–6(–8) mm broad, tapering to long attenuate tip. Inflorescence densely-flowered contracted panicle, branches erect or loosely erect-spreading with age. Panicle branches numerous, shorter than main axis; branches and pedicels with median groove, fringed on margins with long silky hairs. Pedicelled spikelet neuter, much narrower and usually shorter than sessile one. Sessile spikelet glumes: first larger than second and clasping it along margins below apex; first glume flat dorsally, rounded or loosely keeled on margins; second broadly keeled. Awn of lower floret mostly 8–18 mm, geniculate below middle with both segments loosely twisted (as stated in supplied p.593; preserve original wording and organ context).
Two varieties treated as follows.

### 1A var. torreyana (Steud.) Gould — p.593, Fig.312 p.594
Gould citation: Southw. Naturalist 3:212 (1959). SILVER BLUESTEM. Fig.312. Plants conspicuous basal cluster of leaves. Culms mostly 50–80 cm tall. Herbage/spikelets commonly glaucous. Chromosome number 2n=60. Panicles 6–10(–13) cm long, glumes ovate relatively broad/blunt, dull green, commonly glaucous with whitish waxy bloom; pollen averaging 32–38 microns. Gould distribution all Texas regions, less common in eastern/coastal than var. *longipaniculata*, northern and western Texas frequent; Texas-to-northern-Mexico and SW US general statement, alkaline sites, railroad/road embankments, dry ditch and gully banks. Flowering mostly May–November.

### 1B var. longipaniculata (Gould) Gould — p.593, Fig.313 p.595
Gould citation: Southw. Naturalist 3:212 (1959). Synonym *Andropogon saccharoides* var. *longipaniculatus* Gould. LONGSPIKE SILVER BLUESTEM. Fig.313. Plants usually without conspicuous basal cluster of leaves. Culms commonly 60–130 cm tall. Herbage and spikelets usually not glaucous. Chromosome number 2n=120. Panicles of larger culms mostly 10–20 cm; glumes narrowly ovate, shiny green; pollen averaging 39–42 microns. Gould distribution Texas regions 1,2,3,4,6, mostly woods openings, well adapted clayey soils, quite shade-tolerant; southeastern/southern Texas to northeastern Mexico. Flowering mostly May–November.

### Hybridization caution, p.593
Gould notes longspike silver bluestem appears to have arisen by hybridization of *B. saccharoides* var. *torreyana* and *B. exaristata* and doubling the subsequent chromosome number; variety appears genetically stable and homogeneous. This is Gould's historical hypothesis, NOT a confirmed modern phylogenetic conclusion.
