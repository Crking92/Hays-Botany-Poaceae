Intake 2026-11-23. Primary evidence: owner photographs of Frank W. Gould, *The Grasses of Texas* (1975), printed pp. 82–85. **Direct source archival only**: taxonomy and scoring have not yet been reconciled with the 169-taxon Grass Lab; no photos, BONAP or filters edited. The Bromus genus key continues from p.83 onto p.84, and a following species treatment is not in these pages. Preserve historical spellings and original measures and qualifiers. Printed page 85 has Fig.37 depicting *Bromus unioloides* plant, spikelet, floret, cited to Gould and Box 1965 under *B. catharticus*; do not use figure as independently licensed photo.

## Tribe 9. Poeae; 19. BROMUS L. — p.82
Annuals and perennials, culms solitary or in clumps of few to numerous. Leaves with rounded sheaths, united by margins to well above middle. Ligule membranous, usually prominent. Blades thin and flat, often broad. Inflorescence a panicle, infrequently a raceme, spikelets all pedicelled, typically 13–45 mm or more long with 4–numerous florets. Disarticulation above glumes and between florets. Glumes unequal, usually acute and awnless, 1–5-nerved. Lemmas 5–13-nerved, 1-awned from notch of bifid apex or infrequently awnless. Paleas large, adnate to caryopses; caryopses with tuft of hairs at apex. Gould notes about 100 species mostly temperate/cool world; Texas bromes flowering mostly January–June at lower elevations and into summer at high elevations (historical broader statement).

## Identification key — pp.82–84 (hierarchical; do not flatten as universal traits)
Page 82:
- Glumes and lemmas sharply keeled, spikelets strongly laterally compressed: perennial, lemma awns 4–7 mm => 1. *B. polyanthus*. Annual, lemma awns 4–15 mm => 2. *B. arizonicus*. Annual, lemma awnless or awns 1–3 mm => 3. *B. unioloides*.
- Glumes and lemmas not keeled, spikelets not strongly laterally compressed: perennials culm bases firm. Glumes glabrous/scabrous/minutely puberulous, hairs on back as well as margins of lemmas (sometimes absent from upper half): lower sheaths glabrous or more or less villous; anthers ~2.5 mm; blades mostly 2–5 mm; plants not robust => 6. *B. anomalus*. Lower sheaths lanate; anthers 3.6–3.9 mm; blades mostly 5–10 mm; robust => 7. *B. lanatipes*. Lemma hairs restricted to margins or margins/base => 8. *B. richardsonii*.

Page 83 continuation:
- Glumes sparsely to densely hairy: awns 5–11 mm, blades mostly 5–10 mm broad, ligules 0.5–3 mm => 5. *B. pubescens*. Awn 1–4 (rarely –5) mm, blades mostly 3.5–5 mm, ligules ≤1 mm => 6. *B. anomalus* (appears in two key paths; preserve both).
- Annuals, short-lived, culms with soft bases: awns mostly 3–6 cm, second glume 2–3.5 cm => 9. *B. diandrus*. Awns ≤2.5 cm, second glume ≤1.5 cm: first glume 1- or sometimes 3-nerved and gradually tapering to slender, usually acuminate hyaline tip. Awns ≤10 mm and teeth of lemma apex ~1 mm => 4. *B. tectorum*. Awns 11–25 mm and teeth of lemma apex mostly 2–5 mm: panicle branches/pedicels short stout erect or stiffly spreading, mostly <2 cm => 10. *B. rubens*; panicle branches slender flexuous/drooping, often >2 cm, pedicels capillary often S-curved => 11. *B. tectorum* [**source key print appears 'B. tectorum' here as well; verify original printing/alternate taxon concept before any scoring**].
- First glume 3–7-nerved with obtuse, acute or acuminate tip: teeth of lemma apex mostly 3–5 mm; lemmas densely long-pubescent; awns mostly 1.5–2.5 cm => 13. *B. macrostachys*. Teeth <3 mm, lemmas pubescent or glabrous, awns <1.5 cm; glumes/lemmas pubescent => 12. *B. mollis*. Glumes/lemmas glabrous, paleas 1–2 mm shorter than lemmas, mature lemma margins not inrolled, basal sheaths retrorsely pilose with long soft matted hairs, some plants sparse/erect => 14. *B. japonicus*.
Page 84 top continuation of final fork: paleas slightly shorter to slightly longer than lemmas; mature lemma margins inrolled; basal sheaths glabrous or inconspicuously hirsute => 15. *B. secalinus*.

**Potential source-reading ambiguity**: The printed p.83 terminal after S-curved pedicels appears as 11. *B. tectorum*, while p.83 earlier 4. *B. tectorum*; this needs magnified original-page review and should remain unresolved, rather than quietly substituting another species. All key names and numbers are historical, not automatic modern accepted taxonomy.

## 1. Bromus polyanthus Scribn. — p.84
*Bromus polyanthus* Scribn., in Shear, U.S.D.A. Div. Agrost. Bull. 23:56 (1900). POLYANTHUS BROME. Perennial; culms mostly 80–100 cm tall. Sheaths and blades glabrous or latter scabrous. Panicles 15–25 cm long, usually open. Spikelets mostly 2.5–4 cm long and with 6–9 florets. Glumes and lemmas laterally compressed and sharply keeled; glumes glabrous or scabrous, first glume 3-nerved, second 5–7-nerved. Lemmas glabrous/scabrous or puberulent on margins, awn 4–7 mm. Chromosome number 2n=56. Gould historical Texas range occasional open grasslands region 9 and mountain slopes region 10; general Montana–Washington, south Texas/California/northern Mexico. Flowering spring and summer.

## 2. Bromus arizonicus (Shear) Stebbins — p.84
*Bromus arizonicus* (Shear) Stebbins, Proc. Calif. Acad. Sci. IV 25:309 (1944). ARIZONA BROME. Tufted annual, culms mostly 30–70 cm tall. Panicles short, stiffly erect, relatively narrow. Spikelets mostly 2.5–3 cm long and with 5–8 florets; glumes/lemmas laterally compressed sharply keeled. Glumes glabrous or minutely hirsute, first glume usually 3-nerved, second usually 5-nerved. Lemmas glabrous or more commonly sparsely hirsute, awn 7–15 mm, apical teeth 0.7–2 mm. Chromosome 2n=84. Historical Texas regions 10, infrequent on mountain slopes at medium altitudes; general western Texas, Arizona, southern California, southern Pacific mountain ranges into Baja California. Flowering spring.

## 3. Bromus unioloides (Willd.) H.B.K. — pp.84–85, Fig.37 p.85
*Bromus unioloides* (Willd.) H.B.K., Nov. Gen. Sp. 1:151 (1815). RESCUEGRASS. Tufted annual (in Texas), culms mostly 50–80 cm tall. Young shoots soft/succulent, laterally flattened, usually somewhat keeled. Sheaths nearly glabrous to densely puberulent with fine straight spreading hairs. Ligules large, well developed. Blades mostly 5–12 mm broad, glabrous or hirsute with spreading hairs, with yellowish band just above ligule. Inflorescence open, drooping panicle of large, flattened spikelets; spikelets usually 6–12-flowered. Glumes awnless; first usually 5–7-nerved, second usually 7–9-nerved. Lemmas 7–11-nerved, laterally flattened and sharply keeled, glabrous or variously pubescent, awnless or with awn 1–3 mm. Chromosome number 2n=28.
Continuation p.85: Gould Texas distribution all regions; general native to South America, frequent most southern U.S. states and reported as far north as NY, SD, Oregon. Flowering early spring to early summer. Introduced forage species, common weed of roadsides, field borders, vacant lots and other disturbed sites. Gould notes historic usage *Bromus catharticus* Vahl by Hitchcock (1935), Chase (1951), Gould & Box (1965), and *B. unioloides* by Raven (1960), Gould (1962); retain full historical synonym/interpretation notes until present taxonomy is verified.
Figure 37 on p.85: plant, spikelet and floret caption *B. unioloides*, sourced to Gould and Box (1965) as *B. catharticus*.
