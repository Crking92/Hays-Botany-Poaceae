## Research-grounded species proof characters

### Aegilops cylindrica — accepted name *Aegilops cylindrica*

**Existing photos show:** Jointed, slender, cylindrical spike and detached spike sections visible in Hays photos.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Spikelets tightly fitted in niches along unbranched 2-sided flattened axis; sessile spikelets usually 2–5-flowered; upper spikelet lemma/glume awns about 3–8 cm; ligule <1 mm.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** An intact mature spike plus separated one-node/one-spikelet segment, with ruler, and a ligule/collar photo.

**Compare:** Hordeum spp., Triticum aestivum and other goatgrasses. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF.

### Tridentopsis elongata — accepted name *Tridentopsis elongata*

**Existing photos show:** Excellent grass habit, narrow panicle, several close spikelets and partial collar views.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Regional Flora's older Tridens muticus var. elongatus concept: 2nd glume usually 6–8 mm long and 3–7-nerved; blades usually 3–4 mm wide, versus var. muticus's usually 1-nerved glume ~5 mm or less and blades 1–2 mm wide.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** One flattened second glume with nervation illuminated, at least two individual spikelets and a 1-mm scale; measure blade width.

**Compare:** Existing Tridentopsis mutica, Tridentopsis buckleyana. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF. [Open source](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A77313940-1).

### Echinochloa walteri — accepted name *Echinochloa walteri*

**Existing photos show:** Awned panicles, wetland setting and strongly hairy leaf sheaths have excellent supporting Hays views.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Sheaths usually with papilla-based hirsute/hispid hairs; spikelets about 3–5 mm without awn; lower floret lemma awns commonly 1.5–6 cm, but occasionally shortened or absent.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Sheath-hair bases in sharp focus next to ruler, plus two mature spikelets measuring unawned body and lemma awn.

**Compare:** Echinochloa crus-galli, Echinochloa muricata, other barnyardgrasses. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF.

### Sphenopholis obtusata — accepted name *Sphenopholis obtusata*

**Existing photos show:** Dense to elongate spike-like panicles and sheath/collar views from Hays.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Second glume much broader/obovate and blunt-tipped; spikelets shiny and 2–3-flowered, about 1.5–5 mm long; membranous ligule 1.5–3 mm.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Isolated spikelet with broad upper glume face, plus whole floret count and true membranous ligule.

**Compare:** Sphenopholis interrupta, Koeleria spp.. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF. [Open source](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A242045-2/general-information).

### Coelorachis cylindrica — accepted name *Rottboellia campestris*

**Existing photos show:** Thin cylindrical jointed flowering axes very well shown in multiple Hays observations.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Regional Flora Coelorachis cylindrica concept: inflorescence about 3 mm thick, axis jointed with niches where spikelets fit, paired sessile fertile spikelet (pitted glume) and sterile pedicelled spikelet, ligule 0.5–1 mm.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Close view of one node showing the spikelet in niche and glume pit, with 1-mm ruler, plus a section disarticulating at the joint.

**Compare:** Rottboellia/Coelorachis jointed inflorescence types. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF. [Open source](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/62724-2).

### Paspalum langei — accepted name *Paspalum langei*

**Existing photos show:** Several two-ranked racemes and broad leaves supported by multiple Hays images.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Panicles with 2–5 branches usually 4–8 cm; spikelets around 2.2–2.6 mm; first glume present on some or all; second glume and sterile lemma pubescent; leaf blades 7–18 mm wide.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Detached mature spikelets showing both glume faces and hair details with a millimeter scale.

**Compare:** Paspalum pubiflorum, Paspalum distichum and other branched Paspalum. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF.

### Tridens congestus — accepted name *Tridens congestus*

**Existing photos show:** Short, compact/pink-tinged panicles with clear field habit.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Panicles 5–10 cm long; glumes and lemmas thin, papery and often pink-tinged; lemma apex deeply cleft.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Dissect mature lemma and photograph its deeply cleft apex, glumes and 1-mm ruler.

**Compare:** Tridens flavus, Tridens muticus and other tridens. **Reference:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas (1999), user-provided Poaceae PDF.

### Pappophorum bicolor — accepted name *Pappophorum bicolor*

**Existing photos show:** Feathery seedheads against dry rocky Hays grassland, with close ripe florets in one observation.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Typically pink panicles; fertile lemma body about 3–4 mm and apex bearing approximately 11–15 unequal awns; 2–3 bisexual florets plus sterile upper florets.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Detach one mature lemma and count 11–15 unequal awns, show lemma body and 1-mm scale.

**Compare:** Other Pappophorum and feathery-awned grama grasses. **Reference:** Flora of North America Pappophorum bicolor account (Reeder), crosschecked with Kew/GrassBase and Texas A&M. [Open source](https://herbanwmex.net/portal/taxa/index.php?taxon=309).

### Janochloa antidotalis — accepted name *Janochloa antidotalis*

**Existing photos show:** One unusually complete eight-photo Hays series: panicle, spikelets, nodes, culm and collar.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Rhizomatous perennial with swollen nodes and woody culms; panicles 10–45 cm, spikelets 2.4–3.4 mm; shorter lower glumes, usually 3–5-veined; lower floret staminate, upper fertile.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Clear rhizome/crown photo and detached mature spikelets with lower versus upper glumes and fertile floret under scale.

**Compare:** Panicum hallii, Panicum bergii and other blue panic look-alikes. **Reference:** Flora of North America (under Panicum antidotale), and local 1999 Flora Panicum antidotale entry. [Open source](https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?taxon=239881).

### Stapfochloa canterae — accepted name *Stapfochloa canterae*

**Existing photos show:** Macro views of ciliate raceme axis, seedhead and scale-backed split spikelets, good local evidence.

**Authoritative diagnostic concept:** Differentiation from S. ciliata requires precise glume, fertile lemma, sterile floret and awn characters; gross fingerlike inflorescence alone is insufficient. Existing S. ciliata source key has 1 fertile and 2 small sterile florets and strongly ciliate fertile lemma.

**One photo set to obtain/inspect next:** Compare matched spikelet dissections of verified canterae and ciliata at identical scales, with floret count and lemma hair distribution.

**Compare:** Stapfochloa ciliata; related Chloris spp.. **Reference:** Kew POWO/GrassBase Stapfochloa ciliata comparator; do not transfer ciliata dimensions onto canterae. [Open source](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A60469005-2/general-information).


## Field QA rules

1. Prefer existing original Hays specimen observation links in the prior review JSON, not random web images of the same species.
2. For each prioritized Hays specimen, check that all required characteristics occur on the **same plant or same collected specimen**; avoid assembling a synthetic specimen from look-alike observations.
3. Record whether observation is clearly wild/established versus ornamental, pasture sowing, crop, turf, seed-spill or uncertain. iNaturalist wild-tag alone does not settle this.
4. Any proposed public site photo must pass a separate exact-image rights/attribution check; the review phase has not licensed media for publication.
5. Only after the diagnostic trait is visibly measured, the name is accepted, and Hays occurrence is credible should another chat prepare small tested card merges.
