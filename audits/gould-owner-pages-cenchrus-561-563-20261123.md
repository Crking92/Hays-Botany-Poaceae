## 106. CENCHRUS L. — pp.561–562
Gould: annuals and perennials, mostly with weak, geniculate culms. Ligule a short membrane densely ciliate with short or long hairs. Spikelets enclosed in burs, 1–3 per bur. Burs formed by bristles and/or flattened spines (modified branchlets) fused together at least at base; bristles/spines usually retrorsely barbed. Burs subsessile on short, angular, unbranched rachis; disarticulation at base of bur. Glumes thin, membranous, unequal. Lemma of lower floret often thin, equaling or exceeding second glume; palea of lower floret about equaling lemma. Lemma of upper floret thin, membranous, tapering to slender usually acuminate tip, margins not inrolled. Caryopses elliptic to ovoid, dorsally flattened. Gould cites DeLisle (1963) as recognizing 20 species, mostly in Americas, a few throughout warmer world (historical scope).

## Key pp.562–563 (conditional)
Burs lacking stiff flattened basal spines, plants perennial:
- Bristles conspicuously ciliate-pubescent => 1. *Cenchrus ciliaris*.
- Bristles retrorsely scabrous => 2. *Cenchrus myosuroides*.
Burs with stiff flattened basal spines, plants annual or weakly perennial:
- Burs with one whorl of united flattened spines, surrounded by 1–several whorls of bristles => 3. *Cenchrus echinatus*.
- Burs with >1 whorl of flattened spines, spines projecting at irregular intervals throughout bur: mostly 8–40 spines; larger ones frequently 1.5 mm broad at base; upper floret 3.4–5.8 mm long => 4. *Cenchrus incertus*.
- Burs mostly with 45–75 spines; larger ones seldom >1 mm broad; upper floret 5.8–7.6 mm long => 5. *Cenchrus longispinus*.
This is a 1975 key, not a proof of current names or universal filter assignments.

## 1. Cenchrus ciliaris L. — p.563
* Cenchrus ciliaris* L., Mant. 302 (1771). Historical synonym *Pennisetum ciliare* (L.) Link. Common name BUFFELGRASS. Fig.294 (not supplied in this batch).
- Perennial; culms 50–100 cm tall, erect or geniculate-spreading, branched and knotty at base.
- Sheaths laterally compressed and keeled; glabrous to sparsely pilose.
- Ligule short ciliate membrane, hairs mostly 1–1.5 mm long.
- Blades thin, usually flat, scabrous or slightly pilose, mostly 8–30 cm long, 2.5–8 mm broad.
- Inflorescence dense, cylindrical, mostly 4–10(–13) cm long and 1–2 cm thick.
- Bristles 4–10 mm long, purplish, long-ciliate on inner margins; terete, connate only at base or slightly above.
- Fascicles with 2–4 spikelets, on minute pilose peduncle. **Important distinction:** genus introduction says generally 1–3 spikelets/bur; exact species states 2–4 spikelets per fascicle. Preserve rather than force into uniform genus rule.
- Spikelets 2.2–5.6 mm; upper-floret lemma 2–2.5 mm. Caryopses turgid, ovoid, 1.4–1.9 mm long, about 1 mm diameter.
- Chromosome numbers listed: 2n=32, 34, 35, 36, 40, 44, 52 and 54. Preserve each as Gould-listed, not harmonized.
- Gould reports introduced as forage grass; common in sandy rangeland and semi-disturbed sites throughout region 6 and occasionally regions 2,3,7,10. General native India/Africa, widely introduced elsewhere. This is Gould's historical geography, NOT current county occurrence or nativity determination.
- Flowering early spring to late autumn under favorable growing conditions.

## 2. Cenchrus myosuroides H.B.K. — p.563, PARTIAL
Historical citation *Cenchrus myosuroides* H.B.K., Nov. Gen. et Sp. Pl. 1:155, t.35 (1815). BIG SANDBUR. Fig.295 (not supplied).
- Coarse perennial with stout culms in large clumps. Culms more or less woody, mostly 0.7–2 m tall, slightly swollen nodes; little-branched above base.
- Ligule ciliate membrane, hairs 1–3 mm.
- Blades scabrous at least on adaxial surface, sometimes slightly pilose; mostly 12–40 cm long, 4–13 mm broad, flat or somewhat folded.
- Inflorescence mostly 8–20 cm long, 6–12 mm thick; spikelets usually one per bur.
- Spines and bristles of bur not flattened, retrorsely scabrous or barbellate; inner ones about as long as spikelet, outer ones shorter, irregular in length. Bristles fused to form a hard **[continuation cut off on supplied p.563 — check p.564]**.
