## 53. Erioneuron Nash — genus p.215
Low, tufted perennials, some stoloniferous, with narrow, often involute, cartilaginous-margined leaf blades. Ligule short, ciliate membrane. Inflorescence a short, often capitate raceme or panicle. Spikelets large, several-flowered, disarticulating above glumes and between florets. Glumes large, subequal, membranous, glabrous and shiny, 1-nerved. Lemmas broad, rounded on back, 3-nerved, conspicuously long-hairy along nerves or at least below, bilobed at apex (exception noted for *E. pilosum*); midnerve short-awned, lateral nerves often prolonged as short mucro. Paleas slightly shorter than lemmas, ciliate or puberulent on keels, long-hairy below between nerves. Caryopses oblong, glossy and translucent, with embryo more than half as long as caryopsis.

p.217: Gould historically describes five species distributed in low-rainfall areas of southwestern United States and Mexico. This is historical genus geography, NOT BONAP / county nativity.

## Hierarchical key p.217 — three species
- Spikelets in leafy fascicles, not or scarcely surpassing pungent spine-tipped leaf blades; plants often developing slender wiry stolons → **1. *Erioneuron pulchellum***.
- Spikelets on elongated, leafless floral axis; plants not or rarely stoloniferous:
  - Lemma tip acute or notch <=0.5 mm deep → **2. *Erioneuron pilosum***.
  - Lemma tip with notch 1–2.5 mm deep → **3. *Erioneuron avenaceum***.
These are key statements contingent on earlier couplets, not automatic blanket species exclusions. The treatment of *E. avenaceum* is NOT on these pages.

## 1. Erioneuron pulchellum (H.B.K.) Tateoka — p.217
Printed citation: Amer. J. Bot. 48:572 (1961); historical synonym *Tridens pulchellus* (H.B.K.) Hitchc.; common name **FLUFFGRASS**, Fig.112 (not supplied).
- Tufted perennial with or without slender stolons, often appearing annual.
- Culms numerous in tuft, 2–15 cm tall, scabrous below nodes.
- Leaves fascicled at culm nodes; short, short sheaths and short tightly involute acute/cuspidate blades 1 mm or less broad.
- Sheaths often with a tuft of long hairs at base. Ligule ciliate membrane approximately 0.5 mm long.
- Spikelets sessile or short-pedicelled in capitate clusters on short racemes, these exceeded in length by leaves of fascicle.
- Spikelets mostly 7–13 mm long with 6–12 florets.
- Glumes subequal, acuminate or short-awned, scarious, about as long as spikelet.
- Lemmas mostly 3–5 mm, long densely ciliate on nerves, deeply cleft to just above middle, stout straight/curved awn slightly shorter to slightly longer than apical lobes.
- Paleas broad, pubescent on margins and between nerves, puberulent on nerves.
- Chromosome number **2n=16**.
- Gould historical distribution Texas regions **8, 9, 10 and western part of 7**, dry rocky slopes and desert flats. General Utah/Nevada to Texas/Arizona/northern Mexico.
- Flowering **June–November**.

## 2. Erioneuron pilosum (Buckl.) Nash — p.217, PARTIAL
Printed citation: Small, Fl. Southeast. U.S. 144 (1903). Historical synonym *Tridens pilosus* (Vasey) Hitchc.; common name **HAIRY TRIDENS**, Fig.113 (not supplied).
- Tufted perennial, without creeping or long slender stolons. Culms mostly 10–30 cm tall, typically with only one node elevated above basal clusters of leaves.
- Lower sheaths laterally compressed and keeled.
- Ligule a fringe of hairs about 0.5 mm long.
- Blades thick, narrow, flat, glabrous, 2.5–11 cm long and 1–2 mm broad, with thick white margin and abruptly pointed but not spinescent tip.
- Inflorescence a contracted panicle or raceme mostly 2–3(–4) cm long and 1.5–2 cm broad, with usually 4–9(–12) large, pale spikelets.
- Spikelets mostly 10–16 mm long with 7–18 closely imbricated florets.
- Glumes acuminate, approx. 4.5–6 [**visible sentence continues onto p.218; units and further characters not supplied**].
- Historical key p.217: lemma tip acute or notch 0.5 mm or less deep, contrasted with *E. avenaceum*.
