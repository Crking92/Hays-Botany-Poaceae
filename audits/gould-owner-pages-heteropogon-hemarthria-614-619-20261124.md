## 118. Heteropogon Pers. — p.614
Gould describes genus as about eight species in warmer parts of both hemispheres (historical estimate). A full formal genus morphology diagnosis is not visible on these supplied pages.

### 1. Heteropogon contortus (L.) Beauv. ex Roem. & Schult. — pp.614, 617–618 (p.615 MISSING)
Historical reference Syst. Veg. 2:836 (1817), common name **TANGLEHEAD**, Fig.327 p.617.
- p.614: cespitose perennial, culms mostly 20–80 cm tall, much branched at base, leafy branching at upper nodes, glabrous. Leaves glabrous except few long hairs on sheath and blade margins near ligule; sheaths laterally compressed and sharply keeled.
- **p.615 is not present, and p.618 begins mid-description.** Therefore do not mark this as a complete botanical treatment.
- p.618 visible text: ligule short, fringed membrane. Blades flat, mostly 4–6 (rarely –10) mm broad; full length statement partly obscured by overlay. Racemes mostly 4–7 cm long excluding awns. Inflorescence includes paired sessile and pedicelled spikelets; lower spikelet pairs staminate or neuter and awnless, remainder of sessile spikelets perfect, with sharply pointed bearded callus and dark geniculate twisted awn.
- Staminate/neuter spikelets 7–10 mm long, broad thin green many-nerved glumes glabrous/sparsely hispid. Perfect spikelets 5–8 mm, narrow rounded dark-colored several-nerved brownish-hispid glumes. Awn of lemma of perfect floret commonly **5–12 cm** long, hispid, weakly twice-geniculate.
- Gould reports chromosome number mostly 2n=60; separately cites euploid counts 2n=20, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80.
- Historical Texas distribution regions 2,6,7,10; often Gulf Coast grasslands or western TX mountains, usually sandy soils. General warmer tropics/subtropics, southern Texas/New Mexico/Arizona in USA. Flowering March–December, mostly June–November.
- Gould notes Correll and Johnston (1970) report **Heteropogon melanocarpus** (Ell.) Benth. in southeastern Texas but call it rare and probably not persistent. Differentiator: *H. melanocarpus* annual, row of large depressed glands on first glume of staminate spikelets. This is an **adjacent historical occurrence/comparison note, not a verified resident Hays County species**.
- Fig.327 p.617 depicts the tanglehead inflorescence and spikelet, from Gould (1951).

## 119. Hemarthria R. Br. — p.618
Gould historical statement: twelve species, native tropical/subtropical Old World, introduced into Americas.

### 1. Hemarthria altissima (Poir.) Stapf & C.E. Hubb. — pp.618–619
Historical synonyms printed include **Manisuris altissima (Poir.) Hitchc.** and **Rottboellia altissima Poir.** (check exact author formatting before nomenclatural normalization). Common name **LIMPOGRASS**. Fig.328 on p.619.
- Coarse perennial, culms mostly 40–100 cm long, numerous nodes and short internodes.
- Sheaths thin, keeled or not keeled, glabrous/sparsely hairy at margins above.
- Ligule short, ciliate and lacerate membrane.
- Blades thin, flat or folded, 3–8 mm broad.
- Inflorescence glabrous spike-like raceme, **3–5(–10) cm** long, with thick, tough, dorsally compressed/elevate internodes.
- Racemes usually produced at all upper culm nodes, enclosed at base by subtending sheaths.
- Disarticulation at nodes of rachis; spikelets paired, one sessile and perfect, one pedicelled and staminate/neuter, both more or less sunken in corky rachis.
- Sessile spikelet elliptic-oblong, awnless, with triangular callus, not deeply sunken. Lower glume 4–7 mm, flattened on back, not pitted, without conspicuous marginal constriction, obtuse/truncate at tip; upper glume obtuse/acute. Pedicelled spikelets flattened, broadly linear, less robust than sessile spikelets.
- Chromosome number not reported.
- Gould distribution Texas regions 2,6,10, infrequent. General warm world, adventive USA. Flowering mostly June–October, occasionally December.
- Fig.328 p.619 shows inflorescence and paired spikelets; credited Gould and Box (1965), as *Manisuris altissima*.
