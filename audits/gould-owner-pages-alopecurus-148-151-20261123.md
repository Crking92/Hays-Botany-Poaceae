## Genus Alopecurus L. — p. 148
Annuals and perennials; flat blades and membranous ligules, margins of ligule continuing downward as sheath margins. Panicle dense, slender, cylindrical, spike-like. Spikelets one-flowered, disarticulating below glumes, falling entire. Glumes equal, awnless, laterally flattened, often dorsally keeled, 3-nerved, as long as spikelets, united along margins on lower one-fourth to one-half. Lemmas about as long as glumes, firm, obtuse, 5-nerved, midnerve extended from lemma body at or near base as an awn. Paleas usually absent. Caryopses laterally compressed, asymmetrically obovate. Gould estimates ~25 northern temperate species and 6 native to US; these are historical statements, not current county occurrence/nativity.

Genus key p. 148:
- *A. carolinianus*: spikelets 1.8–3 mm long; glumes hispid-ciliate on midnerve to apex; anthers 0.3–0.5 mm.
- *A. myosuroides*: spikelets 4–5.7 mm long; glumes glabrous, scabrous or minutely pubescent on midnerve near base; anthers 2–4 mm.

## Alopecurus carolinianus Walt. — pp. 150–151, Fig. 75 p. 151
Historical name: *Alopecurus carolinianus* Walt., Fl. Carol. 74. 1788. Common name: Carolina foxtail.
- Tufted annual; culms glabrous, mostly 10–50 cm.
- Sheaths glabrous, usually much shorter than culm internodes.
- Ligules mostly 3.5–7 mm.
- Blades glabrous or minutely scabrous, mostly 3–15 cm long and 1–4(–5) mm broad.
- Panicles 2–6 cm long, 4–6 mm thick.
- Glumes 1.8–3 mm long; hispid-ciliate on strongly projecting midnerves and margins; lemmas glabrous, obtuse at apex, with midnerve projecting from base or near base as awn 3–5 mm long.
- Anthers 0.3–0.5 mm.
- Caryopses 1–1.4 mm long with embryo about one-fourth length of endosperm.
- Chromosome number not reported.
- Gould distribution: Texas regions 1, 2, 3, 4, 5; moist, often-disturbed soils along ditches, streams, woods borders and pasturelands; general throughout US except extreme northeast.
- Flowering March–June.
- Figure 75 on p.151 depicts inflorescence and spikelet (caption from Gould 1968); **illustration is not independently photo-licensed**.

## Alopecurus myosuroides Huds. — p. 150
Historical name: *Alopecurus myosuroides* Huds., Fl. Angl. 23. 1762. Common name: mouse foxtail.
- Tufted annual, culms glabrous, mostly 20–70 cm.
- Sheaths smooth, shorter than culm internodes.
- Ligules 2–3(–5) mm.
- Blades 3–20(–30) cm long, 2–8 mm broad, scabrous on both surfaces or smooth on abaxial side.
- Panicles 3–14 cm long, 4–8 mm thick.
- Glumes as long as spikelets, with slightly winged dorsal keel; glabrous or scabrous on midnerve or midnerve sparsely short-pubescent near base.
- Lemmas about as long as glumes, awned from base, awn slightly geniculate and twisted, 5–8 mm long.
- Chromosome number 2n = 14.
- Gould distribution: Texas regions 1 and 2, infrequent, in moist soil. General native to Eurasia; adventive on eastern and western US coasts and occasionally elsewhere.
- Flowering March–June.
- Genus key p.148 supplies spikelets 4–5.7 mm and anthers 2–4 mm.
