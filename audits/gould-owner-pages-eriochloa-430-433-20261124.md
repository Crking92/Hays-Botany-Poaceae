## 95. Eriochloa H.B.K. — genus p.430
Cespitose annuals and perennials with thin, flat blades. Ligule minute membranous rim or ridge densely fringed with short soft or stiff hairs. Inflorescence contracted or somewhat open panicle, sessile or short-pedicelled spikelets borne singly or in pairs on erect or slightly spreading, unbranched primary branches. Disarticulation below spikelet. Spikelets 2-flowered; lower floret staminate or neuter, upper perfect. First glume reduced, fused with rachis node to form a cup or collar. Second glume and lower-floret lemma about equal, usually scabrous, hispid, or hirsute, acute or acuminate. Upper fertile-floret lemma indurate, glabrous, finely rugose, with slightly inrolled margins, abruptly apiculate or short-awned at apex. Gould historical estimate about 20 species in tropics/subtropics of world.

## Historical genus key p.430 (conditional; not unconditional species scoring)
- Pedicels glabrous/minutely puberulent; upper-floret lemma awn 0.5–1.5 mm:
  - Spikelets 4–5 mm; lemma awn 1–1.5 mm; perennial => 1. *E. punctata*.
  - Spikelets 3.5–4 mm; lemma awn mostly 0.5–0.8 mm; annual => 2. *E. contracta*.
- Pedicels with at least some hairs one-half as long to as long as spikelet; upper-floret lemma awnless or awn usually <0.5 mm:
  - Plants annual, culms weak, usually decumbent or geniculate below => 3. *E. lemmonii*.
  - Plants perennial, culms stiffly erect from a firm base => 4. *E. sericea*.

## 1. Eriochloa punctata (L.) Desv. ex Hamilton — pp.430–431
Historical citation Prodr. Pl. Ind. Occ. 5:1825 (1825), LOUISIANA CUPGRASS.
Cespitose perennial with erect/spreading-erect culms 60–100 cm tall, culm nodes minutely puberulent. Sheaths glabrous, rounded. Blades glabrous or scabrous, 4–10 mm broad. Inflorescence 8–18 cm long with usually 8–15 or more branches, these 2–6 cm. Branches/pedicels puberulent/pubescent. Spikelets 4–5 mm, glumes and lower-floret lemma sparsely appressed-pilose; glume often short-awned. Upper-floret lemma abruptly tipped with awn 1–1.5 mm. Chromosome 2n=36.
Gould Texas regions 2 and 6, moist swales/ditches and lower watercourses. General southeastern Louisiana to southern Texas, east/south in Mexico and Caribbean to South America. Flowering late March–November.

## 2. Eriochloa contracta Hitchc. — p.431; illustration p.432
Historical citation Proc. Biol. Soc. Wash. 41:163 (1928), PRAIRIE CUPGRASS, Fig.231.
Tufted annual, erect or geniculate-spreading culms mostly 30–70 cm; nodes puberulent. Sheaths/blades at least lower sheaths sometimes with minute spreading/reflexed hairs. Blades mostly 2–7 mm broad. Inflorescence 6–15 cm, usually 6–15 erect/appressed branches 1–2 cm, dense hirsute with short stiff hairs. Spikelets 3.5–4 mm; glume/lower lemma appressed-hispid, short awn-tipped; upper-floret lemma awn mostly 0.5–0.8 mm. Chromosome 2n=36.
Gould reported all Texas regions except 10; somewhat weedy in moist soils, along ditches/lowlands/stream courses, sometimes dry soils. General Missouri/Nebraska to Louisiana, Texas, Arizona, northern Mexico. Flowering April–November.
Fig.231 p.432: illustration of plant, spikelet and floret, from Gould and Box (1965); not a legally cleared grass gallery photograph.

## 3. Eriochloa lemmonii Vasey & Scribn. — p.431; varieties pp.431–433
Species distinction given in key: annual, weak often decumbent or geniculate lower culms; hairy pedicels and upper lemma awn usually <0.5 mm.
Gould's infraspecific key p.431:
- Blades typically pubescent, occasionally glabrous; glume usually <=4 mm, abruptly acuminate; branch hairs dense and often long, to 4 mm => 3A var. *lemmonii*.
- Blades typically glabrous, but pubescent in some populations; glume usually 4.5–6 mm, gradually tapering to acuminate/aristate apex; branch hairs typically short and not dense => 3B var. *gracilis*.

### 3A. E. lemmonii var. lemmonii — p.431
Historical synonym *Eriochloa gracilis* (Fourn.) Hitchc. var. *minor* (Vasey) Hitchc. in Gould's printed treatment; common name SMALL SOUTHWESTERN CUPGRASS.
Tufted annual. Culms 20–60(–100) cm, weak, often decumbent or geniculate below. Blades thin, flat, 3–10 mm broad. Panicle 6–18 cm, appressed/erect-spreading branches 2–5 cm, these more or less densely pubescent with hairs to 4 mm, sometimes glandular hairs. Glume/lower lemma 3–4 mm long appressed-pubescent, usually awnless. Upper lemma often shortly awn-tipped, awn usually <0.5 mm. Chromosome number not reported.
Gould Texas regions 8 and 10, infrequent, open plains/rocky valleys/fields/ditches; general western Texas to Arizona/northern Mexico. Flowering June–November. Gould notes a historical report as *E. gracilis* var. *minor* without examined Texas collections (retain his caution).

### 3B. E. lemmonii var. gracilis (Fourn.) Gould — p.433
Historical synonym *Eriochloa gracilis* (Fourn.) Hitchc. in Gould's printed treatment, SOUTHWESTERN CUPGRASS.
Tufted annual, differing from typical variety by long dense hairs of inflorescence branches often being shorter than glumes/spikelets; glumes comparatively larger and often blunt (as described on page); generally glabrous herbage. Chromosome 2n=36.
Gould Texas regions 7 and 10, infrequent, same types of habitats as typical variety; general Oklahoma/southern California south to western Texas and northern Mexico. Flowering June–November.
**Explicit Gould caution:** He states the two varieties differ conspicuously in characteristic forms, but spikelet size/shape and pubescence of leaves/inflorescence branches are frequently not correlated, and the two taxa cannot be satisfactorily segregated as separate species. Do not set overly rigid exclusions for these characters.

## 4. Eriochloa sericea (Scheele) Munro — p.433
Historical citation in Vasey, U.S.D.A. Div. Bot. Bull. 12 p.1 (1890), TEXAS CUPGRASS.
Perennial, culms tufted 50–100 cm, erect, firm, somewhat knotty base. Nodes pubescent. Sheaths at least lower ones finely pubescent. Blades elongate mostly 2–3 mm broad, flat/infrequently involute, glabrous or finely pubescent. Inflorescence narrow/contracted, usually panicle with 4–10 widely spaced stiff erect branches 1.5–3 cm. Panicle axis and branches ciliate/villous with hairs sometimes 2–4 mm. Spikelets ovate, glumes and lower-floret lemma appressed-hirsute, awnless, 3.6–4.2 mm long. Upper lemma firm, finely rugose, minutely awn-tipped. Chromosome 2n=54.
Gould Texas regions 2,4,5,6,7,8 in prairies and grassy openings, Oklahoma/Texas/northern Mexico. Flowering March–November. Gould notes species persists under grazing and may survive in protected shrub cover, as a historical ecological comment, not a present-day management recommendation.

## 96. Panicum L. — beginning p.433 ONLY
Annuals and perennials of diverse habit, ligule a membrane, fringe of hairs or absent. Inflorescence open or contracted panicle; spikelets awnless, 2-flowered, lower floret neuter/staminate, upper perfect. Source page stops here; obtain p.434 onward before claiming completed genus details or species keys. Do not infer missing traits.
