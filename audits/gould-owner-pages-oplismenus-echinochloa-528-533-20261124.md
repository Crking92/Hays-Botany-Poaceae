## 100. Oplismenus Beauv. — p.529
Gould reports about ten species in tropics/subtropics of both hemispheres, none native to United States (historical genus statement).

### 1. Oplismenus hirtellus (L.) Beauv. — p.529
Historical citation Ess. Agrost. 54, 168, 1812; common name BASKETGRASS, Fig.278. Annual with stoloniferous trailing culms and thin broad short blades. Erect flowering shoots mostly 15–25 cm tall; culm nodes and sheath margins hirsute. Ligule ciliate membrane. Blades scabrous and sparsely hispid or hirsute, ovate to ovate-lanceolate, mostly 1.5–7 cm long and 5–13 mm broad, widely spreading and often reflexed at maturity. Inflorescence a panicle of usually 3–7 widely spaced spike-like branches, these mostly 1–6 cm long and with 2–9 sessile or subsessile spikelets in two rows. Disarticulation usually below glumes but occasionally just below perfect floret. Glumes about equal, awned from minutely notched apex; awn of first glume mostly 0.5–1 cm long, much longer than that of second glume. Lemma of lower floret flared about as long as second glume, awned. Lemma of upper floret smooth, indurate, elliptic, acute at apex, margins almost completely enclosing palea. Chromosome 2n=72.
Gould historical Texas: regions 1,2,3 and southern portion region 4, in shade of shrubs or trees, usually moist soils. General tropical/subtropical New World, introduced/adventive United States. Flowering throughout year under favorable moisture/temperature.
**Taxonomic note:** Gould says most/all Texas plants referable to *O. hirtellus* subsp. *setarius* (Lam.) Mez (= *O. setarius* (Lam.) R. & S.); contrasts Hitchcock (1935) and Chase (1951) Houston record of *O. hirtellus* var. *hirtellus* with long slender branches; suggests possible robust plants of subsp. *setarius*. Preserve historical uncertainty; do not silently resolve accepted name.

## 101. ECHINOCHLOA Beauv. — genus pp.529–531
Gould: coarse annuals/perennials, mostly weak succulent culms, broad thin flat blades. Ligule ring of hairs or absent. Inflorescence panicle with few to numerous simple/rebranched densely flowered branches. Spikelets subsessile, in irregular fascicles or regular rows. Disarticulation below glumes. Glumes and lemma of lower floret variously scabrous/hairy, less frequently glabrous. First glume present, acute or short-awned, much shorter than second glume. Second glume and lower-floret lemma about equal, awned or awnless. Lemma of upper floret indurate, smooth/shiny, inrolled margins, and usually abruptly pointed apex. Palea of upper floret similar in texture, narrowed to free pointed tip. Gould reports about 20 species in warm world, often weedy, and bases US treatment on Gould, Ali & Fairbrothers (1972). This genus statement is NOT a universal hair/ligule score for all species.

## 1. Echinochloa polystachya (H.B.K.) Hitchc. — pp.532–533
Gould citation Contr. U.S. Natl. Herb. 22:135 (1920); coarse perennial, stout culms 1–2 m tall, frequently decumbent at base and rooting at lower nodes. Culm nodes glabrous or minutely puberulent. Leaves glabrous or lower sheaths hirsute/hispid. Ligule ring of stiff yellowish hairs present at least on lower leaves. Blades flat, to 3 cm broad, to 40 cm long. Panicle contracted, usually 15–30 cm, axis and branches sharply angled, stout papilla-based setae on ridges. Spikelets 4.5–6.5 mm long to base of awn. Second glume acuminate or short awn-tipped. Lemma of lower floret with awn 2–15 mm. Lower floret with large well-developed stamens/palea. Lemma of upper floret narrow ovate/elliptic, coriaceous apex obtuse, membranous withering tip, chromosome number not reported.
Gould Texas distribution southern region 2 in wet ditches/swales near coast; general southern Louisiana and Texas, southward West Indies to Argentina. Flowering fall.
Gould notes a June 1950 Jefferson County collection assigned to *E. polystachya* may represent Mexican *E. halciformis* (and an alternative *E. spectabilis* concept), based partly on long awns/flower morphology; **historical taxonomic ambiguity retained**, not an identification confirmed by this page.

## 2. Echinochloa muricata (Beauv.) Fern. — p.533, PARTIAL
Historical citation Rhodora 17:106 (1915), based on *Setaria muricata* Beauv. Coarse annual, culms mostly 50–150 cm, sometimes from a few cm to >1 m, glabrous to somewhat scabrous, branching from base. Sheaths glabrous, basal sheaths prominently distichous in some plants. Ligules absent. Blades mostly 1–2.5 cm broad, glabrous, margins minutely serrate. Panicles mostly 10–30 cm, occasionally longer, typically with spreading, somewhat distant branches 2–8 cm long. Spikelets green/purple, broadly ovate, awned or awnless, usually conspicuously echinate with stout papilla-based hairs. Second glume and lower-floret lemma about equal, both usually hispid with papilla-based hairs. Lower-floret lemma awnless or with awn to 25 mm. Lower-floret palea well developed, whitish/purplish. Upper-floret lemma broadly ovate, abruptly narrowing to acuminate, non-withering tip. Anthers mostly 0.5–1 mm. Caryopsis broadly oblong or orbicular, whitish/brownish at maturity, 1.2–1.9 mm. Chromosome 2n=36.
Key p.533 to varieties:
- Spikelets 3.5 mm or more long to base of awn/mucronate tip of lower-floret lemma; lower lemma usually awn >=6 mm, infrequently all awnless => 2A. *E. muricata* var. *muricata*.
- Spikelets <3.5 mm; lower lemma awnless or awn to 6 (infrequently 10) mm => 2B. *E. muricata* var. *microstachya*.
