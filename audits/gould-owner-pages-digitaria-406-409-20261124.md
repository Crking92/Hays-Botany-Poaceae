## 89. Digitaria Heister — genus p.406
Annuals and perennials with erect or decumbent-spreading culms, membranous ligules, usually thin flat blades. Inflorescence panicle with few to numerous slender spike-like branches, usually subdigitate or sparingly branched near base. Spikelets subsessile or short-pedicelled, in two rows on a flat or 3-angled, often winged rachis. Spikelets 2-flowered, lower floret staminate or neuter, upper perfect. Disarticulation below glumes. First glume minute or absent. Second glume well developed but usually shorter than lemma of lower floret. Lemma of upper floret relatively narrow, acute or acuminate, firm, cartilaginous but not hard, margins thin and flat, not inrolled over palea. About 300 species distributed temperate/tropical world (Gould's historical count).

## 1. Digitaria sanguinalis (L.) Scop. — p.408, continuation p.409
Gould name and citation: *Digitaria sanguinalis* (L.) Scop., Fl. Carn. ed.2 1:52 (1772); HAIRY CRABGRASS; Fig.218 p.409.
- Annual, soft weak stems, decumbent-spreading and branching at base, often rooting at lower nodes.
- Sheaths pubescent with papilla-based hairs.
- Ligules glabrous, irregularly dissected, mostly 1–2.5 mm.
- Blades 5–10 mm broad, usually pubescent on both surfaces, papilla-based hairs.
- Inflorescence with usually 4–9 primary branches digitate at culm apex or at one or two verticils below; branches slender, unbranched, mostly 5–14 cm.
- Branch rachis strongly 3-angled and slightly winged, 1–1.4 mm broad; spikelets in unequally pedicelled pairs, members similar.
- Spikelets 2.2–3 mm long. First glume minute, scale-like, obtuse/acute. Second glume 1–1.7 mm, puberulent on margins.
- Lemma of lower floret with 5 strong nerves, lateral nerves scabrous at least above.
- Palea of lower floret minute or absent. Upper-floret lemma about as long as spikelet.
- Mean pollen size 32.2–34.5 microns. Chromosome number 2n=36.
- Gould reports Texas regions 1,3,4,5,7,8,9,10. General: major distribution North America Canada south to Virginia/Kentucky/Texas and west to California; reportedly adventive from Europe, common weed roadsides, lawns, field borders and gardens. These are historical distribution assertions, NOT Hays county occurrence/nativity proof.
- Flowering July–November.
- Fig.218 on p.409: plant, two views of spikelet and floret; from Hitchcock (1935), printed botanical figure without established independent image-reuse permission.
