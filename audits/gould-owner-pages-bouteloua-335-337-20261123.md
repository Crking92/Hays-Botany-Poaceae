## Genus Bouteloua Lag. — p.335
Tufted annuals and perennials of diverse habit, some rhizomatous or stoloniferous. Mostly basal leaves, blades flat or folded, usually narrow. Ligule commonly a ring of hairs, infrequently a thin glabrous membrane. Inflorescence of 1–numerous short spicate branches arranged close or distant along main axis. Branches with 1–numerous sessile spikelets in two rows along margins of angular or flattened rachis. Disarticulation at base of branch rachis or above glumes. Spikelets with one perfect floret and 1–3 staminate or sterile florets above. Glumes lanceolate, 1-nerved, unequal to nearly equal, awnless or short-awned. Lemmas 3-nerved, midnerve often prolonged into an awn, lateral nerves occasionally short-awned. Paleas membranous, their 2 nerves occasionally awn-tipped. About 40 species, mostly North America but some Central/South America (historical statement).

## Bouteloua curtipendula (Michx.) Torr. — p.337, account begins only
Historical citation: Emory, Notes Mil. Recon. 154 (1848); SIDEOATS GRAMA. Perennial, flat linear leaf blades, herbage mostly glabrous, frequently leaf margins puberulent. Lower margins of blades usually sparsely ciliate with papilla-based hairs. Ligule short dense fringe of hairs seldom >0.5 mm. Inflorescence usually 30–50 short pendant branches 1–3(–4) cm long; branches bearing 1–12 or more sessile spikelets. Mean branch/spikelet relationship noted to vary along culm (p.337 text: number of spikelets per branch less at culm apex than base). Glumes glabrous or scabrous, first glume two-thirds or more as long as second, second usually 5.5–8 mm. Lemma usually slightly shorter than second glume, glabrous or scabrous-strigose, often minutely rugose, acute or slightly 3-toothed at apex, with nerves extending as short mucros. Paleas slightly shorter than lemmas and similar texture. **Continues onto p.338.** Crucially, '1–2 or more' spikelets must NOT be forced into a universal maximum from genus key alone.
