## 79. Hilaria H.B.K. — genus description p.366
Perennials, mostly rhizomatous or stoloniferous. Leaf blades narrow, usually short, flat or involute. Ligule a lacerate, often ciliate membrane. Inflorescence slender, dense, bilateral spike, spikelets in clusters of three at each rachis node; clusters deciduous as a whole. Spikelets of cluster dissimilar: two lateral one- to two-flowered, staminate; central one-flowered, perfect. Glumes firm, tall, usually asymmetrical, bearing awn on one side from about middle. Lemmas thin, three-nerved, awned or awnless. Paleas about as large as lemmas and similar texture. Gould's historical count about seven species, mostly southern North America, one reaching southward to Venezuela.

## Gould genus species key — p.366, HIERARCHICAL (not a verified modern key)
- Base thick and hard, with stout scaly rhizomes, stolons not produced, blades relatively thick, flat or involute:
  - Lateral spikelet glumes broadened and flabellate at apex => 1. *H. mutica*.
  - Lateral spikelet glumes not broadened/flabellate at apex, usually tapering from middle to tip => 2. *H. jamesii*.
- Base not thick/hard, stout scaly rhizomes not produced; slender looping stolons typically present, blades thin and flat:
  - Glumes pale, without black glands or with only few scattered glands; spikelet clusters 5–6 mm long => 3. *H. belangeri*.
  - Glumes dark at least below middle, densely beset with minute black glands; spikelet clusters 6.5–8 mm long => 4. *H. swallenii*.
Important: a genus key's negatives apply to a key path and historical species concept, not automatically to a modern named species with unclear synonym relationship.

## 1. Hilaria mutica (Buckl.) Benth. — pp.366–368
Common name TOBOSA. Historical citation J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 19:62 (1881).
Perennial with culms from a firm or hard, usually rhizomatous base; thick scaly rhizomes usually short and much branched. Culms slender, tough/wiry, mostly 30–75 cm tall, glabrous or scabrous-puberulent at nodes. Leaves glabrous, scabrous or occasionally with a few long hairs. Sheaths rounded on back. Ligule fringed membrane 1 mm or less. Blades firm, flat or more often involute, 2–4(–5) mm broad. Spikes mostly 4–8 cm, with 8–25 closely placed subsessile spikelet clusters. Clusters mostly 6–9 mm, three subequal spikelets: central one perfect, two lateral staminate. Lateral glumes darkened upward to fan-shaped, rounded/truncate, densely or sparsely ciliate on margins, medial glumes lateral, scabrous or hairy and 0.5–3 mm long. Glumes of central spikelet narrow, usually short, irregularly cleft, sometimes short-awned. Lemmas thin, entire or erose/ciliate at apex, awnless or minutely awn-tipped. Lemma and palea of central spikelet mostly 5–6 mm. Upper portion p.368: lateral lemma margins more or less inrolled, dark stigmas protruding; anthers 3–4 mm; chromosome counts reported 2n=36,54,72,150, and aneuploid 2n=33 and 74 (retain Gould reports rather than resolve as a single karyotype).
Gould historical distribution Texas regions 6,7,8,9,10, dry rocky slopes/level plains/plateaus; general Oklahoma/Texas to Arizona/northern Mexico. Flowering April–August, occasionally October. Fig.193 p.367: plant and spikelet cluster; caption from Gould 1951.

## 2. Hilaria jamesii (Torr.) Benth. — p.368
Common name GALLETA. Historical citation J. Linn. Soc., Bot. 19:62 (1881).
Similar to *H. mutica* in general characters. Culm nodes often hairy. Hairs at base of spikelet cluster mostly 3.5–5 mm. Glumes of lateral spikelets narrowing from middle upward, not fan-shaped; medial glumes with lateral awn usually 3–6(–8) mm long. Chromosome number 2n=18.
Gould Texas regions 8,9,10, dry rocky ledges, rolling slopes and valley flats; general Wyoming/Nevada south to Texas/Arizona/southern California. Flowering June–September.
Fig.194 p.369: two views of spikelet cluster; adjacent to *H. belangeri* Fig.195, keep figures separately attributed.

## 3. Hilaria belangeri (Steud.) Nash — pp.368–369
Common name COMMON CURLYMESQUITE. Historical citation N. Amer. Flora 17:135 (1912).
Low tufted perennial, erect floriferous culms from small clumps, stolons wiry, widely spreading, often become free culms mostly 10–30 cm tall. Culm nodes, especially lower ones, densely bearded with spreading hairs. Ligule membranous, lacerate, mostly 0.5–1 mm. Blades short, flat or less commonly involute, scabrous, often sparsely pilose, 1–2(–3) mm broad, mostly 2–3.5 cm long, exserted on slender almost filiform peduncles. Spikes mostly 4–8 spikelet clusters, typically 4.5–6 mm long. Lateral glumes narrowly linear, subequal, usually shorter than lemmas; outer glume slightly broadened above, notched/lobed, inner shorter and narrower, frequently with midnerve extended as mucro <1 mm. Glumes of central spikelet slender, glabrous or scabrous, slightly broadened above with scabrous awns mostly 2.5–5 mm long. Lemmas thin narrow/obovate, awnless. Chromosome number 2n=36,72 and several aneuploid numbers cited. Gould distribution all Texas regions except 1/3, but absent northeastern part of 2; dry rocky slopes, dry hillsides, grassy/brushy plains. General Texas to Arizona/adjacent northern Mexican states. Flowering mostly August–October, occasionally March–November.
