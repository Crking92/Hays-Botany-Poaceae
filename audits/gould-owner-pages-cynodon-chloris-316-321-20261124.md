## 72. Cynodon L. Rich. — p.316
Gould: about 10 species, mostly native Africa and Australia (historical genus estimate).

### Cynodon dactylon (L.) Pers. — p.316
* C. dactylon* (L.) Pers., Syn. Pl. 1:85 (1805), BERMUDAGRASS, Fig.167.
Low, mat-forming stoloniferous and rhizomatous perennial, rhizomes stout creeping extensive. Culms weak, mostly stoloniferous, only floriferous shoots/short tips erect, these 10–50 cm. Sheaths rounded, glabrous except tufts of hair on either side of collar and into ligule. Ligule ciliate membrane mostly 0.2–0.5 mm long. Blades linear, flat or folded, 1–3(–4) mm broad, mostly 3–8(–14) cm long, glabrous. Inflorescence usually 3–5(2–7) digitately arranged spicate branches; branches glabrous or scabrous, mostly 2–6 cm, floriferous to base, with numerous awnless spikelets closely imbricated in two rows on a narrow flattened/triangular rachis. Spikelets single perfect floret, rachilla prolonged behind palea, often bearing rudimentary glume. Glumes lanceolate, subequal, 1-nerved, about two-thirds as long as lemma. Lemmas mostly 2–2.5 mm long, firm, shiny, awnless, acute, laterally compressed/keeled, 3-nerved, lateral nerves near margins, midnerve obscurely pubescent/scabrous. Palea narrow, slightly shorter. Caryopsis 3-sided or laterally flattened (printed “3-sided or laterally flattened”); chromosome 2n=36 for Texas plants, counts of 2n=15 and 40 also reported (retain reported variability and source context).
Gould historical distribution statewide, commonly cultivated lawn/pasture and frequent ditches, vacant lots, roadways, along streams, lakes, marshy swales. General widespread warmer world, native tropical/subtropical Africa. Flowering throughout year under favorable conditions.

## 73. Chloris Swartz — genus pp.316–317
Fibrous-rooted annuals or rhizomatous, stoloniferous or cespitose perennials; erect culms, panicles open to contracted, of few to several digitately to subdigitately arranged one-sided spicate branches. Leaves with rounded to flattened overlapping sheaths, often restricted to lower culm, usually glabrous/scabrous, rarely pilose. Ligule commonly ciliate crown, sometimes wanting. Spikelets imbricate to distant, usually pale to tawny, one-flowered perfect florets; disarticulation above glumes. Glumes usually unequal, lanceolate, glabrous with scabrous nerves, acute sometimes acuminate, shorter than florets. Lemma of lower perfect floret lanceolate, 3-nerved, margins shortly/prominently pubescent, glabrous/scabrous internerves, nerves usually scabrous, awned from tip or between two teeth. Reduced florets sometimes 2–3, varying from rudimentary to cylindrical or inflated-obovoid, acute/truncate, awned or awnless. Caryopses brown ovoid to trigonous, embryo usually one-third to one-half length. Basic chromosome numbers x=9 and 10.
Gould historical estimate ~56 species worldwide, largest US species count in Texas. Gould notes Clayton (1967) merging *Trichloris* into *Chloris*, while keeping *Eustachys* distinct; **historical nomenclature note**, check accepted genus assignments independently before canonical updates.

## Chloris key pp.318–319 — CONDITIONAL source branches

- Lower floret awned between 2 lobes: lobes prominently awned, lateral awns about equal central => 14. *C. crinita*; lobes short-awned, lateral one-fifth or less central => second glume abruptly tapering to prominent awn, branches 7–20, inserted along axis => 13. *C. pluriflora*; second glume narrow lanceolate, attenuate, awnless, branches 3–9 digitate => 11. *C. divaricata*.

## 1. Chloris gayana Kunth — pp.319–320
*Chloris gayana* Kunth, Rev. Gram. 1:89 (1829), RHODESGRASS, Fig.168.
Stoloniferous, occasionally tufted, glabrous. Sheaths glabrous to scabrous. Ligule ciliate fringe. Blades flat, scabrous to 30 cm, 1.5 cm wide. Panicle with 9–30 digitate, somewhat divaricate branches 8–15 cm long. Spikelets imbricate, ~10 per cm rachis, pale to tawny. Glumes narrowly lanceolate, glabrous with scabrous nerves; first glume 1.4–2.5 mm, second 2.5–4.3 mm. Lemma of lower floret ovate to obovate, somewhat gibbous, body 2.5–3.2 mm, awn 1.5–6.5 mm, margins usually with prominent tufts of hairs near apex, occasionally appressed pilose. Sterile florets 2–4 rarely 1, similar to perfect floret but smaller, lowermost 2–2.3 mm, uppermost reduced to turbinate cup. Chromosome counts reported 2n=20, 30, 40.
Gould distribution Texas regions 2 and 6 as important cultivated forage, commercial escape along roadsides, mostly southern Texas; general worldwide warm tropics/temperate, probably originally tropical east Africa. Flowering May–December.
Fig.168 p.320: plant and spikelet from Gould and Box (1965).

## 2. Chloris virgata Sw. — p.321
*Chloris virgata* Sw., Fl. Ind. Occ. 1:203 (1797), SHOWY CHLORIS, Fig.169 (figure NOT supplied).
Annual, extremely variable in habit, usually tufted, glabrous culms from a few to >1 m tall, often decumbent and stoloniferous at base. Sheaths usually glabrous, sometimes densely pilose near ligule. Ligule ciliate fringe or wanting. Blades flat, usually glabrous, occasionally pilose, to 30 cm long and 1.5 cm wide. Panicles 4–20 digitate, more or less erect branches 5–10 cm. Spikelets tawny, tightly imbricate, ~10 per cm of rachis. Glumes narrowly lanceolate, hyaline, glabrous with scabrous midnerve; first 1.5–2.5 mm, second 2.5–4.3 mm. Lemma of lower floret ovate/elliptic, prominently gibbous dorsally, with keels and midnerves, upper margins ciliate, 2.5–4.2 mm; awn usually 5–15 mm. Sterile florets 1, rarely 2, similar shape but reduced, 1.4–2.9 mm, awns 3–9.5 mm. Chromosome numbers 2n=22, 20, 26, 40 reported. Gould distribution throughout Texas roadside/wasteground; general tropical and warm temperate regions worldwide. Flowering May–November.

## 3. Chloris verticillata Nutt. — p.321
*Chloris verticillata* Nutt., Trans. Amer. Philos. Soc. 5:150 (1837), WINDMILLGRASS, Fig.170 (figure NOT supplied). Perennial, culms erect to decumbent, glabrous, 15–40 cm. Sheaths glabrous. Ligule ciliate crown with hairs 2–8 mm long. Blades glabrous/scabrous to 15 cm long, 2–3 mm broad. Panicle with 10–16 laterally spreading branches 5–15 cm, well distributed in 2–5 verticils often separated by 5 mm or more, axis usually terminating upright branch. Spikelets appressed tawny widely spaced, 4–7/cm rachis. Glumes lanceolate, membranous, glabrous except scabrous midnerve; first 2.8–3.3 mm, second 2.3–3 mm. Lemma lower floret elliptic/lanceolate, 2.3–5 mm, glabrous keel and appressed-pubescent margins, acute to obtuse; awn 4.8–9 mm. Sterile floret 1, oblong, somewhat inflated, truncate, glabrous, 1.1–2.3 mm, awn 2–7 mm. Chromosome 2n=40. Gould Texas regions 5,7,8,9,10, often sandy/dry grasslands, roadsides, lawns and parks. General central US, Nebraska/Louisiana/Texas/New Mexico/Arizona. Gould notes extensive hybridization with *Chloris cucullata* where they grow together. Flowering May–September.
