## 44. Elymus L. — genus p.163
Cespitose or rhizomatous perennials; membranous ligules and usually flat blades. Slender pointed auricles usually present on either side of sheath–blade junction. Inflorescence a spike with 2–3 spikelets at a node (Texas species), sometimes a single spikelet at one/more nodes in upper nodes. Disarticulation between florets and above glumes, or at rachis nodes. Glumes firm or soft, narrow and setaceous to broad and blunt, 1–several-nerved, awned or awnless. Lemmas about as long as glumes or longer, usually 5–7-nerved, rounded on back, awned or awnless. Paleas usually obtuse/truncate, about as long as lemma. Historical genus estimate about 75 species, widespread cooler regions Northern Hemisphere and southern South America; Gould notes some formerly referred to *Sitanion*.

## Hierarchical species/variety key — p.163
- Lemmas awnless or awns 0.2 cm or less, rhizomatous => 1. *E. triticoides*.
- Lemmas with awns 0.3–8 cm or more, plants not rhizomatous:
  - Inflorescence rachis persistent at maturity, disarticulation above glumes between florets, glume awns 4.5 mm or less:
    - Glumes strongly bowed out at base, thickened, shiny, 1.2–2 mm broad => 3. *E. virginicus*.
    - Glumes not/slightly bowed:
      - Glumes flat, not/slightly thickened at base, 0.7–1.2 mm broad => 2A. *E. canadensis* var. *canadensis*.
      - Glumes terete/nearly so, indurate at least lower portion, 0.2–0.5 mm broad => 2B. *E. canadensis* var. *interruptus*.
  - Inflorescence axis readily disarticulating at maturity, spikelets falling with sections of rachis, glume awns 5–11 mm => 4. *E. longifolius*.
Preserve terminal placement, historical rank, and alternative states; key not automatically the universal profile for species.

## 1. Elymus triticoides Buckl. — pp.163–164
Historical citation Proc. Acad. Nat. Sci. Philadelphia 1862:99 (1862), CREEPING WILDRYE. Culms firm, erect, glabrous, usually glaucous, 40–120 cm tall, arising from extensive slender creeping rhizomes. Ligules truncate, ciliate, 1–6 mm long. Blades stiff, usually glaucous, flat or involute, elongate, mostly 3–6 mm broad. Spikes 6–20 cm long; spikelets usually paired at lower/middle rachis nodes, solitary above; spikelets mostly 10–16 mm, 4–6-flowered. Glumes slender, subulate, widely separated with back of lowermost lemma more or less centered between them; glume nerves often not evident. Lemmas smooth/slightly scabrous, acute/mucronate. Chromosome 2n=28.
Gould Texas: Chase (1951) reportedly region 10 and Gould (1969) regions 8 and 10 but no Texas specimens seen by Gould. General western US, Montana/Washington south to California/northern Mexico. Flowering late spring/early summer. **Historical Texas specimen uncertainty is NOT county occurrence.**

## 2. Elymus canadensis L. — p.164
Historical citation Sp. Pl. 83 (1753). Tufted perennial lacking rhizomes. Culms usually decumbent at base, 80–150 cm, in small/occasionally large clumps. Slender fragile auricles usually present on either side sheath–blade junction. Ligule truncate membrane 0.5–1 mm. Blades flat or folded, elongate, mostly 4–12 mm broad, glabrous or variously pubescent. Spikes erect or nodding, 8–20 cm; spikelets paired or in threes at each node. Spikelets mostly 3–5-flowered. Glumes about equal, tapering to awn usually longer than body. Lemmas glabrous, hispid or scabrous, mostly 0.8–1 cm or more long, with flexuous awns 1.5–5 cm, usually outward-curving at maturity. Chromosome 2n=28.

### 2A. E. canadensis var. canadensis — pp.164–165
CANADA WILDRYE; Fig.83 p.165. Glumes flat, slightly broadened above base; lemmas glabrous or scabrous. Gould Texas statewide except southern South Texas Plains region 6, mostly shaded sites, stream and river bottoms, woods borders and moist ravines. Flowering mostly March–June, infrequently summer/early autumn. Figure 83 depicts plant, glumes, floret (Gould 1951).

### 2B. E. canadensis var. interruptus (Buckl.) Church — pp.164–165
TEXAS WILDRYE. Glumes 15–30 mm long, equal, base indurate and often terete, usually <0.5 mm broad; lemmas 5–9 mm, glabrous, with straight awn 15–20 mm. Gould Texas regions 7 and 10, rocky/open woodlands and moist canyons, infrequent; general Oklahoma/Texas/New Mexico. Flowering April–August.
Gould explains that some earlier authors treated this as a distinct species; elsewhere the geographic species-level names may differ. **Taxonomic reconciliation hold; don't silently equate with a present-day taxon without crosswalk.**

## 3. Elymus virginicus L. — pp.166–167
Historical citation Sp. Pl. 84 (1753), VIRGINIA WILDRYE; Fig.84 p.166. Culms tufted, stiffly erect or decumbent at base, mostly 60–120 cm tall, green or sometimes glaucous. Sheaths glabrous. Ligule minute, truncate membrane. Blades flat, glabrous, scabrous or minutely pubescent, mostly 5–15 mm broad. Spike usually stiffly erect, often partly enclosed by upper sheath. Glumes strongly nerved, indurate, yellowish, nerveless and bowed out at base; apex often somewhat curved, tapering into straight awn shorter to longer than body. Lemmas glabrous or sparsely hirsute with straight/slightly curved awn 5–25 mm. Chromosome 2n=28.
Gould Texas all regions except 9 and 10, most abundant eastern half, along creek/woods and open woodlands; general through eastern US, not extreme SW. Flowering April–June, occasionally as late as September.
Gould reports several historical varieties as poorly distinctive/inconsistent, including var. *australis*, var. *glabriflorus*, and var. *intermedius*, and notes hybridization with other Elymus in central/eastern/northern states. Preserve as historical discussion, not modern resolved taxonomy.

## 4. Elymus longifolius J. G. Smith — p.167
Gould historical combination *Elymus longifolius* J.G. Smith, 1895; older *Sitanion longifolium* J.G. Smith, LONGLEAF SQUIRRELTAIL. Plants tufted, usually much branched at base. Culms geniculate or strictly erect, 25–60 cm tall. Sheaths usually glabrous and glaucous, occasionally puberulent/pubescent; sheath subtending spike inflated and larger than those below, slender pointed fragile auricles usually developed laterally at apex. Ligule minute membrane. Blades flat/involute, elongate, 2.5–6 mm broad, glabrous or less frequently puberulent/pubescent. Spikes 7–15 cm, usually exserted, with 2 or occasionally 3 spikelets per node. Rachilla readily disarticulating above glumes and between florets. Spikelets 2–6-flowered, terminal floret/florets often sterile. Glumes subulate, scabrous, usually 1-nerved, extending to awn 5–11 mm. Lemmas 7–12 mm, glabrous, scabrous or pubescent, obscurely 3–5-nerved, central nerve extending into stout spreading awn 5–10 cm. Chromosome 2n=28.
Gould Texas regions 7,8,9,10, dry open often-disturbed areas. General desert/montane habitats SD–Oregon south Texas/Arizona/California/northern Mexico. Flowering mostly May–September.
Gould discusses old placement in *Sitanion* and prior treatments as *Sitanion hystrix* var. *longifolium* (with variant spelling in source): historical name crosswalk required.

## 45. Agropyron Gaertn. — p.167 (next genus START ONLY)
Annuals/perennials (Texas plants all perennial), similar to Elymus in general characteristics/spike variation, but occasionally 2 spikelets per node. Gould historical count about 100 species, cooler Northern Hemisphere with few southern South American. Genus separation in North America historically disputed; further account continues beyond p.167. **Do not assign Agropyron description to Elymus.**
