## Melica nitens — Threeflower melicgrass

- **Hays iNaturalist:** 125 documented research-grade, wild-tagged photographed records.
- **Promising original observation:** [363172021, 6 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/363172021); [379835068, 2 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/379835068).
- **What the photo must show:** A whole flowering culm, branching pattern of the panicle; dangling elongated spikelets, two to three fertile florets, and distal narrowed sterile rudiments. If available, a macro showing a short split membranous ligule, glumes and rudiments.
- **Compare against:** *Melica mutica* in *Flora of North Central Texas*, printed p. 1284: *M. nitens* has a typically compound (rebranched) panicle, unequal glumes, nontriangular spikelets, longer ligules (3–6 mm), and narrower, non-truncate rudiments.
- **Caution:** The Flora's species paragraph contains a likely **mm/cm editorial typo** for inflorescence length; USDA NRCS lists open panicles 10–26 cm. Do not convert that printed '10–26 mm' into an ID-key trait. NRCS source: https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/plantmaterials/txpmcpg13835.pdf
- **Decision:** strong photo-review priority; identification and planting-context review still pending.

## Bothriochloa longipaniculata — Longspike beardgrass

- **Hays iNaturalist:** 25 documented research-grade, wild-tagged photographed records.
- **Promising original observation:** [377598342, 16 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/377598342); [392143264, 1 photograph](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/392143264).
- **What the photo must show:** Full silvery panicle against a ruler, branch/rames length versus main axis, comparison of sessile and pedicellate spikelets, several detached mature first glumes showing shape, luster and coloration, plus collar/ligule and vegetative waxiness.
- **Critical name issue:** The *Flora of North Central Texas* (printed p. 1246) treats *B. longipaniculata* within *B. laguroides* subsp. *torreyana*, which is represented in the 112-species project as **Bothriochloa torreyana (silver bluestem)**. Yet USGS Louisiana Plant ID and USDA GRIN recognize longipaniculata separately. This is a **species-concept disagreement**, NOT automatic proof it is a duplicate nor an automatic distinct addition.
- **Differentiation to test, not assume:** USGS notes longer panicles and narrower shiny green glumes in *longipaniculata* versus broader, duller glumes of the *torreyana* concept, with overlap. https://warcapps.usgs.gov/PlantID/Species/Details/3284
- **Decision:** **TAXONOMY HOLD / photo+specimen comparison with the existing silver bluestem card**; never create a separate ID card simply on the basis of iNaturalist's species label.

## Paspalum langei — Rustyseed paspalum

- **Hays iNaturalist:** 23 documented research-grade, wild-tagged photographed records.
- **Promising original observation:** [393289733, 4 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/393289733); [374141829, 1 photograph](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/374141829).
- **What the photo must show:** Whole plant + habitat (wet/shady ditchbank versus landscaping), multiple inflorescence racemes (typically 2–5), broad often crisped leaf blades (7–18 mm), and several spikelets at high magnification.
- **Diagnostic test:** First glume present on some/all spikelets; second glume and sterile lemma with brownish glandular blotches and pubescence. The first glume / gland pattern separates this taxon from several similar Paspalum in the *Flora* key (printed pp. 1306–1307).
- **Evidence caveat:** An ordinary photo of the flowering head cannot demonstrate glandular blotches or first glume presence. If absent, mark 'possible *Paspalum*; not diagnostic' until close-ups/voucher are checked.
- **Decision:** strong photo-review priority; macro spikelet evidence needed.

## Coelorachis cylindrica — Carolina jointtail / cylinder jointtail

- **Hays iNaturalist:** 10 documented research-grade, wild-tagged photographed records.
- **Promising original observation:** [232106369, 4 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/232106369); [279943509, 2 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/279943509).
- **What the photo must show:** Entire exceptionally narrow (~3 mm thick) cylindrical raceme, strongly noded central axis and its niche holding the sessile spikelet, paired fertile sessile and sterile pedicellate spikelets, glume pit and disarticulating joint, plus tiny membranous ligule (~0.5–1 mm).
- **Taxonomic note:** Regional *Flora* uses *Coelorachis cylindrica* (printed p. 1258), with *Mnesithea cylindrica* as a synonym; more recent Kew adopts a different genus placement. Retain name aliases without separate species cards.
- **Decision:** strong photo-review priority; microscopic rachis niche / glume details needed.

## Bromus catharticus — Rescue brome

- **Hays iNaturalist:** 171 documented research-grade, wild-tagged photographed records.
- **Promising original observation:** [379499753, 4 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/379499753); [366862129, 2 photographs](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/366862129).
- **What the photo must show:** Whole winter annual tuft and flowering stem, markedly flattened many-flowered spikelets, strongly keeled glumes/lemmas, short or absent awns, and hairy (spreading-pilose) leaf sheaths; inner ligule if available.
- **Compare against:** Other Hays *Bromus*: the *Flora* key (printed p. 1252) separates B. catharticus by strongly flattened spikelets and sharply keeled glumes/lemmas; short awn (if any). The Flora lists winter growth and Mar–May flowering. Texas A&M: https://rangeplants.tamu.edu/plant/rescuegrass/
- **Ecology:** Common introduced species in disturbed ground. Keep for *field identification* without counting it as a native restoration species.
- **Decision:** strong photo-review priority; multi-photo specimen QA before final ID release.
