# Hays 58 — Texas Hill Country vegetative-key crosswalk (2015)

**New local authority:** Hagenbuch and Lemke (2015), *Grasses of the Texas Hill Country: Vegetative key and descriptions*, Phytoneuron 2015-4:1–93, [free PDF](https://www.phytoneuron.net/2015Phytoneuron/04PhytoN-HillCountryGrasses.pdf).

The 2015 paper covers **160 grass species** from the Hill Country, using **vegetative characters** (ligules, collars, sheaths, nodes, growth forms) and Appendix B herbarium collections, including explicit Hays County labels. We checked its Appendix A named taxa against all 58 current Hays candidate names, preserving 2015 old genus/species concepts instead of conflating them with Kew.

## Targeted vegetative field-ID improvements

- **Melica nitens** (2015 name *Melica nitens*): Closed leaf sheaths nearly to throat; relatively long 3–6 mm membranous ligule with decurrent margins; new diagnostic collar/sheath target distinct from spikelet-only photo.
- **Rottboellia campestris** (2015 name *Coelorachis cylindrica*): Short knotty rhizomes; short 0.5–1 mm truncate membranous ligule; slender vegetative leaf blades. Distinguish current Kew Rottboellia campestris alias.
- **Stenotaphrum secundatum** (2015 name *Stenotaphrum secundatum*): Distichous leaves, short stolons, compressed sheaths and folded vernation. Vegetative features useful but cultivated turf is not proof of wild population.
- **Triticum aestivum** (2015 name *Triticum aestivum*): Cereal characters must be measured and cultivated field/volunteer escape context documented.
- **Echinochloa colona** (2015 name *Echinochloa colona*): Ligule absent; green blades often marked purple bands/V-shapes; compare ligule-free barnyardgrass relatives with spikelets.
- **Setaria viridis** (2015 name *Setaria viridis*): Annual; 1–2 mm ligule formed by fringe of hairs; leaf sheath often keeled, basal nodes sometimes hirsute.
- **Setaria leucopila** (2015 name *Setaria leucopila*): Perennial, culm sheath margins villous at throat; ciliate ligule with about 1–2 mm hairs; blades often pale/glaucous.
- **Sphenopholis obtusata** (2015 name *Sphenopholis obtusata*): Annual with divided often oblique collar; membranous 1.5–3 mm ligule, often lacerate.
- **Eragrostis curtipedicellata** (2015 name *Eragrostis curtipedicellata*): Viscid culms and sheaths, narrow leaves often sharply bent outward around 90 degrees, tiny hair-fringe ligule.
- **Digitaria californica** (2015 name *Digitaria californica*): Perennial with hard knotty base, rounded lower sheaths villous, 1–3 mm truncate membranous ligule; publication explicitly warns vegetation alone cannot reliably distinguish it from D. insularis and D. patens.
- **Janochloa antidotalis** (2015 name *Panicum antidotale*): Under old Panicum antidotale: short rhizomes, hard knotty base, prominent swollen antrorsely pubescent nodes, and 0.5–1 mm ciliate ligule.
- **Sporobolus cryptandrus** (2015 name *Sporobolus cryptandrus*): Perennial tuft, ligule hair fringe about 0.5–1 mm, overlapping sheaths, pilose collar margins; lower sheaths may conceal flowers.

## Scientific safeguards

* Source nomenclature is ~2015 and sometimes broader than the project's current Kew accepted species concepts. *Tridentopsis elongata* in the new classification cannot be certified from the broad 2015 *Tridens muticus* account; similarly B. longipaniculata has wider old silver-bluestem treatment.
* The paper's Hays cited gatherings are strong independent collecting leads, but original herbarium sheets must be found/checked before final identification. This does **not** alter the 317 prior iNaturalist photographs already examined.
* A species included in the Hill Country account is not necessarily independently documented in Hays. Do not infer it was observed locally unless the Appendix B county citation or other verified evidence specifically says Hays.
* Continue using *Flora of North Central Texas* for flower/spikelet traits, this study for vegetative traits and published local collecting context, and Kew for current accepted names.
