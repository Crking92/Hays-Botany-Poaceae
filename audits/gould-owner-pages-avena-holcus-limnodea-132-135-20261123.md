## Genus Avena L. — p.132
Gould: a genus of 15–20 species native to temperate Europe but widely cultivated elsewhere (historical statement). The page moves immediately to *Avena fatua*; do not falsely generalize its entire species description to all Avena taxa.

### Avena fatua L. sensu Gould — p.132
* Avena fatua* L., Sp. Pl. 80 (1753). Annual with thick, succulent culms and broad flat blades, mostly 30–120 cm tall.
- Sheaths glabrous or hispid with spreading hairs.
- Ligule whitish membrane, mostly 2–4 mm long, appearing as continuation of membranous sheath margins.
- Blades flat, elongate, mostly 5–12 mm broad, glabrous or ciliate on lower margins, occasionally sparsely hispid.
- Inflorescence loose panicle or raceme of spikes, usually 8–30 cm long.
- Spikelets usually 2–4 florets; where more than two, uppermost usually reduced and sterile.
- Disarticulation above glumes and between florets.
- Glumes large, broad, glabrous, acute/acuminate, broader hyaline margins and tip, 7–9-nerved, 3 distinct near center.
- Lemmas firm or hard, rounded on back, lowermost mostly 1.5–2 cm.
- Paleas thin, slightly shorter than lemmas, with widely-spaced ciliate nerves.
- Chromosome number 2n=42 (as printed).

### Variety key — p.133
- 1A *Avena fatua* var. *fatua*: lemmas with stiff, usually reddish-brown hairs on dorsal surface; spikelets usually 3–4-flowered; awn of lemma geniculate; 2.5–4 cm long (retain organ context from text: spikelet length).
- 1B *Avena fatua* var. *sativa*: lemmas glabrous; spikelets usually 2-flowered; awn of lemma not geniculate, irregularly developed or absent.

### Varieties, historical taxonomy and range — p.134
- 1A *Avena fatua* L. var. *fatua*, WILD OAT, Fig.66A: Gould distribution Texas Regions 2,3,7,8,9; infrequent weed of roadsides and other disturbed moist sites; general European grass now present almost throughout United States except southeastern states, especially abundant coastal California; flowering mostly April–May.
- 1B *Avena fatua* L. var. *sativa* (L.) Hausskn., Mitt. Geogr. Ges. Thüringen 3:238 (1885). Historical synonym under this treatment: *Avena sativa* L. COMMON OAT, Fig.66B: Gould reports occasional Texas cool-season weed of roadsides, field borders, vacant lots and ditchbanks; introduced from Europe as cultivated crop, not as commonly cultivated as previously; widespread U.S. except southeastern states. Flowering mostly March–June, occasionally winter months.
- Historical inclusion of cultivated oat as var. *sativa* is NOT automatic evidence that modern *Avena fatua* and *Avena sativa* should be merged in the learner. Crosswalk deliberately.

## Holcus L. — p.135
Gould historical intro: about eight species native to Europe/Africa, two adventive in United States.
### Holcus lanatus L. — p.135
*Holcus lanatus* L., Sp. Pl. 1048 (1753). Gould describes perennial, parenthetically “annual in Texas?” (explicit uncertainty). Culms thick, weak, somewhat compressed, mostly 25–100 cm tall.
- Sheaths rounded on back, puberulent/pub escent with spreading hairs, sometimes lower ones closed at base or up to middle.
- Ligule lacerate, ciliate membrane 1.5–3 mm, continuation of sheath margins.
- Blades soft flat elongate, mostly 5–10 mm broad, typically sparsely hispid or hirsute on both surfaces.
- Inflorescence irregularly contracted, densely flowered, 4–15 cm long, 1.5–5 cm broad, main axis and branches hispid.
- Spikelets 2-flowered, 4–6 mm long, disarticulation below glumes.
- Glumes subequal, ciliate on midnerve and often scabrous-hispid on back; first glume 1-nerved 3–4.5 mm, second much broader 3-nerved.
- Lower floret about 2 mm long, perfect, firm, smooth, shiny, awnless.
- Upper floret usually staminate, about as long as lower but slender, lemma with short, stout, hooked yellowish awn exerted from back near apex.
- Chromosome number 2n=14.
- Gould Texas distribution regions 1 and 2, no voucher specimens located by author; general adventive European grass, widely distributed US except some southern states. Flowering May–June. Treat this as historical distribution reporting, not verified Hays occurrence.

## Limnodea L. H. Dewey — pp.135–136, **account completed by owner-supplied p.136**
Gould: monotypic North American genus.
### Limnodea arkansana (Nutt.) L. H. Dewey — pp.135–136
Historical citation: Contr. U.S. Natl. Herb. 2:518 (1894); OZARKGRASS. Fig.67 [figure supplied on p.136; botanical evidence only, no image republication].
- Short-lived annual, weak culms thin and flattened; flat blades and contracted panicle of 1-flowered spikelets.
- Culms small clumps, mostly 20–60 cm tall.
- Sheaths rounded on back, often hispid.
- Ligule lacerate membrane 1–2 mm.
- Blades glabrous or more commonly hispidulous/hispid on both surfaces, 2–8 mm broad, 3–12 cm long.
- Panicles narrow contracted 5–20 cm long with few to several short branches; branches mostly floriferous to base or nearly so.
- Spikelets 3.5–4 mm long excluding awn; disarticulation below glumes.
- Glumes equal, as long as spikelet, firm, obscurely 3–5-nerved, rounded and minutely rugose on back, usually hispid but becoming glabrous or scabrous, acute and awnless at apex.
- Lemmas about as long as glumes but thinner, glabrous and minutely rugose, subacute with twisted and geniculate awn just below acute/minutely bifid apex; lemmas mostly 8–11 mm long [**check source's organ referent here; pictured text says 'Lemmas are mostly 8–11 mm long' but likely awns — do NOT score until rechecked**]. Paleas hyaline, narrow, shorter than lemmas, nerved only at base.
- Chromosome number 2n=14.
- **Continuation on owner-supplied p.136:** Gould reports distribution throughout Texas except regions 9 and 10; common grass of open woodlands, streambanks and ditchbanks of brushy grasslands. General range Arkansas and Oklahoma to Florida, Texas and northern Mexico. Flowering mostly late March to early June. Figure 67, inflorescence and spikelet, credited within book to Gould and Box (1965). This completes the visible treatment.

## Agrostis L. — p.136 (GENUS BEGINS, description possibly continues)
- Annuals and perennials, several with rhizomes. Ligule membranous. Blades flat or involute.
- Inflorescence open or contracted panicle; spikelets small, one-flowered, disarticulating above glumes (except in *A. semiverticillata*).
- Glumes thin, lanceolate, acute to acuminate, nearly equal; first glume usually 1-nerved, second 1–3-nerved.
- Lemmas thin, broad, 3- or 5-nerved, acute to obtuse or truncate at apex, awnless or awned from middle or below. Paleas present or absent.
- Page stops at genus description. Species treatments require later pages. Do not extrapolate to each species.
