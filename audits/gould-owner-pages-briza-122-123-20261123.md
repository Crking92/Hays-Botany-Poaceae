# Gould (1975), owner-supplied pp. 122–123 — Briza

Intake: 2026-11-23. Primary evidence: photographs supplied by owner of Frank W. Gould, *The Grasses of Texas*, printed pp. 122–123. Status: **source evidence archived, not yet reconciled with current Grass Lab scoring or taxonomy**. Keep page-specific historical distribution separate from modern county nativity. Figure 60 referenced only; no authorization to republish image.

## 27. BRIZA L. — p. 122
Annuals and perennials; Texas species annual, usually with open panicles of several-flowered, awnless spikelets. Ligules membranous. Florets crowded and widely spreading from rachilla. Disarticulation above glumes and between florets. Glumes subequal, broad, thin and papery, rounded on back, 3–9-nerved, spreading at right angles to rachilla. Lemmas similar to glumes, broader than long, broadly rounded at apex. Paleas short, rounded, with widely spaced nerves. Gould's historical geography: about 20 species; three native Europe, one Mexico and Central America, remaining South America.

Gould key, p.122:
- Spikelets 2–6 mm long → *Briza minor*.
- Spikelets mostly 12–25 mm long → *Briza maxima*.
This is species-specific evidence from a key and a strong comparative character; do not infer intermediate species absence globally.

## 1. Briza minor L. — pp. 122–123
Name and citation: *Briza minor* L., Sp. Pl. 70 (1753). Common name: LITTLE QUAKINGGRASS. Fig.60 (p.123).
- Delicate, short-lived annual, culms single or in small clumps, mostly 15–50 cm tall.
- Herbage glabrous or essentially so.
- Ligule 5–10 mm long, broadly pointed, with inverted V-shaped area of attachment continuing downward inside sheath margins to become sheath margins well below apex.
- Blades thin, flat, mostly 2–8 mm broad and 4–18 cm long, merging rather imperceptibly into sheath on abaxial surface.
- Panicles 3–15 cm long, typically much-branched and open, broad spikelets on long capillary spreading, usually kinked pedicels.
- Spikelets 2–6 mm long and about as broad, 3–12-flowered, glabrous, awnless.
- Glumes longer than successively shorter lemmas; spikelet somewhat pyramidal.
- Lemmas indistinctly nerved, firm, shiny, more or less pustulate/rugose centrally and broadly, thin margins.
- Caryopses light brown, orbicular, flattened on one side, mostly 0.6–0.8 mm long.
- Chromosome number printed as 2n=10 (2n=14 also reported for Texas plants but needing verification). Preserve uncertainty, do not collapse to a single proven count.
- Gould distribution: Texas regions 1, 2, 3 in moist woodland clearings and semi-disturbed roadside/ditchbank soils. General: native to Europe, established southeastern US, frequent Oregon and California.
- Flowering mostly April–May.
- Fig.60 p.123 *Briza minor* inflorescence credited “From Gould, 1968” (book figure: do not republish without rights).

## 2. Briza maxima L. — pp. 122–123
Name and citation: *Briza maxima* L., Sp. Pl. 70 (1753). Common name BIG QUAKINGGRASS.
- Annual; culms erect or decumbent at base, mostly 30–60 cm tall.
- Herbage glabrous or blades minutely scabrous.
- Ligule of uppermost leaf usually 10 mm or more long.
- Blades flat, 2–8 mm broad and 5–15(–20) cm long.
- Panicle few-flowered, often with only 1–6 spikelets but occasionally a dozen or more.
- Spikelets 12–25 mm long and 8–12 mm broad, on long slender drooping pedicels.
- Glumes and often lemmas with purple or brown margins.
- Lemmas with 5–7 greenish nerves, usually hirsute over back and margins.
- Chromosome number 2n=14.
- Gould distribution: records listed from Texas by Hitchcock's Manual (1951), but Gould questioned whether it persisted in the state and noted it was not listed by Correll and Johnston (1970) for Texas. General: European species introduced to US as ornamental, sometimes casual weed in a few localities.
- Flowering April–May.
- Range and Texas occurrence are **historical Gould statements, not evidence of a present-day Hays County population**.

## Comparison and integration safeguards
- Flowerhead size and number of spikelets are promising beginner-facing traits, but measure whole spikelet (not individual floret).
- *B. minor*: many tiny spikelets in broadly open much-branched panicle; *B. maxima*: few large drooping spikelets (with exceptions).
- Species descriptions and genus key are primary-source statements; filter states must still be reconciled against stored evidence and tested in multi-access key.
- Do not score region nativity/occurrence, change photographs, copy Fig.60 to public site, or modify existing verified taxon records on source intake alone.
