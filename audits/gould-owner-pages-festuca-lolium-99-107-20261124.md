## 22. FESTUCA L. — genus p.99
Tufted perennials with flat or involute blades; ligules membranous. Spikelets with 2–several florets, awned or awnless, in contracted or less frequently open panicles. Disarticulation above glumes and between florets. Glumes narrow, acute/acuminate, 1–3-nerved. Lemmas usually 5–7-nerved, rounded on back, obtuse or acute at apex, awned or awnless. Stamens 3; fruit of elliptic-oblong outline, palea adhering, occasionally fusiform. Historical estimate more than 100 Festuca species in temperate/cool world, only seven Texas species noted in this treatment, many infrequent/rare.

## Festuca hierarchical identification key — p.100
- Blades involute/folded, <3 mm broad, plants of high Trans-Pecos elevations:
  - ligules 3–4 mm, spikelets 2–3-flowered => 1. *F. ligulata*
  - ligules mostly 0.5–1.5 mm, spikelets 4–(infrequently 3)–8-flowered:
    - densely tufted, mostly erect at base, basal sheaths brown/reddish, remaining erect; blades usually glaucous => 2. *F. arizonica*
    - not densely tufted, usually decumbent at base, basal sheaths reddish/purplish, spreading and fibrillose with age; blades not glaucous => 3. *F. rubra*
- Blades flat or flat-and-folded, usually at least some >3 mm broad, plants absent or uncommon in Trans-Pecos:
  - spikelets 10–15 mm or more, florets mostly 5–(3)–10, lower panicle branches of inflorescence with florets widely spaced below middle => 4. *F. arundinacea*
  - spikelets <10 mm, florets 2–5, lower branches with spikelet-bearing below middle:
    - longest glumes 4–7 mm, lemmas 5–7 mm => 5. *F. versuta*
    - longest glumes <=3 mm, lemmas 3–4.5 (rarely 5) mm; spikelets not/slightly overlapping, rather uniformly distributed on upper half/third of branch => 6. *F. obtusa*
    - spikelets well imbricated and more/less densely clustered at branch tips => 7. *F. paradoxa*
**Key branches are conditional historical groupings; do not automatically score every named species as possessing all genus/branch states without exact account review.**

## 1. Festuca ligulata Swallen — pp.100–101
Historical citation Amer. J. Bot. 19:436 (1932); GUADALUPE FESCUE.
Cespitose perennial, culms mostly 50–80 cm tall, usually decumbent at base and often rhizomatous, scabrous below panicle. Sheaths glabrous. Ligules 3–4 mm. Blades 1–2 mm broad, flat or involute, scabrous. Panicle 6–10(–16) cm, with 1–3 stiffly erect/spreading scabrous branches; spikelets sessile or short-pedicelled, branches with 2–3-flowered spikelets. Glumes acute/scabrous, first about 3 mm, 1-nerved, second 3-nerved about 4 mm. Lemmas 4–6 mm, rounded on back, broadly acute/scabrous, faintly nerved; paleas slightly longer, 2-nerved. Chromosome number not reported.
Gould distribution Texas moist shaded higher Trans-Pecos mountain slopes region 10, general endemic to western Texas (historical claim). Flowering June–August.
**Review note:** Gould's key says 2–3-flowered, maintain that condition even if a general Festuca score has more florets.

## 2. Festuca arizonica Vasey — p.101, Fig.47 p.102
Historical citation Contr. U.S. Natl. Herb. 1:277 (1893), ARIZONA FESCUE. Cespitose tufted perennial culms mostly 45–90 cm; tough wiry bases. Leaves usually glaucous. Sheaths much broader than blade, glabrous/scabrous; lower ones laterally lobed at apex, lobes membranous and minutely ciliolate. Ligule membrane ~0.5–1.5 mm, rounded; blade firm filiform, folded/involute, scabrous, mostly 20–50 cm and 1.5 mm or less wide. Panicles 8–15 cm, contracted/open, densely flowered at narrow angles, lower branches occasionally slender/widely spreading. Spikelets 4–(3)–8-flowered, glumes lanceolate/acute or minutely scabrous at apex; first 1-nerved slightly shorter/narrower than second, second 3-nerved. Lemma rounded on back, narrowly acute, awnless or with awn <=5–7 mm; chromosome 2n=42.
Gould Texas high elevation Trans-Pecos region 10; western North American mountainous areas. Flowering June–August.
Fig.47 p.102 shows inflorescence/spikelet, from Gould (1965); reference only.

## 3. Festuca rubra L. — p.101
Historical citation Sp. Pl. 74 (1753), RED FESCUE. Said to be similar to *F. arizonica*, differing mainly in smooth reddish-purple/brownish basal sheaths becoming shredded/fibrillose in age, relatively soft glabrous blades mostly 1.5–2.5 mm broad, and minute ligule 0.1–0.4 mm long. Many chromosome numbers reported (diploid through aneuploid and polyploid), including 2n=14, 28, 42, 56, 70; specific alternate notes in source should be rechecked before scoring cytology. Gould Texas highest Davis Mountains slopes, region 10, infrequent; broad northern/global cooler distribution. Flowering summer.
**Species treated as similar rather than full redundant description; preserve comparison and uncertainty.**

## 4. Festuca arundinacea Schreb. — pp.101–103
Gould historical synonyms include *Festuca elatior* L. sensu stricto and *Festuca elatior* L. var. *arundinacea* (Schreb.) Wimm. (as printed; confirm author spellings for taxonomic code), TALL FESCUE, Fig.48 p.103.
Cespitose perennial without rhizomes. Culms stout, erect or curving-erect at base, 50–150(–200) cm. Sheaths smooth/scabrous, rounded on back, usually with narrow rounded auricles at apex, minutely hairy on auricles and at sheath junction with blade. Ligule from minute ciliate rim to membranous 2 mm. Blades elongate, flat/folded, 3–12 mm broad, often scabrous margins. Panicles mostly 10–30 cm, erect/somewhat nodding, contracted/narrow or lower branches long/spreading, many spikelets on lower one-third to one-half of lowermost branches. Rachilla glabrous to scabrous, disarticulating between florets. Spikelets short-pedicelled, appressed, 10–15(–18) mm, 5–(3)–10-flowered. Glumes glabrous, lanceolate, acute, membranous margins, first 1-nerved 4–6 mm, second 3-nerved, slightly longer. Lemmas 6–9 mm, rounded dorsally, 5-nerved, smooth/minutely rugose and scabrous on nerves; awnless or awn 1–4 mm. Paleas about as long, scabrous margins. Chromosome 2n=42.
Gould historic Texas occasional northern/eastern counties and regions 1,3,4,5,7,8,9, mostly seeded pastures or escaped/introduced and well established in temperate cool North America. Flowering mostly April–June.
Fig.48 p.103 plant, spikelet, floret from Hitchcock (1935) under a historic *F. elatior* combination; preserve caption history, no figure licensing.

### Meadow fescue note — p.103
Gould separately mentions *Festuca pratensis* Huds. as European diploid (2n=14) differing from *F. arundinacea* in several characters, well established in USA but apparently absent Texas except experimental plantings. **This is a historical comparison note, not a verified Texas occurrence and not a full Festuca pratensis treatment.**

## 5. Festuca versuta Beal — p.104, Fig.49 p.104
Historical citation Grasses N. Amer. 2:580 (1896), TEXAS FESCUE. Tufted perennial, slender culms, glabrous, 50–100 cm long. Sheaths rounded on back, not auriculate; ligules 0.5–1 mm. Blades linear, flat or sometimes folded, mostly 2–5 mm broad. Panicles 8–30 cm, slender spreading/arching branches, 2–4 flowered loosely aggregated spikelets. Glumes narrow, lanceolate, well developed, unequal to nearly equal, conspicuously scabrous; first glume 1-nerved mostly 4–7 mm, second 3-nerved 5.5–7 mm. Lemmas rounded on back, narrowly acute/acuminate and somewhat keeled at apex, occasionally minutely awn-tipped, lower 5–7 mm; paleas about as long. Chromosome number not reported. Gould distribution Texas south/eastern portions region 5, southeastern region 7 moist partially shaded sites, infrequent, general Arkansas/Oklahoma/Texas. Flowering March–June.
Fig.49 printed p.104: glumes and floret, reference only.

## 6. Festuca obtusa Biehler — p.104, Fig.50 p.105
Historical citation Pl. Nov. Herb. Spreng. Cent. 11:1807, NODDING FESCUE.
Tufted perennial, slender culms mostly 50–100 mm [**source reading likely cm; verify photographed text before codifying**], usually unbranched above base. Sheaths rounded, glabrous/sparsely hirsute, without auricles. Ligules 0.5–1 mm. Blades flat, elongate, mostly 3–7(–9) mm broad, tapering to long narrow tip, glabrous or sparsely pubescent adaxially. Panicles 10–25 cm, loose/open, relatively few-flowered, lower branches long/flexuous, loosely erect/spreading; spikelets mostly 5–(6) florets. Glumes well-developed, unequal to nearly equal, lanceolate, mostly 2–5 mm; first 1-nerved, second 3-nerved. Lemmas rounded, lanceolate/narrow oblong, acute/narrowly rounded at apex; lowermost florets 3–4.5 mm. Paleas about as long; chromosome 2n=42.
Gould Texas region 4 and northern regions 1/3 in open forest and woods margins, infrequent; general Quebec/Manitoba to Florida/Texas. Flowering April–June. Fig.50 p.105: inflorescence, glumes, spikelet from source, no republication clearance.

## 7. Festuca paradoxa Desv. — p.106
Historical citation Opusc. 105 (1831), CLUSTER FESCUE.
Generally similar to *F. obtusa*, but spikelets more clustered at branch tips and lemmas often broader, more blunt at apex. Chromosome number not reported. Gould Texas northern regions 1/3, open forests/woods borders, infrequent; general Pennsylvania/Wisconsin to Georgia/Mississippi/Texas. Flowering April–June. Gould says *F. paradoxa* is close to and possibly not specifically distinct from the more common *F. obtusa*. **Do not impose artificial sharp separation from this short account.**

## 23. LOLIUM L. — genus p.106
Annuals/short-lived perennials with usually succulent culms and flat/folded blades. Ligules membranous. Blades often with thin membranous auricles. Inflorescence a spike of several-flowered spikelets, solitary and oriented edgewise at nodes of continuous rachis. Disarticulation above glumes and between florets. First glume absent except at terminal spikelet; second glume usually large, broad, several-nerved, awnless. Lemmas 5–9-nerved, rounded on back, awnless or awned from usually broad apex. Historical genus around dozen species Europe/Asia.

### Lolium key — p.106
- Glume shorter than spikelet, long-lived annual/short-lived perennial => 1. *Lolium perenne*.
- Glume exceeding uppermost floret, short-lived annual => 2. *Lolium multiflorum*.
Keep literal source scope; later accounts and modern synonyms require follow-up.

## 1. Lolium perenne L. — pp.106–107
Historical citation Sp. Pl. 83 (1753), RYEGRASS, Fig.51 p.107.
Gould: annual or short-lived perennial without rhizomes/stolons. Culms 25–70 cm, glabrous, relatively thick and succulent when green. Leaves glabrous/scabrous, dark green. Sheaths often with distinct membranous auricles. Upper margins of sheath thin/hyaline, merging at blade base to form membranous ligule. Blades 2–10 mm broad, shrivelling/twisting to become yellowish and dry. Spike usually 10–20 cm with 15–30 appressed or somewhat spreading spikelets borne singly at nodes. Spikelets mostly 5–12-flowered, oriented edgewise to rachis; first glume absent except terminal spikelet. Glumes mostly 5–10 mm long, one-third to two-thirds as long as spikelet, 3–7-nerved. Lemmas about as long as glumes, 5-nerved, obtuse/obscurely pointed at margins/apex, awnless or with awn <=1–2 mm. Palea about as long, scabrous or short ciliate on nerves. Chromosome number 2n=14.
Gould historic Texas all ten regions but apparently absent south South Texas Plains; occasional seeded pastures/cool season lawn grass; common road margins/ditch borders/disturbed sites. European species introduced widely to USA, sometimes common weed. Flowering March–June.
Gould notes *L. perenne* historically first cultivated pasture grass in Europe; very close and perhaps not specifically distinct from *L. multiflorum*, according to Hitchcock (1951), and discusses variations in culms/spikelets and possible Texas intergrades/hybrids. Preserve uncertainty without synonymizing in Lab.
