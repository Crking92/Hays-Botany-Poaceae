## Genus Eragrostis von Wolf — p.177
Annual or perennial bunch grasses. Sheaths free on margins. Ligules ciliate. Spikelets in open or contracted, sometimes spicate panicles, 2–many-flowered and laterally compressed. Disarticulation above glumes, at rachilla and/or palea, sometimes persistent. Glumes usually hyaline, one-nerved, deciduous. Lemmas usually membranous, three-nerved, with nerves sometimes inconspicuous. Paleas commonly hyaline, shorter than lemmas, two-keeled, usually ciliolate on keels, longitudinally bowed out by mature grain. Grains lenticular or subellipsoid tapering to apex, or cylindrical and tending to be truncate at both ends; generally somewhat laterally compressed, sometimes grooved adaxially, surface smooth to reticulate, typically reddish brown and translucent. Basic chromosome number x=10. Historical context: Gould warns that some *Eragrostis* species introduced for pasture/erosion control may establish along roadsides and rail yards and thus not necessarily belong to the native flora.

## Hierarchical key — pp.177–180
**Important:** Each statement is a conditional key branch, not automatically a verified trait assigned to every species named at its end. The key is dense; the following outline preserves useful diagnostic pathways and must be checked against exact printed couplets before code integration.

### Annual branch
- Plants annual, without innovations/buds in basal sheaths:
  - Paleas ciliate on keels, cilia 0.4–0.8 mm => 18. *E. ciliaris* (p.177).
  - Paleas scabrous/ciliolate on keels, cilia <0.1 mm:
    - Prostrate plants rooting at nodes, sheaths with ring of very short hairs at base => 19. *E. hypnoides*.
    - Culms sometimes decumbent at base but not prostrate/rooting, sheaths glabrous at base: small 2–3 mm spikelets with 2–5 florets and panicles nearly two-thirds length of culm => 20. *E. capillaris*.
    - Other panicle/spikelet shapes continue p.178: linear spikelets nearly as thick as broad => 25. *E. barrelieri* (printed epithet check); ovate/lanceolate spikelets with glandular pits on glume/lemma keels => 24. *E. cilianensis*; those lacking pits => 21. *E. mexicana*.
    - For spikelets narrowly ovate/linear with small width and often verticillate lower branches: lower branches capillary, spikelets usually <1 mm wide => 23. *E. pilosa*; lower branches solitary or bunched, not capillary, spikelets >1 mm => 22. *E. diffusa*.

### Perennial branch, early paths
- Plants perennial with innovations (with or without basal-sheath buds).
- Panicles dense/spicate => 2. *E. spicata*.
- Panicles open or dense but not spicate:
  - Spikelets sessile and panicle branches simple => 1. *E. sessilispica*.
  - Spikelets pedicelled on branches at least sometimes divided:
    - Branches densely flowered, spikelets in imbricate groups => 14. *E. secundiflora*.
    - Loosely flowered branches with a knotty rhizomatous base: plant viscid, including culms/sheaths/blades and upper branches => 16. *E. silveana*; not viscid => 15. *E. spectabilis*.
    - Plants lacking knotty rhizomatous base proceed through numerous further key branches pp.178–180, including the species below.

### Additional named key terminals pp.179–180
- *E. curtipedicellata*: spikelets on pedicels appressed to stiffly spreading branchlets (p.179).
- *E. refracta*: spikelets on spreading, strongly scabrous capillary branches; keyed spikelets 10–30 (mostly >15) flowered.
- *E. elliottii*: alternative long-pedicellate spikelets in the same branch, preserve full ancestor split.
- *E. swallenii*: plants with glandular ring below nodes and usually on pedicels.
- *E. curvula*: long attenuate blades arching toward ground, often >30 cm long and ~1 mm broad; panicle oblong long relative to width, branches similar length.
- *E. lehmanniana*: blades <30 cm, fine attenuate, often 2–10 cm; culms frequently branched lower nodes.
- *E. palmeri*: blades 20–35 cm, culms branching only at base; another branch of the above key, not universal.
- *E. trichodes*: panicles ovate, longest branches basal or below middle, taper distally; grain about 1 mm; rachilla persistent and lemmas reddish-tinged.
- *E. erosa*: grain about 1.6 mm; rachilla disarticulating; lemmas greenish/golden tips.
- *E. hirsuta*: lemmas ~1.5–2.4 mm; 2–6-flowered spikelets; blades 4–8 mm wide.
- *E. lugens*: 5–11-flowered spikelets, blades 1–3 mm, linear <=1 mm wide spikelets and 1.5–1.6 mm lemmas, grain without adaxial groove or only shallow; culm/branches often sharply flexuous.
- *E. intermedia*: spikelets ovate to narrowly lanceolate 1.6–1.8 mm wide, lemmas 1.8–2.2 (rarely 1.6) mm, grain with strong adaxial groove; lower panicle branches often in clusters of 2–3.

## 1. Eragrostis sessilispica Buckl. — pp.180–181
Historical citation Proc. Acad. Nat. Sci. Philadelphia Monogr. 1862:97 (1862). Common name TUMBLE LOVEGRASS.
- Perennial; culms tufted, 30–90 cm, with few innovations.
- Sheaths overlapping below, glabrous.
- Blades flat to involute, rigid glabrous, 10–30 cm long and 1–3 mm wide.
- Panicles ovate, open, few-flowered, 25–65 cm long and 10–25 cm wide, rigid rachis; branches distant, stiff, short, ascending/slender (as described); lower branches may spread and become distant as plant ages.
- Spikelets sessile, appressed, somewhat distant, oblong to rachilla, compressed, 1.2–5 mm long, 2–3 mm wide, 5–12-flowered; some plants with longer spikelets (read source prior to using exact dimensions because the photographed line is dense).
- Glumes persistent, acuminate, first ~2–6 mm, second ~3–6 mm.
- Lemmas indurate, barely imbricate, straw-colored to purplish-tinged, acuminate, 3–5 mm; paleas well-developed, persistent, greenish-hyaline, with fringed keels.
- Grains ellipsoid, tapering toward apex, ~1.25 mm long; chromosome number 2n=40.
- Gould historical Texas distribution southern region 2, western regions 4 & 5, eastern portions regions 6, 8 and 9, in sandy prairies; general SW Kansas/western Oklahoma/central Texas/northern Tamaulipas, Mexico.
- Flowering March–September.
- Fig.91 p.181 depicts inflorescence/spikelet from Gould & Box (1965); book figure not licensed for new public gallery.

## 2. Eragrostis spicata Vasey — p.181
Historical citation Bot. Gaz. 16:146 (1891). Common name SPICATE LOVEGRASS.
- Perennial; culms tufted, 30 cm tall, with firm innovations.
- Sheaths overlapping, glabrous.
- Blades glabrous, flat to involute, 20–30 cm long, 4–8 mm wide.
- Panicles dense, narrow, spicate, 30–35 cm long, 4–5 mm wide; branches appressed, densely flowered, about 15–(20) mm long; spikelets subsessile, ovate, compressed, 1.5–2 mm long, 2–3-flowered, rachilla disarticulating.
- Glumes hyaline or pale, obtuse, persistent, first slightly shorter than second; lemmas greenish-white, ovate, somewhat obtuse, 3-nerved, about 0.5 mm wide, lateral nerves evident.
- Paleas hyaline, ciliate on keels; grains narrowly ellipsoidal, ~0.85 mm long. Chromosome 2n=40.
