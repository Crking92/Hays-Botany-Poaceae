## *Steinchisma hians*

**Reference concept:** Panicum hians Elliott (older name; Steinchisma subgenus); DIRECT_OR_HISTORIC_TREATMENT.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **habit:** Perennial with upright or decumbent bases; typical flowering stems about 20–75 cm. *1999 regional Flora / Kew GrassBase*. Needed: rooted base.
- **ligule:** Very short membranous ligule near 0.5 mm, its rim glabrous to finely ciliolate depending on source. *1999 regional Flora / Kew GrassBase*. Needed: inner ligule at mm scale.
- **panicle:** Open or partially contracted panicle about 6–20 cm; lowest 1–3 cm of some branches lacks spikelets. *1999 regional Flora*. Needed: complete panicle with branch bases.
- **spikelet:** Glabrous, approximately 1.8–2.6 mm, at maturity its tip characteristically gapes. *1999 regional Flora*. Needed: mature spikelet side view.
- **lower glume:** Both glumes present; the lower generally about one-third to half the spikelet length. *1999 regional Flora*. Needed: isolated unequal glumes.
- **neuter palea:** Inflated broadly obovate palea of lower sterile floret may protrude above its lemma; major gaping diagnostic. *1999 regional Flora*. Needed: lower-floret palea dissection.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Loose branched panicle with small drooping florets over lanceolate leaf blades, some contextual rocky ground photographs.

**Previously unresolved:** Only three images and no spikelet closeup, lemma/membrane characters or base; no independent confirmation versus related Panicum/Steinchisma.

**Look-alikes:** Other Steinchisma/Panicum: Panicum dichotomiflorum and Panicum repens; don't confuse gaping palea with glume margins

**Next diagnostic task:** Get a same-plant ripe spikelet dissection that clearly shows inflated lower-floret palea, lower glume ratio and truly gaping apex, along with collar and base.

**Evidence hold:** Original 3 Hays source photos lack close spikelets; species ranking remains unverified.

**Sources:** Flora of North Central Texas, p. 222 (file page), printed p. 1298 · [Online treatment](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:243676-2/general-information)

## *Dichanthelium acuminatum*

**Reference concept:** Panicum acuminatum Sw. (historic broader concept with three varieties); DIRECT_OR_HISTORIC_TREATMENT.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **seasonal rosette:** Perennial develops a leafy basal winter rosette, spring flowering culms and smaller crowded autumn branches. *USFS FEIS Dichanthelium acuminatum*. Needed: winter rosette and spring flowering stem.
- **early inflorescence:** Spring culms tend to be less branched, bearing a more open terminal panicle. *USFS FEIS*. Needed: spring whole plant.
- **late inflorescence:** Later in season stems branch repeatedly into shorter leaves and reduced or partly sheathed panicles. *USFS FEIS*. Needed: autumn branching.
- **blade width:** Old broad Panicum acuminatum concept records blades mostly 3–12 mm wide. *1999 regional Flora*. Needed: blade width with scale.
- **sheath hairs:** 1999 broad-key var. acuminatum has spreading hairs about 2.5–4 mm; historic var. lindheimeri may have fewer and more ascending hairs. *1999 regional Flora (variety-conditional)*. Needed: sheath and node close-up.
- **spikelet variation:** Historic Flora concept groups spikelets about 1–2.7 mm depending on variety; single measurement cannot prove modern species boundary. *1999 regional Flora (broad concept)*. Needed: several vernal mature spikelets.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Single leafy groundcover patch with narrow lanceolate leaves in shaded/rocky context.

**Previously unresolved:** No discernible flowering panicle, spikelets, stem/leaf pubescence, winter rosette or sheath details; complex D. acuminatum taxon concept.

**Look-alikes:** Dichanthelium lindheimeri/villosissimum and other members of the acuminatum complex

**Next diagnostic task:** Find a same-location winter rosette, vernal flowering specimen and hairs on sheath/node with mm scale; use a current species/variety concept and mature vernal spikelet measures.

**Evidence hold:** Historic var. villosum and lindheimeri combinations may have modern separate placements; do not auto-transfer their traits to a narrowly defined acuminatum. Single Hays photo is vegetative and does not resolve species.

**Sources:** Flora of North Central Texas PDF p. 219 printed p.1295 · [Online treatment](https://research.fs.usda.gov/feis/species-reviews/dicacu) · [Online treatment](https://auth1.dpr.ncparks.gov/flora/species_account.php?id=3763)

- **glume:** Lower glume short, around one-quarter spikelet length; upper nearly full length and several-veined. *Kew GrassBase*. Needed: isolated upper and lower glumes.

## *Panicum repens*

**Reference concept:** Not treated as named species in 1999 regional Flora; NOT_DIRECTLY_TREATED_USE_MODERN_SOURCES.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **habit:** Perennial spreads by long branching rhizomes in wet or disturbed ground; forms dense colonies. *University of Florida IFAS / USFS FEIS*. Needed: dug rooted colony.
- **rhizomes:** Tough creeping underground stems often end in sharply pointed growing tips—the torpedo feature. *University of Florida IFAS / USFS FEIS*. Needed: intact rhizome terminal bud.
- **foliage:** Flat to folded narrow blue-green leaves, sometimes waxy or pale. *USFS FEIS*. Needed: leaf blade underside.
- **ligule:** Short ciliate membrane generally around 0.5–1 mm. *University of Florida IFAS*. Needed: inner collar.
- **panicle:** Open terminal panicle with ascending branches, often roughly 7–22 cm long. *USFS FEIS*. Needed: whole panicle.
- **spikelet:** Small ovate, unawned spikelets approximately 2–3 mm long. *USFS FEIS*. Needed: mature spikelet with mm scale.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Dense green grassy mat with some thin flowering branches visible only at distance.

**Previously unresolved:** Rhizome/stolon structures and diagnostic spikelets absent; one image cannot resolve torpedograss.

**Look-alikes:** Panicum dichotomiflorum, Panicum virgatum, other rhizomatous wetland grasses

**Next diagnostic task:** Critical target: excavate an intact sharp-pointed scaly rhizome connected to flowering stem and document spikelet size and ligule; distinguish from shallow turf runners.

## *Pappophorum bicolor*

**Reference concept:** Not covered as a named species in 1999 regional Flora; NOT_DIRECTLY_TREATED_USE_MODERN_SOURCES.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **habit:** Warm-season perennial tufted bunchgrass; flowering stems commonly 30–100 cm in source treatments. *Texas A&M Texas Rangelands / Kew GrassBase*. Needed: rooted tussock.
- **panicle:** Usually narrow pink to purplish flowering panicle, but head color is not diagnostic alone. *Texas A&M / Kew GrassBase*. Needed: flower head.
- **spikelet:** Spikelet has about 3–5 florets, generally lower 2–3 bisexual and upper florets reduced. *Texas A&M / Flora of North America*. Needed: dissected spikelet count.
- **awn crown:** Each mature lemma terminates in approximately 11–15 separate awns, often unequal in length. *Kew GrassBase / Texas A&M*. Needed: isolated lemma awn count.
- **lemma:** Lemma body about 3–4 mm with hairy lower margins and midvein; spikelets attached by slender pedicels. *Kew GrassBase*. Needed: lemma both faces at scale.
- **collar:** Ligule a short ring or tuft of hairs rather than a prominent membrane. *Texas A&M Texas Rangelands*. Needed: true collar.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Dry grassland tuft with long hairy feathered flowering heads and good separate head details.

**Previously unresolved:** Individual lemma appendage number and attachment details not counted.

**Look-alikes:** Pappophorum mucronulatum and other pappusgrasses; generic fuzzy seedheads do not prove bicolor

**Next diagnostic task:** Photograph one ripe detached lemma at microscope scale and count each awn, then count fertile florets and compare panicle branch density against Pappophorum mucronulatum.

**Evidence hold:** Source Hays photos show feathery pink heads but not 11–15 awns counted from one lemma. Taxon name is candidate until dissection.

## *Stapfochloa ciliata*

**Reference concept:** Not directly treated as named species in 1999 Flora; historical Chloris ciliata; NOT_DIRECTLY_TREATED_USE_MODERN_SOURCES.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **habit:** Tufted perennial, typically roughly 25–60 cm tall. *Kew POWO GrassBase (Chloris ciliata)*. Needed: rooted base.
- **ligule:** Fringe of hairs or almost absent, not long solid membranous flap. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: leaf collar.
- **racemes:** Several digitate or spreading one-sided racemes, normally 3–5(–7), roughly 3.5–6(–8) cm long. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: whole head and raceme count.
- **spikelet:** Strongly laterally compressed spikelet 1.8–2.8 mm long; one fertile basal floret with two upper sterile florets. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: isolated spikelet.
- **lemma hairs:** Fertile lemma has conspicuous hairs along middle nerve and margins, around 0.5–1.5 mm. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: fertile lemma face and edge.
- **awns:** Fertile lemma has short subapical awn about 0.9–2.7 mm; first sterile lemma often also awned. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: fertile and sterile lemmas side-by-side.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Clear several digitate/fingerlike and strongly curved flowering branches, plus scaled rachis and collar region; good diagnostic imagery.

**Previously unresolved:** Exact hairy structures along rachis, sterile/terminal florets and ligule details still need close confirmation; other Chloris/Stapfochloa types occur locally.

**Look-alikes:** Stapfochloa canterae, Stapfochloa elata, Chloris verticillata and related fingergrasses

**Next diagnostic task:** Dissect a single spikelet to expose one fertile plus two sterile florets, measure lemma hairs and awns, and pair with scale of branch count and hairy collar; compare directly against canterae.

**Evidence hold:** Genus-level windmill arrangement is shared by several local species. Most critically, modern Stapfochloa differs from some Chloris by fully ciliate lemma margins; exact species characters require formal matched comparison.

## *Stapfochloa canterae*

**Reference concept:** Not directly treated as named species in 1999 Flora; historical Chloris canterae; NOT_DIRECTLY_TREATED_USE_MODERN_SOURCES.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **taxonomy:** Kew accepts Stapfochloa canterae, formerly Chloris canterae, as a distinct species. *Kew POWO*. Needed: taxonomy provenance.
- **perennial:** Perennial tufted grass originally native to southeastern South America, introduced to Texas. *Kew POWO*. Needed: rooted adult plant.
- **head shape:** Racemes commonly digitately or subdigitately clustered, sometimes somewhat pendent or curved. *Kew POWO / field guide of Chaco (habit level)*. Needed: whole flowering head.
- **genus floral structure:** Stapfochloa generally has one fertile basal floret and two to three reduced sterile florets above it. *Sousa and Santos 2022 genus concept, not species-exclusive*. Needed: dissected floret stack.
- **genus hairy lemmas:** Stapfochloa fertile lemmas have densely ciliate longitudinal margins, unlike many Chloris species. *Sousa and Santos 2022 genus concept, not species-exclusive*. Needed: flattened lemma edge.
- **species differential status:** Verified published modern key with exact canterae-versus-ciliata sterile floret and awn measurements NOT YET VERIFIED in this packet. *Evidence gap – no asserted numeric diagnosis*. Needed: paired matched microscope comparison.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Very useful image set: ciliate racemes, culm node/ligule, detached spikelets and metric ruler in frame.

**Previously unresolved:** Precise fertile/sterile floret/awn comparison against Stapfochloa ciliata needs formal measurement.

**Look-alikes:** Stapfochloa ciliata, Stapfochloa elata, Chloris verticillata

**Next diagnostic task:** Use already saved Hays mm-ruler photo to measure 1 fertile + sterile floret stack and awn insertion; find modern FNA or treatment explicitly contrasting canterae and ciliata before publishing separate key states.

**Evidence hold:** Kew accepted species status and Texas introduction do NOT constitute a verified Hays observation or a differential key. The reported canterae Hays photo is strong but no source-photo pixels reexamined now.

## *Megathyrsus maximus*

**Reference concept:** Not directly treated by 1999 regional Flora; historical Panicum maximum; NOT_DIRECTLY_TREATED_USE_MODERN_SOURCES.

**Source-described character/photo targets (not validated on Hays source specimens):**
- **habit:** Generally robust tufted perennial, sometimes short-rhizomatous, often 75–200 cm or taller. *Kew POWO GrassBase (under Panicum maximum)*. Needed: whole rooted crown.
- **ligule:** Ciliate membrane at the blade–sheath junction. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: inner collar macro.
- **leaf:** Broad linear to lanceolate blades, often 12–40 cm long, about 4–35 mm wide across variable forms. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: leaf with ruler.
- **panicle:** Large open oblong or pyramidal panicle roughly 12–45 cm, lower primary branches sometimes whorled. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: complete panicle with scale.
- **sheath:** Basal sheaths can be pubescent, culm nodes may be glabrous or bearded depending on form. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: basal sheath and node.
- **reproduction:** Panicle has individual pedicelled spikelets, not a dense solitary finger-shaped flowering branch. *Kew POWO GrassBase*. Needed: isolated spikelets and rachis.

**Previously visible on Hays source photos:** Bunch of tall green grass and diffuse panicle visible only from a distance.

**Previously unresolved:** Images lack spikelet close-ups, ligule/collar, and any convincing wild establishment evidence; one is nighttime landscaping.

**Look-alikes:** Urochloa maxima historical labels, Sorghum halepense, Panicum virgatum and similar tall introduced pasture grasses

**Next diagnostic task:** Verify actual same-plant species from a ripe spikelet and inner collar, then establish whether Hays plants are landscape-planted or producing a self-sustaining population.

**Evidence hold:** Previous imagery is only tall landscape grass and distant open panicles, including nighttime lawn context; species ID and wild status unproven.
