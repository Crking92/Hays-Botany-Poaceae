## Source-backed field characters and unresolved proof

### *Phyllostachys aurea*

**References:** Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.235 (printed p.1311).

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Woody rhizomatous perennial forming spreading colonies with culms approximately 2–8 m tall. **Needed:** complete culm and basal rhizome/rooting colony.
- **culm:** Glabrous green to yellowish culms, flattened on one side immediately above the branch-bearing node. **Needed:** close view of culm node, branch base and flat/grooved internode.
- **branches:** Usually two, occasionally three, branches arise per node, with one markedly larger than the other. **Needed:** same node with all branches and a scale.
- **foliage:** Leaf blades narrowly lanceolate, usually 5–12 cm long and 1–2 cm broad, short petiole about 3–5 mm. **Needed:** full blade with ruler and petiole junction.
- **sheath apex:** Leaf sheaths mostly smooth, with two small clusters of bristles at sheath apex. **Needed:** close view of both sheath-apex bristle tufts.
- **flowering:** Flowers rarely observed locally; flowering cannot be a required field identification character. **Needed:** vegetative culm-node comparison rather than mandatory flower.

**Comparison group:** Phyllostachys congeners; Arundinaria gigantea.

**Hold:** Cultivated running bamboo can spread into vacant land; a Hays observation does not establish naturally established population. Species-level culm-node views required..

**Previous Hays review:** Woody running bamboo thicket, narrow lanceolate leaves and node branching, multiple culms; compatible with Phyllostachys..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Cortaderia selloana*

**References:** Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.182 (printed p.1258).

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Large dense perennial tuft to about 3 m tall and at least 1 m wide. **Needed:** whole plant and basal clump with scale.
- **sexual system:** Plants described as dioecious, with male and female flowers on different individuals. **Needed:** document separately collected staminate and pistillate material.
- **leaf:** Long mostly basal blades roughly 0.6–1 m or longer, with abrasive rough margins. **Needed:** long leaf and close view of edge.
- **ligule:** Ligule consists of dense hairs approximately 3–5 mm long. **Needed:** inside blade-sheath junction photographed with millimeter scale.
- **panicle:** Broad conspicuous silvery, feathery panicle about 25–100 cm long. **Needed:** entire uncut flowering head with height reference.
- **spikelet:** Spikelets have about two to three florets. **Needed:** dissected ripe spikelet showing florets.
- **lemma hairs:** Female (pistillate) lemmas have long silky hairs; male (staminate) lemmas are comparatively glabrous. **Needed:** sex-known flower dissection, not a fluffy panicle alone.

**Comparison group:** Miscanthus sinensis; Cenchrus setaceus; Arundo donax.

**Hold:** Cultivated ornamental may persist for years; verify wild escape independently; sexual floral differences matter..

**Previous Hays review:** Some tall, silvery plume-bearing ornamental clumps look consistent with pampas grass; narrow, strap-like leaves in tuft..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Panicum miliaceum*

**References:** Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.224 (printed p.1300).

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Coarse annual grass, approximately 20–100 cm tall in the regional account. **Needed:** whole rooted mature plant and disturbed-site context.
- **sheath hairs:** Leaf sheaths carry long hairs arising from papilla-like swollen bases. **Needed:** sharp macro of individual long hairs and their basal swellings.
- **blade hairs:** Leaf blades range from visibly pubescent to nearly glabrous. **Needed:** both surfaces of several blades from one plant.
- **ligule:** Blade–sheath junction bears a fringed membrane about 1–3 mm long including hairs. **Needed:** inner ligule photographed with millimeter scale.
- **spikelet shape:** Mature spikelets are plump and broadly oval to elliptic. **Needed:** mature detached spikelet in face/profile with ruler.

**Comparison group:** Panicum capillare; Echinochloa spp.; cultivated millet forms.

**Hold:** Crop, bird-seed or cover-crop escape is possible; do not infer reproducing wild population from scattered plants..

**Previous Hays review:** Green grain-bearing inflorescence, coarse grass-like blades and stem closeups visible; a separate full plant photographed in disturbed ground..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Muhlenbergia capillaris*

**References:** Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.212 (printed p.1288).

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Tightly clumped perennial with upright flowering culms typically 60–100 cm tall. **Needed:** whole rooted basal tuft and flowering culm.
- **panicle shape:** Open, diffuse spreading panicle to roughly 35–40 cm long and 8–20 cm across. **Needed:** whole flowering head at maturity.
- **pedicels:** Fine hairlike branches and pedicels spread broadly at maturity. **Needed:** branch node and pedicel close-up.
- **lemma length:** Lemmas mostly 3.5–4.5 mm long, sometimes approaching 5 mm. **Needed:** detached mature lemma beside ruler.
- **lemma surface:** Lemma slightly roughened and with sparse hairs near base. **Needed:** lemma base under hand lens or macro.
- **awn length:** Lemma awn length varies, roughly 2–15 mm in the regional account. **Needed:** several mature awned lemmas from one head with scale.

**Comparison group:** Muhlenbergia reverchonii; Muhlenbergia lindheimeri; cultivated muhly forms.

**Hold:** An airy pink plume on a Hays ornamental is not sufficient for species ID or local nativity; compare scaled florets..

**Previous Hays review:** One airy purple-pink muhly plume over dense basal tuft..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Melinis repens*

**References:** Flora of North America (J.K. Wipff), Melinis repens via Open Herbarium https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?tid=239884; Kew POWO Melinis repens https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:2945817-1/general-information.

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **life cycle:** Annual or short-lived perennial; culms can be decumbent and root at lower nodes. **Needed:** rooted base and several nodes along culm.
- **stem:** Nodes often pubescent; sheaths may have papilla-based hairs. **Needed:** sheath and stem node macro.
- **ligule:** Ligule is a fringe of hairs, approximately 0.7–2.2 mm in the FNA account. **Needed:** inside blade–sheath junction with ruler.
- **head:** Open soft-looking often pink or silvery-pink to purple panicle; appearance varies with maturity. **Needed:** whole panicle plus growth-stage context.
- **spikelet:** Spikelets roughly 2–5.7 mm long in the FNA description. **Needed:** detached ripe spikelet with ruler.
- **hair source:** Long silky hairs associated with spikelets/callus, rather than lemma awns alone, create the fuzzy plume. **Needed:** spikelet separated from surrounding hairs to show their actual attachment.

**Comparison group:** Tricholaena/other Melinis; Muhlenbergia capillaris; Cenchrus setaceus.

**Hold:** Only historical Hays photo descriptions are reused; fine spikelets remain unverified and pink plumes alone are not decisive..

**Previous Hays review:** Characteristic pink fuzzy open plume-like seedheads and clumped foliage visible; strong general photo evidence..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Nassella tenuissima*

**References:** Flora of North America (M.E. Barkworth), Nassella tenuissima via Open Herbarium https://openherbarium.org/portal/taxa/index.php?tid=630068; Kew POWO Nassella tenuissima https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:281184-2/general-information; Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.214 treats Nassella genus and N. leucotricha, not N. tenuissima.

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Dense tuft-forming perennial, not rhizomatous. **Needed:** whole rooted crown and surrounding soil.
- **leaf width:** Blades extremely fine, typically rolled or folded, about 0.2–1.5 mm wide under FNA concept. **Needed:** blade cross-section or spread blade and millimeter ruler.
- **ligule:** Small smooth membranous ligule about 1–5 mm; FNA calls tip acute while GrassBase describes obtuse, so avoid binary tip-state scoring. **Needed:** inner ligule with scale on several shoots.
- **panicle:** Narrow to somewhat loose panicles 8–50 cm long, sometimes partially enclosed at maturity. **Needed:** intact panicle with sheath position.
- **glume:** Narrow glumes about 5–13 mm long surrounding much smaller floret. **Needed:** separated glumes and floret with scale.
- **awn:** One very long slender lemma awn about 45–100 mm, often bent twice. **Needed:** complete awn from base to tip with scale.
- **lemma crown:** Fertile lemma small with characteristic crown/rim and short hairs near awn insertion. **Needed:** dissected lemma tip under high-resolution macro.

**Comparison group:** Nassella leucotricha; Aristida spp.; cultivated fine-leaf grasses.

**Hold:** This species is not directly treated in the user's 1999 excerpt; Flora of North America and GrassBase disagree slightly on exact ligule tip shape. Hays ornamental source context is unresolved..

**Previous Hays review:** Fine threadlike foliage and flowing pale feathery seedheads above tufted base; compatible with Mexican feathergrass..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Cenchrus setaceus*

**References:** Flora of North America, Pennisetum setaceum via Burke Herbarium https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Pennisetum+setaceum; Kew POWO Cenchrus setaceus https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:77106046-1/general-information; Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.147 only notes cultivated Pennisetum species, not this species treatment.

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Densely tufted perennial with erect stems approximately 40–150 cm tall in FNA description. **Needed:** complete rooted plant and basal crown.
- **leaf:** Very narrow often rolled/folded roughened leaves, mostly around 2–3.5 mm across in FNA. **Needed:** leaf expanded with ruler and midrib view.
- **ligule:** Short hair-fringe ligule roughly 0.5–1.1 mm under historical Pennisetum concept. **Needed:** inner collar close-up with millimeter scale.
- **head:** Dense cylindrical brushlike flowering panicle, commonly pink to burgundy, around 8–32 cm in FNA description. **Needed:** whole mature inflorescence with scale.
- **bristles:** Spikelets grouped in fascicles with many sterile involucral bristles, some conspicuously longer and feathery; bristles are NOT lemma awns. **Needed:** detach a complete fascicle to show bristle attachment.
- **spikelets:** Usually one to four spikelets per fascicle, each about 4.5–7 mm. **Needed:** intact and dissected fascicle with millimeter ruler.

**Comparison group:** Cenchrus alopecuroides; Cenchrus longisetus; Cenchrus ciliaris.

**Previous Hays review:** Original sample not available for image review.

### *Lolium × boucheanum*

**References:** Kew POWO Lolium × boucheanum https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:407425-1; EPPO Lolium × boucheanum https://gd.eppo.int/taxon/LOLBO; Maity et al., Weed Science Texas ryegrass diversity https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/weed-science/article/species-identification-and-morphological-trait-diversity-assessment-in-ryegrass-lolium-spp-populations-from-the-texas-blackland-prairies/5A683DE517E45D8834C4CCF2A3EA16D8; Flora of North America Lolium perenne excerpt via Burke Herbarium https://burkeherbarium.org/waflora/checklist.php?Taxon=Lolium+perenne.

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **generic spike:** Compare a mature Lolium terminal spike with lateral multileaved spikelets; this is genus-level context, not proof of hybrid. **Needed:** complete mature spike and rachis with ruler.
- **lemma awns:** Awn expression can vary from unawned to awned among the relevant Lolium parent taxa and hybrids; do not force one state for this named hybrid. **Needed:** several florets from multiple spikelets photographed with scale.
- **floret number:** Count mature florets per spikelet and compare with parental populations; character ranges overlap and cannot uniquely diagnose hybrid. **Needed:** two or more mature spikelets dissected and counted.
- **leaf bud:** Document young leaf rolling versus folding and basal vegetative shoots to test resemblance to the putative parents; not decisive independently. **Needed:** new shoot cross-section and basal shoots.
- **hybrid status:** Hybrid identity remains an evidence/provenance question; a typical intermediate morphology cannot establish lineage on its own. **Needed:** voucher, documented seed pedigree or specialist assessment; genetic evidence when necessary.

**Comparison group:** Lolium perenne; Lolium multiflorum; Lolium rigidum.

**Hold:** Critical hybrid-origin conflict: Kew POWO says L. perenne × L. rigidum, while EPPO and agronomic usage say L. multiflorum × L. perenne. Morphology overlaps and can backcross; no Hays source images accessible and no photo-only hybrid confirmation authorized..

**Previous Hays review:** Original sample not available for image review.

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

### *Miscanthus sinensis*

**References:** Diggs, Lipscomb & O'Kennon (1999), Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas, user-supplied monocots excerpt, excerpt PDF p.208 (printed p.1284).

**Reference traits to investigate on one Hays plant:**

- **habit:** Dense clump-forming perennial 1–3 m or taller with arching to upright stems. **Needed:** whole plant and clump base in context.
- **leaf:** Blades on basal shoots and up the stem, sometimes variegated; margins very rough and abrasive. **Needed:** leaf surface, rough edge and multiple nodes.
- **ligule:** Ciliate membrane about 1.5–3 mm long. **Needed:** inside collar with ruler.
- **panicle:** Dense terminal panicle about 10–35 cm long; branches ascending. **Needed:** whole mature plume without cropping.
- **spikelet pairs:** Spikelets in unlike-pedicel-length pairs: one short-stalked and one long-stalked; both spikelets otherwise similar. **Needed:** same intact inflorescence branch with pair and scale.
- **spikelet hairs:** Spikelets roughly 4–5.5 mm with basal glume hairs slightly longer than spikelet. **Needed:** detached paired spikelets showing hair bases.
- **lemma awn:** Lemma may carry a twisted bent awn approximately 5–10 mm long. **Needed:** dissected lemma and complete awn with millimeter scale.

**Comparison group:** Cortaderia selloana; Arundo donax; Saccharum spp..

**Hold:** Cultivated variegated ornamental may persist without escaping; verify non-planted populations separately..

**Previous Hays review:** Clumped tall arching narrow leaves in an ornamental-looking garden setting..

**Current stage:** reference-only; specimen-level verification, rights, local context and public scoring remain withheld.

## Important taxon conflict: *Lolium × boucheanum*

Kew's accepted-name entry lists **L. perenne × L. rigidum**; EPPO lists **L. multiflorum × L. perenne**, a concept widely used in forage science. Do not silently equate these incompatible parentage accounts and do not identify the hybrid from intermediate photo morphology alone. Preserve the candidate but do not make it a confident key endpoint.
