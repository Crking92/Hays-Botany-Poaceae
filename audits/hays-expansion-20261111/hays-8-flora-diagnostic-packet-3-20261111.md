## *Bromus catharticus*

**Source applicability:** Direct 1999 Flora species and brome key.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **habit:** Winter annual 10–70 cm in flower; green growth may start late in fall. *Flora 1999*. Photo: rooted habit.
- **spikelet:** Strongly flattened spikelet; glumes and lemmas sharply keeled. *Flora 1999*. Photo: spikelet profile.
- **lemma:** Lower lemma about 2.5–3.2 mm wide from keel to margin. *Flora 1999*. Photo: scaled lemma.
- **awn:** Lemmas awnless or with short awn about 1–3 mm. *Flora 1999*. Photo: awn closeup.
- **teeth:** Minute lemma-apex teeth around 0.1–0.3 mm. *Flora 1999*. Photo: dissected apex.
- **sheath:** Leaf sheath with spreading pilose hairs; spikelets hairless. *Flora 1999*. Photo: sheath macro.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Open to somewhat loose grass panicle with flattened-looking elongated hanging spikelets; green grassy stand and culm close-ups visible.

**Unresolved:** Sharp keel, exact awn lengths, lemma teeth/veins and collar/ligule not resolved; cannot rule out lookalike Bromus from gross view alone.

**Look-alikes:** Bromus hordeaceus, B. japonicus, B. secalinus. **Exact follow-up:** A complete mature spikelet, keeled glumes/lemmas and tooth/awn measurements, sheath hairs and collar on the same Hays specimen.

**Original observations:** [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/379499753) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/366862129) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/366673949) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/358472027)

## *Eragrostis superba*

**Source applicability:** Direct 1999 Flora species; FNA/Kew additional reproductive traits.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **habit:** Tufted perennial; can be planted for range improvement. *Flora 1999*. Photo: rooted base.
- **spikelet:** Broad, strongly compressed and showy, around 3–10 mm wide. *Flora 1999*. Photo: spikelet profile.
- **lemma:** Lemma has conspicuous keel. *Flora 1999*. Photo: lemma macro.
- **florets:** Multiflowered spikelet, roughly 4–22 florets in FNA. *FNA via NEON species treatment*. Photo: floret count.
- **disarticulation:** Mature spikelet may detach whole below the glumes. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: ripe spikelet.
- **palea:** Palea keels broadly winged, a crucial tiny structural character. *FNA via NEON species treatment*. Photo: palea dissection.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Tufted dry to green bunchgrass; rather large flattened light tan spikelets on branched stems, with some hand-scaled seedhead views.

**Unresolved:** Detached florets, glume and lemma veins/hair characters not resolved; introduced planting versus established escape not confirmed.

**Look-alikes:** Eragrostis intermedia, E. spectabilis, E. curvula. **Exact follow-up:** Ripe flattened spikelet on mm scale, isolated lemma and winged palea, true ligule, basal crown and evidence of unplanted reproduction.

**Original observations:** [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/396903239) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/394713386) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/359405762) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/354818424)

## *Lolium perenne*

**Source applicability:** 1999 Flora treats multiflorum as subspecies, while modern Kew recognizes two species.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **head:** Single upright two-ranked axis bearing solitary edge-on spikelets. *Flora 1999 / Kew POWO*. Photo: whole spike.
- **glume:** Glume shorter than total spikelet, often 5–10 mm in old regional key. *Flora 1999*. Photo: measured glume.
- **awn:** Typical L. perenne lemma awnless; hybrids and rare variants complicate simple splits. *Flora 1999 / Kew POWO*. Photo: ripe lemmas.
- **auricle:** Shorter, relatively rounded auricles compared with older multiflorum concept. *Flora 1999*. Photo: leaf base.
- **florets:** Typically 4–14 fertile florets per spikelet in Kew GrassBase account. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: dissected spikelet.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Unbranched ryegrass-like flowering spikes with spikelets edge-on along central rachis; a basal bunch and habitat views.

**Unresolved:** Awn presence/absence, floret and glume dimensions and discrimination from L. multiflorum or their hybrid not confidently resolved.

**Look-alikes:** Lolium multiflorum; Lolium × boucheanum hybrid. **Exact follow-up:** Same-plant auricles, 3 mature spikelets showing glume/lemma/awn/floret count, and seeded turf versus wild context.

**Original observations:** [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/365063951) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/360912372) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/354912942) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/354772388)

## *Bothriochloa longipaniculata*

**Source applicability:** Historical 1999 Flora LUMPS longipaniculata into torreyana; only group-level descriptions usable.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **paired_spikelets:** Sessile/pedicelled pairs and grooved internodes characterize Bothriochloa genus. *Flora 1999 genus only*. Photo: rachis and spikelet pair.
- **silvery_head:** Whitened or silvery branched seedhead belongs to broad historical silver-bluestem group. *Flora 1999 broad-group only*. Photo: scaled panicle.
- **group_spikelet:** Historic B. laguroides group key describes short sessile spikelets below 4.5 mm and awns usually 18 mm or shorter, NOT a modern longipaniculata diagnosis. *Flora 1999 broad-group only*. Photo: glume and awn scale.
- **old_habit:** Old torreyana treatment notes stems 50–130 cm and exserted flowering panicles; NOT species-exclusive. *Flora 1999 broad-group only*. Photo: whole habit.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Silvery narrow feathery panicle and upright grass culms; basal grassy tuft shown. Multiple close flowering head views are present.

**Unresolved:** No glume macro adequate to check narrow shiny green vs broad dull glumes; reliable panicle-length measurement unavailable; can't separate from B. torreyana on these views.

**Look-alikes:** Bothriochloa torreyana, B. barbinodis. **Exact follow-up:** Find true species-rank modern differential; compare same-scale panicle and detached sessile glumes between independently verified longipaniculata and torreyana.

**Original observations:** [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/392143264) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/377598342) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/364866470) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/353902207)

## *Bothriochloa bladhii*

**Source applicability:** Kew POWO FTEA/GrassBase description supplies species concepts; Flora genus key only.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **habit:** Tufted or shortly rhizomatous perennial with erect stems. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: rooted base.
- **panicle:** Numerous short racemes, around 2–5 cm, on a central branching axis. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: whole flowering head.
- **spikelets:** Sessile fertile and usually smaller pedicelled sterile spikelets occur as pairs. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: paired spikelets.
- **rachis:** Rachis internodes and pedicels flattened with a median translucent groove/line. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: rachis anatomy.
- **glume:** Sessile spikelets about 3–4 mm with usually hairy lower glume, sometimes pitted but pit variable. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: lower glume macro.
- **awn:** Geniculate awn often 10–25 mm across Kew source treatments. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: awn measure.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Several fingerlike burgundy-purple racemes and paired spikelets/awn-like projections; strong magnification at hand scale and an exposed collar area in one observation.

**Unresolved:** Distinctive paired-spikelet glumes, exact lemma awns, node collar anatomy and distinction from Dichanthium or KR bluestem unresolved.

**Look-alikes:** Bothriochloa ischaemum, Dichanthium aristatum, D. annulatum. **Exact follow-up:** Measure a matched sessile/pedicelled pair, glume pit/hair pattern, rachis median line, lemma awn, collar and root crown.

**Original observations:** [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/410398752) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/331046168) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/331046186) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/196764282)

## *Dichanthium aristatum*

**Source applicability:** 1999 Flora Dichanthium page is comparison-only; Kew POWO/PlantNET provide species treatment.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **habit:** Perennial with ascending stems about 20–100 cm. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: rooted base.
- **racemes:** One to six near-digitate racemes, mostly about 2–8 cm. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: whole flowering head.
- **base:** Raceme base can be bare but notably hairy; bare bases alone mislead a Bothriochloa separation. *NSW PlantNET*. Photo: raceme base.
- **pedicel:** Pedicel lacks Bothriochloa-type transparent median line. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: rachis/pedicel macro.
- **spikelet:** Sessile fertile spikelet about 2–5 mm and lower glume often hairy below middle. *Kew POWO/FTEA*. Photo: glume macro.
- **awn:** Fertile lemma often carries twisted/bent awn roughly 10–20 mm. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: lemma awn.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Two to several reddish spreading racemes with awns, some extremely close flowering axis views, a ligule/collar view and bunchgrass basal shoots.

**Unresolved:** The distinguishing pair of sessile and pedicellate spikelets, nerve and awn anatomy versus B. bladhii not measured; species confirmation pending.

**Look-alikes:** Bothriochloa bladhii, Dichanthium annulatum, B. ischaemum. **Exact follow-up:** Examine pubescent raceme base, spikelet pairs, transparent median-line absence, glume hairs and bent awn against verified comparators.

**Original observations:** [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/374898786) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/359421744) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/326368914) · [iNat](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/250706832)

## *Lolium multiflorum*

**Source applicability:** Direct old infraspecific Flora treatment, modern Kew separate species.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **head:** Single two-ranked ryegrass spike with alternating spikelets. *Flora 1999 / Kew POWO*. Photo: whole spike.
- **awn:** Typically awned lemmas, often about 5–10 mm, though exceptions occur. *Flora 1999 / Kew POWO*. Photo: mature awn.
- **auricle:** Longer, more pointed auricles in regional subsp. multiflorum treatment. *Flora 1999*. Photo: leaf base.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule commonly around 1–2 mm. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: inner ligule.
- **florets:** Often about 5–15 florets per spikelet in Kew GrassBase account. *Kew POWO/GrassBase*. Photo: floret dissection.
- **rachis:** Rachis can be distinctly scabrous, a supporting comparison with smoother perenne. *Go Botany ryegrass key*. Photo: rachis surface.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Flattened spikelets alternating along an unbranched ryegrass rachis, some clearly awned in close-up; roadside or grassy habitats.

**Unresolved:** Not all specimens' awns, glume characters or hybrid status clear; L. perenne / L. × boucheanum lookalikes possible.

**Look-alikes:** Lolium perenne, Lolium × boucheanum, L. temulentum. **Exact follow-up:** Same plant leaf auricles and ligule, 3 mature spikelets showing awn, glumes, florets and rachis texture, with seeding/escape context.

## *Bromus brevis*

**Source applicability:** Kew accepted name and global range only; NO defensible Hays species-level diagnostic description yet.

**Description targets from references (not yet verified on the Hays observation specimen):**
- **status:** Accepted Bromus species at Kew; taxon acceptance alone says nothing about Hays occurrence. *Kew POWO taxonomy*. Photo: voucher identity.
- **habitat_range:** Known native range in central/southwestern Argentina; Kew introduced regions presently listed outside Texas. *Kew POWO distribution*. Photo: specimen provenance.

**Earlier actual Hays photo inspection:** Some long open or somewhat contracted brome-like panicles and elongated spikelets photographed in lawns and disturbed grassland.

**Unresolved:** Photographs vary markedly in spikelet/panicle habit; diagnostic glume and lemma metrics not shown. One observation has recorded ID disagreement. Current global literature does not clearly support a naturally established Hays population.

**Look-alikes:** Bromus catharticus and other similar rescue bromes. **Exact follow-up:** Obtain independent Hays voucher expert ID and a modern Bromus brevis species key, then measure glumes, lemmas, awns and florets side-by-side with local B. catharticus.
