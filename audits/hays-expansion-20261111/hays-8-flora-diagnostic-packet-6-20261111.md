## *Triticum aestivum*

**Treatment:** Triticum aestivum (PDF p.258, printed p.1334).

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **habit:** Annual cereal grass about 40–100 cm tall in the regional Flora; crop or transient escaped volunteer is possible. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: whole rooted plant with habitat.
- **auricle:** Two narrow pointed leaf auricles are present but may wither early. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: blade–sheath junction showing auricles.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule about 2–3 mm in regional treatment. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: inner ligule with mm scale.
- **spike:** Stiff, unbranched, two-sided terminal spike with sessile spikelets at opposite sides of a zigzag rachis. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: complete spike and rachis joints.
- **florets:** Spikelets typically 2–5-flowered, with lower two or three florets fertile. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: dissected spikelet.
- **glumes:** Broad asymmetric glumes at least around 3.6 mm wide, usually 3 or more veined, potentially awned or awnless. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: flattened glume with veins and ruler.

**Previously seen in source Hays photos:** Distinct stiff, terminal wheatlike cereal spikes; some mature awned and some green heads, with full plant bases.

**Not proven:** Wheat versus other Triticum, Hordeum or cereal cultivar forms cannot be established with certainty at this scale; observations may be crop or seed spill escapes rather than wild populations.

**Lookalikes:** Hordeum vulgare, Secale cereale, Aegilops cylindrica and mixed seeded cereal grasses

**Required same-specimen evidence:** Pair mature wheat spikelet and glume measurements with leaf collar and independently verify cultivated field/roadside spill versus reproducing wild Hays population.

**Evidence caution:** The old regional reference explicitly notes transient escape; authentic Hays occurrence is not equivalent to a naturally established population.

**Reference list:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas 1999, Poaceae p.1334 (library PDF page 258) · Hagenbuch & Lemke 2015 Texas Hill Country grass study p.68

## *Avena sativa*

**Treatment:** Avena sativa (PDF p.168, printed p.1244).

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **habit:** Cultivated or volunteer annual, often growing by roadside seed spill and erosion-control seedings. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: whole plant and land-use context.
- **head:** Open often drooping panicle with large pendulous oat spikelets. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: whole flowering panicle.
- **spikelets:** Usually 1–2 fertile florets in hulled oats, but substantially more in some naked-oat types; normal spikelets commonly 25–32 mm long. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: mature spikelet beside ruler.
- **disarticulation:** In cultivated oats, ripe florets generally stay attached to one another instead of easily breaking below each floret. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: mature rachilla/break-off demonstration.
- **callus:** Floret calluses generally glabrous or with very short hairs, unlike conspicuously bearded wild oat calluses. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: detached floret callus macro.
- **awns:** Lemmas typically awnless but sometimes carry weakly bent awns; absence of awns alone is not a species proof. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: mature floret dorsum.

**Previously seen in source Hays photos:** Pendulous oat-like spikelets and grass basal leaves; some field habitat.

**Not proven:** Cultivated oats versus wild oats/forage mixtures and naturally established populations not determined.

**Lookalikes:** Avena fatua, Avena sterilis, Avena strigosa; also cereal crop/forage mixtures

**Required same-specimen evidence:** Examine mature spikelets and document whether florets stay attached to rachilla, the floret callus hairs, the glume lengths and awn structure; confirm whether plants were deliberately seeded.

**Evidence caution:** Cultivated and wild oats overlap; awn/no-awn is insufficient. The original reviewed Hays views provide only broad oat habit.

**Reference list:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas 1999, Avena genus (PDF 168) · Flora of North America, Avena sativa (republished Burke Herbarium https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Avena+sativa) · USDA identification guide https://idtools.org/id/weed-tool/key/GrapeGrassKey/Media/Html/fact_sheets/Ave-sat.html

## *Eragrostis minor*

**Treatment:** Not treated under this name in 1999 Flora.

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **habit:** Usually low tufted annual grass, about 10–45 cm tall in FNA. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: rooted whole plant.
- **collar:** Ligule short and ciliate, around 0.2–0.5 mm. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: true inner ligule and fringe.
- **glands:** Crater-shaped glands can appear on leaf margins, panicle pedicels and below culm nodes. *FNA via Burke Herbarium / Kew POWO*. Needed view: pedicel, node and leaf-edge macro.
- **panicle:** Open to somewhat contracted panicle about 4–20 cm long with stiff divergent branches. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: complete measured panicle.
- **spikelet:** Many-flowered compressed spikelets often around 4–7 mm long, generally about 7–12 florets in FNA sample; wider ranges occur. *FNA via Burke Herbarium*. Needed view: detached spikelet and floret count.
- **lemma palea:** Small approximately 1.4–1.8 mm lemmas may carry glandular keel; paleas persist as lemmas detach upward along rachilla. *FNA via Burke Herbarium / Kew POWO*. Needed view: floret separation under magnification.

**Previously seen in source Hays photos:** Small weedy grass with open branched seedhead in disturbed bare ground.

**Not proven:** Species identification requires mature glumes and lemmas, leaf glands/hairs, and precise spikelet measurement, not shown.

**Lookalikes:** Eragrostis cilianensis, E. barrelieri, E. pilosa and other small lovegrasses

**Required same-specimen evidence:** Show crateriform glands at pedicel/leaf margin with microscope macro, a measured ripe spikelet and retained paleas, plus the true ligule.

**Evidence caution:** Crateriform glands can be absent on individual structures; verify multiple characters. Old Hays photographs do not show these details.

**Reference list:** Flora of North America, Eragrostis minor (via Burke Herbarium https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Eragrostis+minor) · Kew POWO Eragrostis minor https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:401317-1/general-information

## *Briza minor*

**Treatment:** Briza minor (PDF p.174, printed p.1250).

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **habit:** Mostly hairless annual up to about 50 cm tall. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: whole rooted plant.
- **ligule:** Relatively prominent ligule approximately 5–10 mm long. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: blade–sheath junction and scale.
- **head:** Open panicle with distinct individually dangling spikelets on fine, often kinked stalks. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: full flowering panicle.
- **spikelet:** Usually triangular or tapered toward tip, roughly as broad as long, about 2–6 mm across. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: measured mature spikelet face.
- **florets:** Roughly 3–8 florets per spikelet. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: separated florets with scale.
- **size comparison:** Smaller and more triangular than broad, longer-than-wide spikelets of Briza maxima. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: side-by-side B. minor and B. maxima.

**Previously seen in source Hays photos:** Small nearly triangular pendulous spikelets on fine pedicels and low annual grassy plant, compatible with little quaking grass.

**Not proven:** No scaled multi-floret dissection or ligule measurements; only one Hays record.

**Lookalikes:** Briza maxima, Briza media, and immature pendulous Melica spikelets

**Required same-specimen evidence:** Count actual florets and capture a millimeter-scale profile of the triangular head and 5–10 mm ligule; independently confirm the single Hays observation.

**Evidence caution:** Only one Hays observation with three prior images; photo shape is suggestive, but no scaled dissection was performed.

**Reference list:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas 1999, Poaceae p.1250 (PDF p.174)

## *Cenchrus ciliaris*

**Treatment:** Pennisetum ciliare (with Cenchrus ciliaris synonym) (PDF p.234, printed p.1310).

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **habit:** Tufted or spreading perennial forage grass introduced from Old World tropics. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: rooted plant and dense colony.
- **head:** Dense cylindrical bristly inflorescence; looks like Setaria from a distance. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: full seedhead on mm scale.
- **bristles:** Bristles roughly 4–10 mm long, basally fused into a small bur/involucre. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: detached entire bur and basal bristle union.
- **disarticulation:** Mature bristles fall together with the spikelet as a unit. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: involucre with ripe spikelet.
- **setaria difference:** Unlike Cenchrus, Setaria typically drops spikelets separately above persistent bristles. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: paired disarticulation photos.
- **synonym:** Modern candidate accepted name Cenchrus ciliaris; old regional flora printed Pennisetum ciliare. *Taxonomic crosswalk; regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: record provenance.

**Previously seen in source Hays photos:** Dense cylindrical buffelgrass-like head with long bristles, collar and habitat views.

**Not proven:** Fused bristle bases forming detachable bur, and comparison to Setaria are not conclusive.

**Lookalikes:** Setaria parviflora, Setaria pumila, Pennisetum villosum and Cenchrus setaceus

**Required same-specimen evidence:** On a collected mature specimen show the basal union of bristles and detachment as one bur, plus ligule and spikelet, contrasting Setaria.

**Evidence caution:** A cylindrical bristly head by itself proves no species. Invasive/introduced status is descriptive background, not a native-plant recommendation.

**Reference list:** Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas 1999, historical Pennisetum ciliare account, printed p.1310 (PDF p.234)

**Original Hays observation links:** [iNaturalist](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/135456536)

## *Sclerochloa dura*

**Treatment:** Sclerochloa dura (PDF p.240, printed p.1316).

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **habit:** Very small prostrate to erect annual, typically 3–10 cm, sometimes 18 cm, mostly hairless. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: whole plant with ruler.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule about 1–2 mm. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: inner collar with scale.
- **panicle:** Compact dense often unilateral spike-like head around 1–3 cm, rarely 4.5 cm. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: complete flowering head.
- **spikelet:** Ripe spikelets about 6–11 mm with about 3–4 florets. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: isolated spikelet with ruler.
- **glumes lemmas:** Blunt-ended thick glumes and lemmas showing prominent nerves. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: backlit glume and lemma veins.
- **ecology:** Typically roadside/disturbed hard-ground early-season annual. *Regional Flora 1999*. Needed view: rooted habit with pavement/soil context.

**Previously seen in source Hays photos:** Small low grass with short dense one-sided green inflorescence spikes in disturbed ground.

**Not proven:** No scale for multi-flowered spikelets, ligule or glume/lemma nerve count.

**Lookalikes:** Poa annua, Catapodium rigidum, Puccinellia-like grasses and other small disturbed-ground annuals

**Required same-specimen evidence:** Photograph one whole 3–10 cm plant with mm scale, true ligule, and a mature dissected spikelet showing 3–4 florets, stout glumes and strong nerves.

## *Elymus macgregorii*

**Treatment:** Not recognized as separate named species in older 1999 Flora; compare historical Elymus virginicus (PDF p.192, printed p.1268).

**Literature character targets—not yet established on Hays plants:**
- **taxonomy:** Modern Kew accepts a separate species from Elymus virginicus; names cannot be inferred from old regional Elymus virginicus account. *Kew POWO*. Needed view: voucher name provenance.
- **spike:** In a published key E. macgregorii has mostly erect spikes with often 15–30 rachis nodes. *Native Plant Trust Go Botany*. Needed view: entire spike with numbered rachis nodes.
- **glumes:** E. macgregorii has broader flattened glumes (generally 0.7–2.3 mm), contrasting narrow bristle-like Elymus villosus glumes. *Native Plant Trust Go Botany*. Needed view: flattened glumes at mm scale.
- **comparison nodes:** E. virginicus in northeastern key commonly has 9–18 spike nodes, versus more nodes in E. macgregorii; geographic overlap warrants caution. *Native Plant Trust Go Botany*. Needed view: paired full-spike comparison.
- **auricles:** E. macgregorii often has shorter auricles (0–2 mm) than E. virginicus (2–3 mm) in cited key. *Native Plant Trust Go Botany*. Needed view: leaf auricle and collar on scale.
- **hairiness:** Glumes and lemmas may be pubescent in E. macgregorii versus often glabrous or subtly rough in E. virginicus. *Native Plant Trust Go Botany*. Needed view: lemma/glume macro.

**Not proven:** NO_ACCESSIBLE_PRIOR_SPECIMEN_PHOTOGRAPHS

**Lookalikes:** Elymus virginicus, E. villosus and E. curvatus, with particular concern for transitional Texas material
