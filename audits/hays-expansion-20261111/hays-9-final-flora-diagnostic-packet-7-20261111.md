## *Phyllostachys aurea*

**Source:** 1999 Flora PDF p.235, printed p.1311; [additional source](https://midatlanticherbaria.org/portal/taxa/index.php?clid=4891&taxauthid=1&tid=34613).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Woody perennial spreads via rhizomes, forming dense bamboo colonies; old Flora stems 2–8 m.
- **nodes:** Lower culm internodes often shortened and compressed; vital fishpole-bamboo feature.
- **groove:** Culm flattened or grooved on one side above branching nodes.
- **branching:** Usually two to three unequal branches at nodes.
- **leaf:** Narrow lanceolate leaves often 5–15 cm, with small petiole; flowers uncommon.

**Prior image evidence:** Woody running bamboo thicket, narrow lanceolate leaves and node branching, multiple culms; compatible with Phyllostachys.

**Unresolved:** Basal culm internode crowding/compression characteristic of fishpole bamboo, grooved internodes and branch node details not demonstrated; planting or escaped-colony provenance not established.

**Comparison:** Other Phyllostachys and native Arundinaria gigantea

**Same-specimen proof:** Expose lower culm nodes, internode compression, grooves and branches; document landscape planting versus spread.

**Hold:** Cultivation/escaped colony and lower culm anatomy not verified.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/355471088) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/337005807) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/337005707) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/329633572)

## *Cortaderia selloana*

**Source:** 1999 Flora PDF p.182, printed p.1258; [additional source](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?lvl=sp&name=Cortaderia~selloana&page=nswfl).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Huge perennial tussock, usually 2–3 m or more.
- **leaf:** Long coarse leaves with sharp finely serrate margins.
- **ligule:** Dense fringe of hairs approximately 3–5 mm.
- **panicle:** Large multi-branched feathery panicle around 25–100 cm, not small cylindrical bottlebrush.
- **florets:** Multi-flowered spikelets; pistillate lemmas densely silky, staminate florets less hairy.

**Prior image evidence:** Some tall, silvery plume-bearing ornamental clumps look consistent with pampas grass; narrow, strap-like leaves in tuft.

**Unresolved:** At least one observation (62141431) shows multiple short cylindrical fountain-grass-like plumes that are NOT clearly the large branched Cortaderia panicle, raising a possible mixed/misidentified iNaturalist set. Cultivated landscaping likely.

**Comparison:** Cortaderia jubata and Cenchrus setaceus fountain grass

**Same-specimen proof:** Recheck iNaturalist 62141431 for possible misidentification; compare entire branching plume, ligule and floret hairs.

**Hold:** CRITICAL: some saved source photos resemble fountain grass and may be misidentified; likely planted.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/306566662) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/62141431) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/36035841) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/34559895)

## *Panicum miliaceum*

**Source:** 1999 Flora PDF p.224, printed p.1300; [additional source](https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Panicum+miliaceum).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Annual coarse millet, cultivated or casual seed-spill volunteer.
- **sheath:** Long hairs borne on papillae on sheaths.
- **ligule:** Short ciliate membrane about 1–3 mm including hairs.
- **panicle:** Rather dense branched panicle about 6–20 cm in Flora of North America.
- **spikelet:** Plump ripe spikelets 4–6 mm; lower glume about one-half to three-quarters of spikelet, upper many-veined.

**Prior image evidence:** Green grain-bearing inflorescence, coarse grass-like blades and stem closeups visible; a separate full plant photographed in disturbed ground.

**Unresolved:** Several views show unusually narrow/contracted green heads versus typically broad, loose proso millet panicle, which could reflect immaturity or incorrect ID; no ripe grain/spikelet morphology or cultivar origin verified.

**Comparison:** Panicum capillare, P. dichotomiflorum, other seeded millets

**Same-specimen proof:** Measure ripe spikelet/glume and photograph hairy sheath from same plant; establish seed spill or spontaneous wild context.

**Hold:** Narrow immature-looking heads in previous image sample; not species-verified or established.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/232929491) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/220772915) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/205146982) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/117019222)

## *Muhlenbergia capillaris*

**Source:** 1999 Flora PDF p.212, printed p.1288; [additional source](https://sernecportal.org/portal/taxa/index.php?taxon=Muhlenbergia+capillaris).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Densely tufted nonrhizomatous perennial, around 60–100 cm in old regional Flora.
- **panicle:** Wide diffuse pink-purple head with hairlike branches and pedicels.
- **ligule:** Firm membranous decurrent ligule approximately 1.8–5 mm in FNA.
- **spikelet:** Usually one-flowered about 3–5 mm; glumes notably shorter than lemmas.
- **awn:** Lemma may carry variable (2–)5–15 mm awn in old Flora; some FNA specimens awnless.

**Prior image evidence:** One airy purple-pink muhly plume over dense basal tuft.

**Unresolved:** Single image consistent with ornamental pink muhly, not species confirmation or wild population.

**Comparison:** Muhlenbergia reverchonii, M. lindheimeri and horticultural pink muhly

**Same-specimen proof:** Separate glume and mature lemma with awn and inner ligule; check plant origin and possible cultivar.

**Hold:** One prior ornamental-looking image; no verified wild Hays species identity.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/65015271) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/36441935) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/17126784)

## *Melinis repens*

**Source:** Modern species/hybrid treatment; [additional source](https://www.worldfloraonline.org/taxon/wfo-0000879582).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Annual or short-lived tufted perennial; lower stems may root at nodes.
- **panicle:** Fluffy silvery-pink or purple panicle fading gray.
- **hairy spikelet:** Ovate spikelet covered in reddish silky hairs several millimeters long.
- **glumes:** Lower glume much smaller than often five-veined upper glume with narrow beak.
- **ligule:** Short membrane about 0.5–1.4 mm under one botanical treatment.

**Prior image evidence:** Characteristic pink fuzzy open plume-like seedheads and clumped foliage visible; strong general photo evidence.

**Unresolved:** Scenes resemble ornamental or managed bunches; ligule and florets not resolved and independent wild established stand not shown.

**Comparison:** Pink Eragrostis and Muhlenbergia

**Same-specimen proof:** Photograph ripe isolated spikelet hairs, different glumes, collar and rooted Hays population.

**Hold:** Pink heads visible but minute anatomy and independent naturalization not proven.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/62114751) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/34747879)

## *Nassella tenuissima*

**Source:** Modern species/hybrid treatment; [additional source](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:281184-2/general-information).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Tightly tufted perennial 25–70 cm in GrassBase.
- **blades:** Threadlike or needlelike rolled blades about 0.4–0.5 mm across.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule 1.5–4 mm, not ciliate.
- **spikelet:** Narrow panicle with few spikelets; long hyaline glumes around 5–10.5 mm.
- **lemma awn:** Hardened lemma 1.8–3.6 mm with long geniculate awn often 50–90 mm.

**Prior image evidence:** Fine threadlike foliage and flowing pale feathery seedheads above tufted base; compatible with Mexican feathergrass.

**Unresolved:** Could reflect planted ornamental Mexican feathergrass, and awns/lemma microcharacters are invisible.

**Comparison:** Nassella leucotricha, other Nassella/Stipa

**Same-specimen proof:** Measure entire bent awn, glumes and true ligule from same plant and document whether planted.

**Hold:** Potential ornamental planting; earlier photos lack ripe spikelet microcharacters.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/118195974) · [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/111091761)

## *Miscanthus sinensis*

**Source:** 1999 Flora PDF p.208, printed p.1284; [additional source](https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Miscanthus+sinensis).

**Reference characters or taxonomic statements (not Hays-confirmed traits):**
- **habit:** Large tufted/rhizomatous perennial about 1–3 m.
- **leaf:** Pale central blade midrib and rough edges; some cultivated forms variegated.
- **ligule:** Ciliate membrane about 1.5–3 mm in old regional Flora.
- **panicle:** Large branched panicle 10–35 cm with ascending branches.
- **paired spikelets:** Paired stalked spikelets with long basal hair tuft, one short and one long pedicel.
- **awn:** Spikelets about 4–5.5 mm and twisted bent lemma awn about 5–10 mm in regional Flora.

**Prior image evidence:** Clumped tall arching narrow leaves in an ornamental-looking garden setting.

**Unresolved:** No panicle or spikelets; cannot verify species or wild occurrence.

**Comparison:** Miscanthus sacchariflorus, Cortaderia and Saccharum

**Same-specimen proof:** Find ripe paired silky spikelets and twisted awns with leaf midrib and collar; check landscape origin.

**Hold:** Only one old photo of ornamental foliage, no verified spikelets or naturalized Hays stand.

**Original observations:** [record](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/352734605)
