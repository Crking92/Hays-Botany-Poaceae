## *Paspalum notatum*

**Taxonomic treatment:** 1999 Flora species treatment and Kew POWO/GrassBase; old variety key conflicting with the broad species branch-count summary must not be promoted as diagnosis.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Mat-forming rhizomatous perennial, with flowering culms typically about 20–75 cm in the regional Flora. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: rooted rhizomes.
- **head:** Usually two nearly paired racemes at the summit, occasionally a third; each roughly 4–12(–15) cm in the Flora. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: complete scaled inflorescence.
- **spikelet:** Broadly ovate to obovate glabrous spikelets, usually 2.5–3.8 mm across modern treatments. Source: Flora 1999 and Kew POWO. Image requirement: isolated mature spikelet and ruler.
- **rows:** Spikelets single and arranged in two rows along the raceme axis. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: raceme side and face.
- **glume:** Lower glume typically absent or poorly developed, upper glume smooth and about as long as the spikelet. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: dissected glumes.
- **collar:** Short ligule with hairs described in POWO accounts; examine exact collar rather than inferring from turf. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: inner leaf collar.

**Prior visual review:** Clearly forked Y/V-shaped pair of spike-like racemes with small spikelets; smooth narrow leaves and mowed lawn/field context.

**Missing direct evidence:** Paired racemes strongly support Bahia grass but microscopic broadly ovate glumes and seed size not confirmed; many records from lawns and could be introduced turf.

**Comparison:** Paspalum distichum, P. dilatatum, P. pubiflorum, other paired-raceme Paspalum

**Next proof:** Measure and dissect a mature isolated spikelet and verify rhizome/base, true ligule and branch orientation on the same Hays plant; separate mowed planted turf from naturalized colonies.

**Hold/caution:** Several Hays observations are mowed lawns. Two forked racemes alone do not establish wild Hays occurrence. The regional Flora's infraspecific branch counts are inconsistent with its species-level generalization; do not use varietal thresholds without a modern key.

## *Bromus tectorum*

**Taxonomic treatment:** 1999 Flora explicit species and brome couplet; Kew POWO modern detailed species morphology.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Introduced cool-season annual with often soft-hairy lower sheaths. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: rooted plant and lower sheath.
- **panicle:** Open or nodding panicle with drooping branches, not a unique species-level character. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: whole inflorescence.
- **lemma awn:** Old regional Bromus key gives about 12–18 mm awns, while broader Kew treatment gives 10–25 mm; use source-specific ranges. Source: Flora 1999 / Kew POWO. Image requirement: mature measured awns.
- **lemma teeth:** Very elongated thin transparent apical lemma teeth, about 2–5 mm under regional key. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: dissected mature lemma apex.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule approximately 2–5 mm, margin often torn. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: inner ligule and scale.
- **lemma surface:** Lemma may be hairy or nearly hairless under varieties described in the regional Flora. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: lemma pubescence macro.

**Prior visual review:** Two observations display open, drooping, long-awned brome panicles.

**Missing direct evidence:** Unmeasured lemma awns and sheaths do not distinguish cheatgrass reliably from other annual brome species; just 2 local records.

**Comparison:** Bromus japonicus, B. secalinus, B. hordeaceus, B. catharticus

**Next proof:** Detach mature lemma and show long apical teeth plus distinct long awn and glume/lemma nervation with mm scale; verify basal sheath hairs on the same plant.

**Hold/caution:** Only two previously reviewed Hays images and no measured lemma-apex teeth. Do not infer cheatgrass merely from drooping long-awned brome heads.

**Original Hays observations:** [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/209232730) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/3092228)

## *Sphenopholis interrupta*

**Taxonomic treatment:** Direct 1999 Flora under Trisetum interruptum with explicit Sphenopholis interrupta alternate classification; POWO accepted Sphenopholis genus placement.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Annual grass roughly 7–50(–60) cm tall, leaf sheaths pubescent. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: rooted base and sheath hairs.
- **ligule:** Asymmetrical membranous ligule 1.5–2 mm with irregular ragged margin. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: inner ligule mm scale.
- **panicle:** Narrow spike-like panicle, which can appear interrupted. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: scaled panicle.
- **spikelet:** Spikelets 2–3-flowered, around 4–6 mm excluding the awns. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: dissected spikelet count.
- **lemma apex:** Lemma with two slender apical teeth. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: isolated lemma face and tip.
- **awn:** Awn arising on back near base of apical teeth, twisted and bent, about 5–8 mm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: side-view lemma awn origin.

**Prior visual review:** Slender interrupted-looking spikes with close-up overlapping green spikelets and sheath/stem junction views.

**Missing direct evidence:** Second glume shape, ligule and floret details not decisively visible; compare existing Sphenopholis obtusata candidate.

**Comparison:** Sphenopholis obtusata, Koeleria macrantha, Rostraria cristata

**Next proof:** Separate mature spikelets to count florets, backlight the lemma's two teeth and precisely show the insertion of its twisted awn; obtain true ligule macro and compare with S. obtusata.

**Hold/caution:** Interrupted-looking flowering heads are not species-specific; the old Trisetum genus label is a nomenclatural alias, not an extra candidate.

**Original Hays observations:** [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/359410608) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/349892863) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/157853430)

## *Rostraria cristata*

**Taxonomic treatment:** Species-rank diagnosis comes from Kew POWO/GrassBase and Flora of Oman, not from 1999 Flora.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Small tufted annual, culms generally 5–45 cm in GrassBase. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: rooted whole plant.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule about 1 mm, lacking long hair fringe. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: inner ligule.
- **panicle:** Narrow dense to interrupted spike-like panicle approximately 1.5–10 cm in GrassBase. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: measured panicle.
- **florets:** Spikelets approximately 3–7 mm and typically 3–8 fertile florets. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: separated florets with ruler.
- **glumes:** Lower glume often 1-veined and shorter, upper glume around 3-veined and broader. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: flattened glume nerves.
- **awn:** Lemma awn may be apical/subapical and short, approximately 0–3 mm. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: lemma lateral view.

**Prior visual review:** Short cylindrical green bristly panicle with densely overlapping awned or pointed spikelets; small tufted grass.

**Missing direct evidence:** Actual glume and lemma awn origins, floret counts and ligule unclear; compact spikelike heads resemble other small pooid grasses.

**Comparison:** Koeleria, Sphenopholis, Trisetum-like grasses

**Next proof:** Isolate several ripe florets and both glumes with a 1-mm scale, photograph awn origin, floret count and true ligule; compare with similar dense-headed annual Pooideae.

**Hold/caution:** Compact green bristly heads are not species proof. The 1999 regional Flora has no direct Rostraria cristata species treatment.

**Original Hays observations:** [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/281345779) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/276345150) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/273467058) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/74683342)

## *Polypogon viridis*

**Taxonomic treatment:** Direct Flora species treatment; Kew POWO/GrassBase modern confirmation. Glume-awn absence separates from bristly P. monspeliensis..

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Glabrous grass with bent/decumbent stem base, often in wet creek and spring habitats; Flora culms about 15–45(–70) cm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: whole base and habitat.
- **ligule:** Membranous ligule approximately 2–7 mm in regional Flora (other treatments may give shorter values). Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: true inner ligule mm scale.
- **head:** Condensed panicle roughly 4–12 cm long and 1–3 cm broad in regional Flora. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: scaled flowering head.
- **spikelet:** Small spikelets about 1.3–2(–2.5) mm in the Flora. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: dissected spikelet.
- **awns:** Glumes and lemmas typically awnless; unlike rabbitfoot P. monspeliensis with long glume awns. Source: Flora 1999 / Kew POWO. Image requirement: isolated glume apex.
- **glume surface:** Glumes lightly roughened, one-veined, broad near apex in GrassBase account. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: flattened glumes.

**Prior visual review:** Moist rock/creek-edge habitat and narrow, somewhat contracted flowering panicles.

**Missing direct evidence:** Awnless spikelet structure and ligule length not distinguished from other bentgrasses.

**Comparison:** Polypogon monspeliensis, Agrostis spp., other wet meadow bentgrasses

**Next proof:** Photograph a complete creekside Hays specimen with measured ligule and detached awnless spikelets, then compare with bristle-awned P. monspeliensis.

**Hold/caution:** Wetland setting and a narrow panicle alone cannot distinguish this taxon; old Agrostis combination should be kept searchable.

**Original Hays observations:** [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/156821533) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/76750381) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/44142099)

## *Hordeum murinum*

**Taxonomic treatment:** Flora 1999 treats subsp. leporinum; Kew accepts murinum species and several subspecies—do not assign subspecies from unmeasured Hays photos.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Annual hare barley, generally 30–75 cm in older regional subsp. leporinum account. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: rooted flowering plant.
- **spikelet groups:** Barley-like spike of groups of three spikelets at successive joints, central fertile with lateral companions. Source: Kew POWO/GrassBase. Image requirement: intact triad and rachis.
- **glume hairs:** Glumes of central spikelet have hairs/ciliate margins in regional key. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: glume edge micro.
- **auricles:** Leaf sheath auricles present; distinguish from H. pusillum lacking auricles in regional key. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: collar auricle view.
- **awn:** Lemma awns in the leporinum account typically around 10–35 mm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: mature awn with scale.
- **lemma width:** Regional group key mentions slender lemmas 1.3–1.9 mm wide and leaf blades 1–6 mm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: flattened lemma and blade scale.

**Prior visual review:** Short dense barley-like awned heads and annual bunches shown.

**Missing direct evidence:** Glume/ciliate margins and hybrid/subspecies-level distinctions not observed; older Flora treats subsp. leporinum.

**Comparison:** Hordeum pusillum, H. vulgare and other wild barley grasses

**Next proof:** Separate an intact three-spikelet group and photograph glume fringes, lateral pedicels and awn lengths with mm scale; record true auricles, basal habit and whether old leporinum subspecies applies.

**Hold/caution:** A typical awned barley head supports the genus, not murinum subspecies; the Flora's leporinum treatment cannot automatically assign modern lower-rank taxon.

## *Dactyloctenium aegyptium*

**Taxonomic treatment:** Direct Flora species; corroborated by modern Kew descriptions.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Spreading annual, often roots from lower stem nodes; flowering stems commonly 10–60 cm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: rooted creeping base.
- **ligule:** Short ciliate membrane usually 0.1–1 mm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: inner leaf junction.
- **head:** Two to seven or more radiating fingerlike branches, usually around 1.5–6 cm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: complete digitate head.
- **branch tip:** Flattened flowering branch has a sterile narrow tip extending about 1–7 mm beyond last spikelet. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: terminal branch tip macro.
- **spikelets:** Tightly packed sessile flattened spikelets in two rows along one side of branches; about 3–4 mm. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: branch side macro.
- **florets:** Each spikelet typically contains 3–5 florets; upper glumes and lemmas often shortly awned. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: dissected spikelet.

**Prior visual review:** Fingerlike branches radiating from culm apex, consistent with crowdfoot grass inflorescence.

**Missing direct evidence:** Images not sufficiently close to confirm branch tips projecting beyond spikelets, floret details, or rooting nodes.

**Comparison:** Eleusine indica, Dactylis-like digitate grasses, Chloris species

**Next proof:** Photograph sterile rachis projection beyond last mature spikelet with mm ruler, plus single detached flattened 3–5-flowered spikelet and rooted nodes.

**Hold/caution:** Fingerlike heads alone misclassify several Hays grasses. Needs branch-end projection and floret dissection.

**Original Hays observations:** [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/388784436) · [observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/379049304)

## *Oplismenus hirtellus*

**Taxonomic treatment:** Direct Flora species account plus Kew POWO/GrassBase for modern accepted name; former O. setarius lumping is a real species-concept caution.

**Literature-described identification characters (not yet verified on Hays specimens):**
- **habit:** Sprawling perennial stems root at lower nodes and form groundcover in shaded places. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: rooted node and habitat.
- **leaves:** Broad spreading blades about 1.5–7 cm by 5–15 mm in Flora account. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: measured blade.
- **ligule:** Short ciliate membrane at inner collar. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: true leaf collar.
- **racemes:** About 3–7 short widely separated unilateral flowering branches, roughly 1–6 mm in old regional description. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: whole inflorescence and branch scale.
- **spikelets:** Spikelets in two ranks along one side; paired in modern GrassBase; two florets with fertile upper floret. Source: Flora 1999 / Kew POWO. Image requirement: paired spikelet macro.
- **glume awn:** Glumes awned; first glume awn typically around 5–10 mm and distinctly longer than second. Source: Flora 1999. Image requirement: isolated glumes and awn base.

**Prior visual review:** Shaded groundcover with broad lanceolate leaf blades, sprawling stem habit and distinct long-bristled/hooked-looking spikelets in close-up.

**Missing direct evidence:** The precise floret/awn base and bristle count distinguishing Oplismenus species not fully resolved; no plant establishment context vetted.

**Comparison:** Oplismenus undulatifolius and other creeping shade grasses, including historic O. setarius concept

**Next proof:** Same Hays plant: trace rooted creeping node, show ciliate ligule and paired awned spikelets at mm scale, inspect historical setarius distinction and site planting context.

**Hold/caution:** Flora treats O. setarius broadly within hirtellus while other historical sources split it. A shaded leafy groundcover and hooked awns do not independently settle species concept or wild occurrence.
