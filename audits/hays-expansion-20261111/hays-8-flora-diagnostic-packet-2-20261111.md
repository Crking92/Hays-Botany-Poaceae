## *Aegilops cylindrica*
**Flora treatment:** *Aegilops cylindrica*; user Library PDF p. 159, printed p. 1235.

**Source-described character targets (not approved traits):**
- **habit:** Annual, generally 35–75 cm tall (Flora). Photo role: HABIT.
- **sheath auricles:** Leaf sheaths mostly hairless apart from margins; blade base may flare into small auricles (Flora). Photo role: COLLAR_MACRO.
- **ligule:** Membranous, shorter than 1 mm (Flora). Photo role: INNER_LIGULE.
- **spike axis:** Slender cylindrical spike with spikelets fitting niches along an unbranched flattened, bilaterally organized axis (Flora). Photo role: SPIKE_NODE_MACRO.
- **florets:** Sessile spikelets with 2–5 florets; upper florets often reduced (Flora). Photo role: SPIKELET_DISSECTION.
- **awns:** Glumes and lemmas of upper spikelets bear long awns, approximately 3–8 cm (Flora). Photo role: AWN_MEASURE.

**Already visible in prior Hays visual QA:** Good views of slender jointed cylindrical spike; detached spike segments/units and auricle-area photos visible.

**Not independently demonstrated:** Glume arrangement, exact joint/awn dimensions and competing Aegilops species not measured.

**Comparison:** Hordeum spp., Triticum aestivum and other Aegilops. **Next same-specimen evidence:** Photograph one intact ripe spike and detached spike joint/spikelet on a millimeter scale, exposing glumes, floret count, ligule and auricle.

**Media:** NC_LICENSE_PHOTOS_ONLY_NONCOMMERCIAL_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED; no current license recheck, no photo publication approval. **Caution:** Photo architecture strongly supports jointed goatgrass but not independently verified species anatomy; confirm spontaneous roadside occurrence.

## *Eragrostis curtipedicellata*
**Flora treatment:** *Eragrostis curtipedicellata*; user Library PDF p. 194, printed p. 1270.

**Source-described character targets (not approved traits):**
- **habit:** Tufted, hard-based perennial, usually 15–75 cm tall (Flora). Photo role: ROOTED_BASE.
- **sheath:** Sheaths mostly hairless except margins and the uppermost opening (Flora). Photo role: SHEATH_MACRO.
- **panicle:** Open panicle with visibly sticky/viscid axis, often detaching with age (Flora). Photo role: PANICLE_AXIS_MACRO.
- **leaf blade:** Narrow leaf blades may project abruptly outward, near a right angle (Hill Country 2015). Photo role: LEAF_BLADE_SIDE.
- **ligule:** Very short fringe of hairs rather than a prominent long membrane (Hill Country 2015). Photo role: INNER_LIGULE.

**Already visible in prior Hays visual QA:** Single open and diffuse lovegrass-like panicle image.

**Not independently demonstrated:** Crucial viscid/gummy panicle axis cannot be demonstrated; mature spikelet dimensions not visible.

**Comparison:** Other small-spikeleted Eragrostis including E. spectabilis and E. intermedia. **Next same-specimen evidence:** Obtain a sharp same-plant macro demonstrating sticky panicle axis/sheath and identifiable mature spikelets, plus scale-backed collar and crown.

## *Stenotaphrum secundatum*
**Flora treatment:** *Stenotaphrum secundatum*; user Library PDF p. 252, printed p. 1328.

**Source-described character targets (not approved traits):**
- **habit:** Low stolon-forming, sod-forming perennial, to roughly 30 cm (Flora). Photo role: STOLON_NODES.
- **sheath:** Distinctively flattened and keeled sheaths, leaves often creased lengthwise (Flora). Photo role: SHEATH_MACRO.
- **ligule:** Very short ring/fringe of hairs (Flora). Photo role: INNER_LIGULE.
- **inflorescence axis:** Flowering head has a broad flattened corky axis with spikelets impressed into one side (Flora). Photo role: FLOWERING_RACHIS.
- **spikelet:** Spikelets 4–5 mm long, awnless, two-flowered with lower floret sterile/staminate and upper fertile (Flora). Photo role: SPIKELET_DISSECTION.
- **leaf arrangement:** Leaves borne in two ranks; folded vernation and rooted stolons (Hill Country 2015). Photo role: STOLON_AND_LEAF_ORDER.

**Already visible in prior Hays visual QA:** Broad-bladed mowed sod/turf habit, compatible with commonly planted St Augustine grass.

**Not independently demonstrated:** Turf scenes do not expose stolon joints, keeled compressed sheath, hair-ring ligule, or unique sunken florets on a corky rachis; research-grade wild tag does not establish spontaneous Hays occurrence.

**Comparison:** Other planted stoloniferous turfgrasses; nonflowering lawns cannot verify this taxon as wild. **Next same-specimen evidence:** Find a flowering plant with a corky one-sided head, true collar hair ring and rooted stolons, and independently record whether it is escaped or merely a maintained lawn.

**Media:** NC_LICENSE_PHOTOS_ONLY_NONCOMMERCIAL_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED; no current license recheck, no photo publication approval. **Caution:** All reviewed source images largely show maintained turf. Published Hays collection does not prove sampled iNaturalist turf is a wild population. Establishment remains on hold.

## *Echinochloa colona*

**Source-described character targets (not approved traits):**
- **spikelet length:** Spikelets approximately 2.5–3 mm long (Flora). Photo role: SPIKELET_MEASURE.
- **habitat:** Often low ground or disturbed moist sites (Flora). Photo role: HABITAT_CONTEXT.
- **ligule:** Ligule absent at the collar (not a membranous flap or tuft of long hairs) (Hill Country 2015). Photo role: INNER_COLLAR.
- **blade pattern:** Blades may have conspicuous purple V-like transverse bands; this is variable and not diagnostic alone (Hill Country 2015). Photo role: LEAF_BLADE.
- **spikelet comparison:** Reproductive discrimination must include glumes and lemma surface and awn condition versus other Echinochloa (Flora genus/key context). Photo role: SPIKELET_DISSECTION.

**Already visible in prior Hays visual QA:** Small tufted/stolonlike grass and short branched, sometimes crowded panicle with small spikelets; one wet-soil observation.

**Not independently demonstrated:** Panicle architecture and glume/lemma hair/awn details do not conclusively separate E. colona from other Echinochloa and lookalike Paniceae.

**Comparison:** Echinochloa crus-galli, E. muricata, E. walteri. **Next same-specimen evidence:** Use one mature Hays specimen to show the actual absence of a ligule and measure several detached spikelets/lemma surfaces; compare with E. crus-galli, E. muricata and E. walteri.

## *Tridens congestus*
**Flora treatment:** *Tridens congestus*; user Library PDF p. 254, printed p. 1330.

**Source-described character targets (not approved traits):**
- **habit:** Usually glabrous tufted perennial, approximately 30–75 cm tall (Flora). Photo role: ROOTED_BASE.
- **panicle:** Compact flowering panicles generally 5–10 cm long (Flora). Photo role: PANICLE_MEASURE.
- **glumes lemma:** Glumes and lemmas thin and papery, often pink-tinged (Flora). Photo role: SPIKELET_MACRO.
- **lemma tip:** Lemma apex deeply cleft, a major diagnosis for this taxon (Flora). Photo role: ISOLATED_LEMMA_TIP.
- **floret context:** Genus Tridens has multi-flowered spikelets, often 4–12 florets; genus-level context only (Flora genus overview). Photo role: SPIKELET_DISSECTION.

**Already visible in prior Hays visual QA:** Dense pale tan to slightly pink compressed flowering panicles, green stems and tufted field habit across separate observations.

**Not independently demonstrated:** Thin papery pink-tinged glumes and the deeply cleft lemma apex needed to separate pink tridens not resolved.

**Comparison:** Tridens flavus, Tridentopsis mutica, other compact Tridens. **Next same-specimen evidence:** On a Hays voucher, separate and backlight an actual ripe lemma tip to reveal the deep cleft; measure panicle and glume dimensions and compare Tridens flavus and T. muticus.

**Media:** NC_LICENSE_PHOTOS_ONLY_NONCOMMERCIAL_ASSESSMENT_REQUIRED; no current license recheck, no photo publication approval. **Caution:** Pink-tinged crowded panicles support a preliminary grouping; the diagnostic lemma cleft has not been seen in the earlier Hays photographs.
