## Source discrepancies newly surfaced

1. **Bromus catharticus life history is contradictory:** Diggs et al. (1999, printed p.1252) describes a winter annual, Hagenbuch & Lemke (2015, printed p.39) call it perennial and the Kew POWO overview gives annual/biennial while retaining a perennial GrassBase description. All three perspectives are now linked to the specimen hold. **Do not introduce duration as a hard filter**.
2. **Setaria leucopila's ligule is both membrane and cilia:** H&L (2015, p.61) explicitly shows a tiny ca. 0.5 mm membrane with 1–2 mm marginal hairs. A field observer could legitimately select the membrane or hairs based on their image. The species must remain possible under both visually supported choices after proper specimen release. Hays reference vouchers are *Breckenridge 490* and *Tabler s.n.* (SWT). The associated *S. parviflora* has short knotty rhizomes in the same source, so basal proof matters.
3. **Stenotaphrum secundatum likewise has a ciliate membrane:** H&L (2015, p.65) and FNA use membranous-plus-hairy rather than an exclusively hairs-only description. Most staged Hays observations are mowed lawn views; historic *Litchfield s.n.* (SWT) doesn't prove escape from cultivation.
4. **Bothriochloa longipaniculata has a genuine species-level USDA reference:** Maher & Reilley (2019) gives measurable flowering-head, sessile spikelet, ligule and awn dimensions, while the 1999 Flora uses a *B. laguroides* subsp. *torreyana* broad treatment. We can build a targeted diagnostic photograph set without pretending the broad older key proves a modern *longipaniculata* specimen. Glume profile and matched *B. torreyana* comparisons remain decisive.
5. **Ryegrass complex:** The 1999 Flora treats modern *Lolium perenne* and *L. multiflorum* under two subspecies and recognizes significant intermediate cases and awn polymorphism; Kew treats the two as separate accepted species. The hybrid remains its own unresolved provisional candidate. Preserve alternate awn conditions and never infer definitive species boundaries from a single seedhead.
6. **Bothriochloa bladhii vs Dichanthium aristatum:** Both names are currently accepted by Kew, introduced to Texas, and not directly diagnosed as separate species by the 1999 regional Flora. Their raceme grouping alone does not determine genus/species. Require a clearly measured sessile/pedicelled pair and pedicel median groove/line anatomy, avoiding transfer of *D. annulatum* or *B. ischaemum* species details.
7. **Eragrostis superba locality:** 2015 H&L lists related regional herbarium vouchers but the specific *E. superba* appendix entries found were in Bastrop, Brazos, Llano, Mason and Menard rather than Hays. Staged 59 Hays photographic observations still need independent specimen ID and planting context. A state or regional occurrence is not proof of an individual Hays observation.
8. **Triticum aestivum is a crop/roadside waif, not a native meadow addition:** Historic *Ruiseco 128* (SWT) Hays voucher corroborates county collection. The 2015 and 1999 source accounts explicitly note cultivated and transient escape settings; distinguish key inclusion from native restoration recommendation.
