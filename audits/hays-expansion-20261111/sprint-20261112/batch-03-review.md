## New source and false-exclusion findings

1. **Basketgrass — _Oplismenus hirtellus_**. A September **2026** USDA Forest Service report, GTR NRS-241 **printed page 35** (PDF page 43, screenshot inspected), reports taxonomic disagreement among Kew, FNA, ITIS and local atlases over _O. hirtellus_, _O. setarius_ and _O. compositus_. The lowest panicle branch measurements are often 13–70 mm (_hirtellus_) versus under ~11 mm (_setarius_), yet keys **overlap**; _O. burmannii_ differs partly in awn texture not reliably visible in digital imagery. The staged Diggs-based packet's '1–6 mm racemes' is inconsistent with these longer branches and should be rechecked against the actual regional source before using as a measurement. **Do not promote a specific species from an undissected, shady groundcover view.** [USDA Forest Service report](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/81382.pdf).
2. **Walter's barnyardgrass — _Echinochloa walteri_**. Prior Hays photos reportedly show papilla-based hairs on the sheaths and awned heads. [Go Botany key](https://gobotany.nativeplanttrust.org/dkey/echinochloa/) and [FNA-sourced treatment](https://biogator.org/taxa/index.php?tid=3557) support this as promising but note variability: rare nearly glabrous sheaths or short/absent awns occur. Hard “hair present” and “long awn present” constraints could **falsely exclude** legitimate _walteri_. Next obtain mature spikelet lemma and sheath hair bases on the *same local plant*.
3. **_Bromus brevis_**. [Kew POWO](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A36988-2) recognizes this species from Argentina and introduced areas not currently listing Texas; its existence as a taxon is *not evidence* that any Hays photograph is the taxon. With no independent voucher and an old photo ID disagreement, hold Hays species identity and any scored admission, regardless of ten source labels.
4. **_Rostraria cristata_**. [Kew POWO](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/419582-1) recognizes it as an introduced species in Texas. Three **historical CC BY metadata photo candidates** credited to Chris Hughes are preserved in the audit as prospective diagnostic sources, but access and exact license have not been reconfirmed; do not publish them yet. Its small dense head needs actual glume/floret/awn dissection against _Koeleria_ and _Sphenopholis_.
5. **_Polypogon viridis_**. [Kew](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A418741-1), [ITIS](https://itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=504522) and [FNA via Burke Herbarium](https://burkeherbarium.org/imagecollection/taxon.php?Taxon=Polypogon+viridis) confirm the current name, with _Agrostis semiverticillata_ and _Polypogon semiverticillatus_ as synonyms. Diggs describes a 2–7 mm ligule vs FNA up to about 5 mm, an overlapping source variation. Avoid tight cutoff; distinguish long-glume-awned _P. monspeliensis_ using actual spikelets.
6. **_Steinchisma hians_**. [Kew](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn%3Alsid%3Aipni.org%3Anames%3A243676-2) accepts this name and lists Texas as native, but **no Hays BONAP county map determination was verified**. The older Flora uses _Panicum hians_. Inflated protruding lower palea/gaping ripe spikelet is key; prior Hays photos show only general panicles.
7. **_Hordeum murinum_**. The 1999 regional treatment is specifically **subsp. _leporinum_**; [ITIS](https://itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=524161) retains that rank. Hays photos do not show a measurable barley triad, ciliate central glumes or fully documented auricles; do not assign subspecies from a barley-like head.
8. **_Paspalum notatum_**. Paired terminal racemes are a lead, not proof. The existing regional packet says introduced pasture/turf and highlights infraspecific branch-count complexity. Do not treat planted Y-shaped turf heads as an established wild Hays population.
9. **_Sphenopholis obtusata_**. This is the important differential control for Batch 1 _S. interrupta_. Verify upper-glume shape and floret detail by same-scale comparison; broad panicle outline and seasonally interrupted branching are not decisive.
10. **_Aegilops cylindrica_**. [Texas A&M AgriLife](https://agrilife.org/westtexasrangelands/beware-of-a-new-invader-in-the-concho-valley-jointed-goatgrass/) supports the diagnostic jointed spike and West Texas occurrence, **not** these particular Hays IDs. Photograph an intact spike plus disarticulated joint with florets and ligule from one source specimen.
11. **_Avena sativa_**. Distinguish crops/seed spills and wild oat relatives by ripe floret disarticulation/callus hair. No automatic wild or native label, and awn absence alone doesn't prove the cultivated species.
12. **_Bromus tectorum_**. Two Hays old views of drooping awned bromes are insufficient. Get 2–5-mm translucent apical teeth plus scaled lemma awn, glume and hairy lower sheath to separate similar bromes.
