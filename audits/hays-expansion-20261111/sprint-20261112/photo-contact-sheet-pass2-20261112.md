## Additional species-specific inspections and holds

The source notes below are based on actual fresh preview-pixel examination (full detailed records and photo IDs are in [companion JSON](photo-contact-sheet-pass2-20261112.json)):

- *Bromus catharticus*: growth stages and heads vary across the 12 images; retain brome-level confidence until a ripe lemma keel, apex teeth and collar are linked on an individual plant.
- *Bromus brevis*: seven images are inconsistent enough in flowering-head outline to merit observation-by-observation expert review; no measured specimen separates it from rescue brome, and independent Texas occurrence remains questionable.
- *Echinochloa colona*: short multiple racemes are shown, but no same-plant ligule absence, lower lemma or mature spikelet dissection.
- *Eragrostis superba*: dry panicle and hand-held broad-spikelet views offer an anatomy starting point, not a confirmed genus-species endpoint or naturalized Hays population.
- *Avena sativa*: oatlike heads without harvested mature floret/rachilla attachment and callus hair; crop or transient seed-spill status open.
- *Bromus tectorum*: brown awned Bromus closeups still lack the transparent lemma apical teeth and glume diagnostics.
- *Dactyloctenium aegyptium*: fingerlike head supports identification group; photograph distal **spikelet-free rachis projection** and fertile floret count.
- *Dichanthelium acuminatum*: only a small shady patch of leaves; no documented flowering or seasonally different winter rosette; cannot establish accepted narrow species.
- *Eragrostis curtipedicellata*: a single airy-panicle image; the defining **viscid panicle axis** remains unseen.
- *Eragrostis minor*: two very small sparsely flowered annual grass photos; gland, palea and floret characters absent. Historic Sam Kieschnick photo credits marked CC BY, **not freshly reverified**.
- *Hordeum murinum*: awned heads likely genus Hordeum, but no dissection of **three spikelets at each joint** or auricles; do not assign leporinum subspecies automatically.
- *Megathyrsus maximus*: only distant grass/landscaping and nighttime context, not flowering head anatomy. Low specimen-readiness.
- *Melinis repens*: pink soft flowering heads good for visual lesson, but silky hair origin and true ligule still needed; mostly ornamental/managed-looking.
- *Muhlenbergia capillaris*: only a single tuft with purple-pink airborne head; cannot distinguish *M. reverchonii* or confirm wild status.
- *Nassella tenuissima*: fine cascading foliage and dense ornamental-looking tuft; no bent awn still attached to crowned fertile lemma and no Hays native county proof.
- *Polypogon viridis*: wet-rock vegetation and short compact inflorescence; detached awnless glume and ligule need confirmation against *P. monspeliensis*.

## Historical names and source validity

**`Coelorachis cylindrica` → `Rottboellia campestris` under Kew POWO.** Kew [lists *Coelorachis cylindrica* as a synonym](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/62724-2) of [the accepted *Rottboellia campestris*](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/419621-1), which is native to Texas at statewide level. [ITIS](https://www.itis.gov/servlet/SingleRpt/SingleRpt?search_topic=TSN&search_value=507984) instead accepts *Mnesithea cylindrica*. Preserve alias/citation concepts and do not create two Hays learner species for one historical sheet.

**Historical `Elymus glabriflorus` preview ≠ new `Elymus macgregorii` verified photo.** Its two image tiles remain an **old name concept**, merged into existing *E. virginicus* under the active Kew inventory policy. Alternative taxonomic treatments persist. No specimen has enough spike-node, auricle or glume measurements to resolve the disagreement or qualify the separate *E. macgregorii* candidate.
