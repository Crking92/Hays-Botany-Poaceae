## Per-species source expectations versus photographs

### *Melica nitens*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 208, printed page 1284; listed under *Melica nitens*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Rhizomatous perennial, typically 50–120 cm tall
- inflorescence: Branched flowering panicle with elongated spikelets; published length '10–26 mm' appears inconsistent with habit and is not used as a field threshold
- spikelet: Spikelets about 8–15 mm with usually 2–3 fertile florets
- rudiments: Terminal sterile florets/rudiments are narrow and not divergent from the rachilla; contrast M. mutica spreading, broadly obovate rudiments
- ligule: Membranous ligule 3–6 mm, sheath nearly closed to throat (regional 2015 vegetative key)

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Loose panicle branches with pendulous pale spikelets, leaf blades and tussock context; some hand-scaled flowering heads.

**Not independently resolved:** Fertile-floret count and sterile-rudiment shape not resolved; glume inequality and inner ligule dimensions not measurable.

**Look-alikes:** Melica mutica. **Next specimen action:** Show one complete spikelet with fertile floret count and terminal rudiments, and a separate 1-mm-scaled ligule photo.

**Potential CC source(s):** No non-NC photo in the saved source sample; see rights preflight.

**Caution:** Possible typo in 1999 Flora panicle length; do not encode the reported 'mm' panicle value without independent confirmation.

### *Rottboellia campestris*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 182, printed page 1258; listed under *Coelorachis cylindrica*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Perennial with hard base, short knotty rhizomes, erect or arching culms 25–100 cm
- ligule: Short membranous ligule about 0.5–1 mm
- inflorescence: Very slender cylindrical spike-like raceme about 3 mm in diameter; axis breaks apart at joints
- spikelet_position: Rachis joints have niches in which spikelets fit tightly
- spikelet: Pairs include sessile fertile spikelet with pitted glume and pedicelled sterile spikelet

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Very narrow pencil-like cylindrical inflorescence with obvious successive nodes/segment pattern and hand scale; erect to arching culms and natural grassy habitat in several independent observations.

**Not independently resolved:** Sessile spikelet rachis-niche architecture, pitted glume and exact membrane ligule cannot be resolved in photographs; confirm with dissection before final species-ID signoff.

**Look-alikes:** Other jointed spike grasses. **Next specimen action:** Isolate one rachis segment showing its niche and sessile/pedicelled spikelet pair with a scale.

**Potential CC source(s):** [cc0 source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/684707043)

**Caution:** Current Kew accepted genus differs from 1999 Flora; preserve Coelorachis/Mnesithea historical names.

### *Paspalum langei*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 230, printed page 1306; listed under *Paspalum langei*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Tufted perennial approximately 30–100 cm tall; leaf blades about 7–18 mm broad
- inflorescence: Usually 2–5 spike-like branches 4–8 cm long
- spikelet: Paired/closely arranged spikelets 2.2–2.6 mm in regional Flora (FNA reports broader variation)
- lower_glume: First/lower glume present on some or all spikelets
- pubescence: Second/upper glume and sterile lemma pubescent, including papillose-based hairs in FNA account

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Several slender racemes on branched flowering stalks, paired rows of spikelets in macro view, strap-like basal leaves, wooded/rocky ground context in some observations.

**Not independently resolved:** First-glume presence and dark brown glandular blotches on second glume/sterile lemma cannot be verified at this review scale; species identification unproven.

**Look-alikes:** Paspalum pubiflorum (lower glume absent), P. distichum. **Next specimen action:** Examine original TEX00568501 spikelets at 10×–20× for the lower glume and pubescence, comparing co-collected P. pubiflorum.

**Potential CC source(s):** [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/683823449) • [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/391733453)

**Caution:** Three original TEX Hays sheets inspected but minute hairs and lower glume not independently resolved; 2019 yard specimen has cultivation uncertainty.

### *Setaria viridis*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 244, printed page 1320; listed under *Setaria viridis*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Annual up to about 100 cm, leaf blades generally 3–10 mm wide
- sheath: Leaf sheaths somewhat compressed
- inflorescence: Soft, flexible flowering head with usually green bristles
- spikelet: Spikelets about 1.8–2.6 mm
- lemma: Fertile lemma very finely wrinkled/rugose

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Bottlebrush-like narrow fuzzy panicles, often green but some tan, and visible radiating bristles, clumped annual-looking plants.

**Not independently resolved:** Short-burst bristle arrangement and finely rugose fertile lemma are not resolved against Setaria pumila, S. parviflora and other foxtails; many closeup images of head but no spikelet dissections.

**Look-alikes:** Setaria leucopila (perennial), S. pumila, S. parviflora. **Next specimen action:** Verify mature fertile lemma surface, 1–2 mm hair-fringe ligule, and an annual fibrous root system on same plant.

**Potential CC source(s):** [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/592178855) • [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/592178866)

**Caution:** Introduced Eurasian grass in Flora, appropriate to identify but not a native restoration planting recommendation.

### *Setaria leucopila*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 243, printed page 1319; listed under *Setaria leucopila*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Tufted perennial, about 20–100 cm tall
- inflorescence: Bristles typically about 4–15 mm long
- lemma: Fertile lemma finely rugose with transverse wrinkles
- sheath: Vegetative key notes hairiness around sheath throat and hair-fringe ligule

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Very narrow cylindrical green flowering heads with short bristles, basal leafy tussocks and flowering culms with hand scale.

**Not independently resolved:** Bristle length only approximate, maturity and perennial rhizomatous/tufted base uncertain; fertile lemma fine rugosity/wrinkles unresolvable.

**Look-alikes:** Setaria viridis (annual), S. parviflora. **Next specimen action:** Document perennial crown, short sheaths/ligule hairs, actual bristle lengths, and fertile lemma wrinkles.

**Potential CC source(s):** No non-NC photo in the saved source sample; see rights preflight.

**Caution:** Setaria vegetative differences can be subtle; measure same-specimen morphology rather than inferring species solely from head.

### *Echinochloa walteri*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 190, printed page 1266; listed under *Echinochloa walteri*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Coarse grass typically 1–2 m tall, often in low moist habitat
- sheath: Sheaths typically with rough/hispid papilla-based hairs, especially toward summit
- spikelet: Spikelets about 3–5 mm excluding awn, with or without papilla-based hairs
- awn: Lower floret lemma awn often 1.5–6 cm long; occasionally shortened or absent

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Well-displayed branched awned inflorescences, broad leaf blades, markedly hairy/papillose sheaths in closeups, and wetland edge habitat; multiple independent observations.

**Not independently resolved:** Awn and papilla-based sheath hairs strongly favor Walter's barnyardgrass, but the critical lower floret lemma/awn origin, glume size and comparison with E. crus-galli are not fully measured.

**Look-alikes:** Echinochloa crus-galli, E. muricata, other barnyardgrasses. **Next specimen action:** Photograph mature spikelet with awn origin and adjacent sheath hairs showing papilla-like raised hair bases.

**Potential CC source(s):** No non-NC photo in the saved source sample; see rights preflight.

**Caution:** Not every E. walteri has long awns; absence of long awn cannot automatically exclude.

### *Sphenopholis obtusata*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 248, printed page 1324; listed under *Sphenopholis obtusata*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Annual with single or tufted culms about 15–100(120) cm
- ligule: Membranous ligule around 1.5–3 mm long
- inflorescence: Narrow dense spike-like flowering panicle
- spikelet: Shiny, usually 2–3-flowered spikelets about 1.5–5 mm long
- upper_glume: Second/upper glume broadly obovate, blunt at apex (unlike Koeleria)

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Narrow erect densely flowered spikes/panicles, tan maturing flowers, green panicle heads, a close sheath/leaf junction and multiple independent field scenes.

**Not independently resolved:** Second glume wide obovate blunt apex, floret arrangement and distinction from Koeleria or other wedgescales cannot be proven from contact-size images.

**Look-alikes:** Sphenopholis interrupta, Koeleria. **Next specimen action:** Isolate and photograph second glume broad blunt face, plus floret count and measured true inner ligule.

**Potential CC source(s):** No non-NC photo in the saved source sample; see rights preflight.

**Caution:** Gross dense panicle does not establish correct Sphenopholis species.

### *Tridentopsis elongata*

**Regional source:** *Illustrated Flora of North Central Texas*, PDF page 254, printed page 1330; listed under *Tridens muticus var. elongatus*.

**Species characters described in Flora:**
- habit: Roughly 20–80 cm in the broad Tridens muticus account, narrow elongated 7–20(25) cm panicle
- upper_glume: Var. elongatus: second glume usually 3–7 nerved and about 6–8 mm long
- leaf_blade: Leaves usually 3–4 mm wide in elongatus concept
- comparison: Var. muticus: usually single-veined second glume around 5 mm or shorter, blades typically 1–2 mm wide

**What Hays photographs actually show:** Exceptionally useful Hays photos: small tuft, long narrow panicles, scaly overlapping spikelets, partial collar/ligule, mature branches in hand.

**Not independently resolved:** Species boundary versus Tridentopsis mutica and T. buckleyana depends on lemma/callus, florets, ligule and anther dimensions not all reliably measured.

**Look-alikes:** Existing Tridentopsis mutica, Tridentopsis buckleyana. **Next specimen action:** Backlight one isolated upper glume, count all nerves and measure length, then compare against verified T. mutica.

**Potential CC source(s):** [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/689363680) • [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/689363761) • [cc-by source](https://www.inaturalist.org/photos/689363853)

**Caution:** Kew currently recognizes distinct species; Flora and FNA older taxon concept includes variety. Do not count broad muticus herbarium labels as elongata.

## Release guardrails

The numerical Flora characters describe taxon concepts, not measurements taken from source Hays photographs. Reproductive dissections, crop/planting context, photography credits and license, and current taxon concept must all be checked before any author asserts a verified field-key species identification.
