## Chasmanthium Link — genus pp.50–51
Moderately tall perennials, some rhizomatous; broad flat blades. Ligule hyaline, ciliate membrane or fringe of hairs. Inflorescence open or contracted panicle or spike-like raceme. Spikelets long- or short-pedicelled, 2–many-flowered, lower 1–4 sterile. Disarticulation above glumes and between florets. Glumes subequal, shorter than lemmas, acute to acuminate, 3–7-nerved, laterally compressed and keeled. Paleas shorter than or about as large as lemmas, 2-keeled, keels serrate-winged. Flowers perfect, stamens 3, caryopses ovate to elliptic, laterally compressed, with embryo slightly less than half endosperm. Gould historical genus count five species restricted to eastern to southeastern US.

### Genus key pp.51–52
- Inflorescence open, drooping branches, long-pedicelled usually pendent spikelets 20–50 mm => *Chasmanthium latifolium*.
- Inflorescence usually contracted, erect or ascending branches, subsessile/short-pedicelled nonpendent spikelets 5–18 mm:
  - Sheath collar pubescent, at least lower sheaths usually long-pubescent/hirsute, rarely glabrous => *C. sessiliflorum*.
  - Sheath collar glabrous, sheaths glabrous or nearly so => *C. laxum*.
These are source key pathways, not standalone evidence that any other taxon should be excluded without taxon-context and source validation.

## 1. Chasmanthium latifolium (Michx.) Yates — p.52
Historical synonym *Uniola latifolia* Michx. Rhizomatous perennial, usually unbranched culms 1.5 m tall and leafy to four-fifths height; nodes glabrous, often reddish-purple. Leaf sheath and collar glabrous. Ligule ciliate membrane 1 mm or less. Blades thin, flat, glabrous or slightly pilose near ligule; mostly 1–2 cm broad, gradually narrowing at both ends. Inflorescence typically loose, occasionally somewhat contracted; flattened many-flowered awnless spikelets borne on slender pedicels. Main panicle axis and branches/pedicels often drooping. Spikelets 6–17(–26) flowered, mostly 0.6–1.6(–2) cm broad and 2–5 cm long, tip glumes and lemmas sharply keeled and laterally compressed. Lower 1–2 florets neuter. Glumes 0.5–0.7 mm long, subequal, acute, 5–7-nerved [**units need direct image recheck before scoring: likely cm, not mm**]. Lemmas entire or bifid, 11–15-nerved; paleas entire/bifid, shorter than lemmas. Anthers yellow, 4–6 mm; caryopses 2–2.5 mm wide, 3–5 mm long, brown to reddish-black/black. Chromosome 2n=48.
Gould Texas regions 1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8, along streambanks and moist woodland habitats; general Pennsylvania/Illinois/Nebraska southwest to Florida/Gulf Coast/Texas and other recorded areas. Flowering June–October.

## 2. Chasmanthium sessiliflorum (Poir.) Yates — pp.52–53
Historical synonym *Uniola sessiliflora* Poir. Cespitose rhizomatous perennial, short without erect culms 0.7–1.5 m tall, knotty rhizomes. Culms leafy for less than half height; inflorescence exserted on long peduncle. Nodes glabrous to minutely pubescent. Sheaths glabrous to densely hispid. Leaf collar pubescent. Ligule minute, short-ciliate membrane. Blades long and narrow, mostly 0.4–1.2(–1.5) cm broad, gradually narrowing at both ends, sparsely pilose on adaxial surface. Inflorescence contracted, few-flowered panicle or spike-like raceme mostly 15–50 cm; main axis stiffly erect; branches widely spread, 0.5–5 cm. Spikelets sessile or short-pedicelled, glabrous, 3–6-flowered, 3–5 mm broad and 5–18 mm long, with lower 1–2 florets not seed-bearing. Glumes 1–2.5 mm long, equal/subequal, acute or subacute, 3-nerved. Lowermost lemma only slightly larger than glumes/similar in appearance, upper lemmas mostly 3–5 mm, often beaked apex. Paleas thick, acute, slightly shorter than lemmas. Anthers 1.5–2 mm, reddish-brown. Caryopses broadly ovate, 1–2 mm broad and 2–3 mm long, dull brown to reddish-black/black per p.53 continuation. Chromosome 2n=24.
Gould Texas regions 1,2,3 and borders of 4; moist pine/deciduous forest and adjacent prairie openings, often dominant woodland grass. General southeastern US through Texas and NE Mexico. Flowering June–November.

## 3. Chasmanthium laxum (L.) Yates — p.54
Historical synonym *Uniola laxa* (L.) B.S.P. Cespitose rhizomatous perennial; generally similar to *C. sessiliflorum* but collar glabrous, herbage more glabrous generally. Chromosome 2n=24.
Gould Texas regions 1,2,3,4,6, woodlands/meadows/marshy areas, apparently not common; general southeastern US to southeastern Missouri, southern Oklahoma and eastern Texas. Flowering June–November. **Species account is short; retain the direct comparison rather than manufacture detailed measurements.**
Gould adds no evidence of hybridization between *C. laxum* and *C. sessiliflorum* per Yates (1966), historical finding.

## 8. Arundo L. — p.54
Gould historical genus about 6 species, all native Asia.
### Arundo donax L. — pp.54–55
Historical citation Sp. Pl. 81 (1753), GIANT REED, Fig.18.
Perennial stout culms 2–6 m tall and thick knotty rhizomes. Culms and leaves glabrous. Leaves relatively uniformly spaced and distichous on culm; sheath rounded. Ligule a ciliate membrane 1 mm or less. Blades thick, flat, elongate, acuminate, scabrous on margins, mostly 4–7 cm broad on main culms. Inflorescence dense contracted many-flowered panicle typically 30–60 cm long, branches pectinate with spikelets mostly 5–25 cm or more long. Spikelets mostly 2–4 flowered, 10–15 mm; glabrous rachilla joints; glumes broad, thin, nearly equal, irregularly 3–several-nerved, narrowly acute/obtuse at apex. Lemmas thin 3–5 nerved, 5–10 mm, densely pilose with soft hairs 6–9 mm, acuminate often short awned at apex, upper lemmas notched on either side of awn; paleas broad much shorter than lemmas, minutely ciliate on nerves. Chromosomes 2n=110 and 120 reported.
Gould Texas distribution: statewide except High Plains region 9, largely established by highway planting along culverts/ditches and spreading into fields; general Old World species widely introduced southern US. Flowering mostly September–November.
Figure 18 p.55 depicts plant, spikelet, floret; caption credits Hitchcock (1935).

## 9. Phragmites Adans. — pp.54–55
Gould historical genus 2–3 species.
### Phragmites australis (Cav.) Trin. ex Steud. — pp.54–55, INCOMPLETE
Historical citation Nom. Bot. ed.2, 2:324 (1841), COMMON REED, Fig.19.
Stout perennial, culms 2–4 m tall, creeping rhizomes; stolons also frequently developed. Culms and leaves glabrous. Culms leafy to base of inflorescence. Sheaths smooth, rounded. Ligules minute, ciliate, hairs longer than membranous base. Blades flat, elongate, minutely scabrous on margins; blades of main culms mostly 1.5–5 cm broad. Inflorescence densely flowered with spikelets borne on slender wiry pedicels and branchlets of contracted but much-branched panicle. Disarticulation above glumes and between florets. Spikelets 10–15 mm, mostly 4–8 florets. Rachilla joints villous with hairs 1 cm or more long, glumes narrowly lanceolate, acute, first glume 1-nerved, second 3–5-nerved, mostly 6–8 mm long, acute or mucronate. **Treatment continues p.56; do not infer rest of species anatomy, habitat or season from the current pages.**
